- COBRA2026 is a living-patient dataset pairing measured kV CBCT projections (with acquisition geometry) to a planning CT for 867 pelvic radiotherapy patients from six European centers, i.e. soft-tissue-surrounded pelvis on fixed RT kV imagers rather than a mobile C-arm; 692 train / 52 val / 123 test. Whether the lumbar spine/sacrum lies inside the pelvic CBCT field of view is NOT stated on the page (unverified). (https://zenodo.org/records/21322350)
- Projection pose is fully recoverable: each case ships projections as .mha with an RTK-format geometry.xml plus vendor calibration files (Scan.xml, calibration files), so no fiducial or registration step is needed to obtain the projection matrices. (https://zenodo.org/records/21322350)
- The dataset is described as 'raw' CBCT acquisitions, but the Zenodo page does not document whether the stored projection intensities are detector counts, air-normalized transmission, or log-transformed line integrals; this must be checked in the arXiv paper / preprocessing code (unverified on this page). (https://zenodo.org/records/21322350)
- COBRA2026 stores CBCT projections as raw detector signal (16-bit unsigned, not log/line-integral) for both vendors: Elekta .his frames are stacked into projections.mha without conversion to line integrals (flood-field already applied by the vendor before export; a scalar mAs-ratio normalisation is documented), and Varian .xim frames are likewise stacked raw, with air-scan (I0) calibration projections supplied separately in a Calibrations directory. Only the challenge-platform variant is pre-converted to line integrals. This is a per-vendor raw-vs-processed documentation that directly contrasts with DeepFluoro's log-compressed display images. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20037)
- Every acquisition ships with a per-projection geometry file (geometry.xml, RTK format, built from Elekta Frames.xml or Varian Scan.xml + .xim headers, every gantry angle recorded, offset-detector flag per scan) and a planning CT that is rigidly then B-spline registered to the CBCT with origins shifted to the CBCT isocenter, so the CT-to-projection pose is recoverable for a kV imager other than the Siemens Cios Fusion. The explicit rigid matrix between original CT and CBCT is not stated to be provided separately. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20037)
- The SPARE dataset is a radiotherapy 4D cone-beam CT projection set for thoracic (lung) patients hosted as open access under CC BY-NC 4.0 at the University of Sydney eScholarship repository, distributed as a single archive of over 200 GB with a direct download URL (https://ses-data.library.sydney.edu.au/public/34378_Shieh/SPARE_PublicArchive.zip) and a named contact; it is NOT a C-arm, cadaver, or lumbosacral set, so it fails gap criterion (a) and its projection geometry is a fixed linac gantry, not a mobile C-arm. (https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/handle/2123/34378)
- Most of the SPARE projection data are Monte Carlo SIMULATED from 4D-Lung CT volumes (32 CBCT scans of nine patients), and only two scans are real clinical kV projections (one Varian, one Elekta); therefore the set contributes at most two real-projection-plus-prior-CT pairs of the thorax, which is too few to fit nine device terms or test a display-curve hypothesis on a second device. [Simulated/clinical split verified from the linked outcome paper, PMID 31247134, DOI 10.1002/mp.13687, not from the repository page itself.] (https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/handle/2123/34378)
- The TCIA 4D-Lung collection distributes only reconstructed 3-D/4-D volumes (DICOM modalities CT and RTSTRUCT); the ~2500 raw kV cone-beam projections per 4D-CBCT acquisition were used for in-house FDK reconstruction and are not part of the downloadable data, so the collection cannot supply paired CT + real 2-D projection frames for a formation-model calibration. (https://doi.org/10.7937/K9/TCIA.2016.ELN8YGLE)
- The paper releases no data: it has no Data Availability statement, the projection pairs were acquired manually from physical phantoms at MSKCC for an in-house super-resolution model, and no repository, request route or licence is given, so this set cannot fill the gap (criterion e fails). (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12270334/)
- Access route and licence: the ~750 GB training set is openly downloadable without registration under CC BY-NC 4.0 (released 13 July 2026); the validation (~60 GB) and test (~140 GB) sets are embargoed until 1 March 2032 for the grand-challenge, so only 692 of the 867 cases are usable now. (https://zenodo.org/records/21322350)
- The projections come from two vendors' kV on-board imagers (Elekta XVI-class and Varian OBI/TrueBeam-class systems), giving at least two devices distinct from the Siemens Cios Fusion used in DeepFluoro; this is stated in the linked dataset paper (arXiv 2607.20037), not on the Zenodo page itself, and specific models/kVp/detector pitch are not given in the abstract. (https://zenodo.org/records/21322350)
- Scale and device diversity: 867 living pelvic-radiotherapy patients from six European centres, 634 on Elekta XVI (120 kV, 504x504 px at 0.8 mm, 189–730 projections/scan) and 283 on Varian (125–140 kV, 1024–3072 x 384–768 px at 0.28–0.39 x 0.39–1.12 mm, 406–895 projections/scan), with clinically reconstructed CBCT and planning CT for each; split 692 train / 52 validation / 123 test. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20037)
- Access: CC BY-NC 4.0; the 692-case training set (~748 GB) was released July 2026 with no registration via the CREATIS tomoradio-warehouse (Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.21322350 holds only a 2.2 GB overview zip and the paper PDF); validation and test sets (60 GB + 140 GB) are embargoed until 1 March 2032; preprocessing code at github.com/cobra-challenge-2026/preprocessing. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20037)
- Limitations against the stated gap: these are fixed-gantry linac kV CBCT projections (Elekta XVI / Varian OBI-class flat panels), not mobile C-arm fluoroscopy; the field of view is pelvic and the paper does not state whether L4–S1 or the sacrum are within the longitudinal FOV (unverified from the text; only fov_cbct.mha masks are supplied); no bone, spine or vertebra masks are included. Varian data additionally had a kernel-based scatter correction applied in the converted chain, which must be accounted for when fitting a scatter term. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20037)
- Each SPARE scan is paired with a prior (planning) 4D-CT of the same living patient, with soft tissue present and gantry geometry known, so the two clinical scans do satisfy gap criteria (c) soft tissue, (d) recoverable pose, and (e) downloadable with a stated licence, and the thoracic spine will be incidentally in the field of view of a lung CBCT; whether the spine is fully covered, and the exact number of projections and kVp, is NOT stated on the repository page and must be read from Instructions_Datasets.doc inside the archive (unverified here). (https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/handle/2123/34378)
- The repository record does not document whether stored projections are raw detector counts, line integrals, or log/vendor-processed display images; the challenge explicitly permitted participants to apply 'scatter correction, projection smoothing' etc., which implies projections were released in a form suitable for reconstruction (pre-log or line-integral) rather than as display images, but this is inference and is UNVERIFIABLE from the page. (https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/handle/2123/34378)
- The underlying 2-D projection data were acquired on a fixed radiotherapy kV flat-panel imager (Varian On-Board Imager) at 125 kVp, 20 mA, 20 ms per frame in a single slow 360 degree gantry arc, i.e. a kV (not MV) source on a device different from the Siemens Cios Fusion; the raw projections therefore exist at the originating institution (VCU, Hugo group) even though TCIA does not host them (that they could be obtained on request is unverified). (https://doi.org/10.7937/K9/TCIA.2016.ELN8YGLE)
- The collection comprises 20 patients with locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer imaged with 4D fan-beam CT (16-slice Philips Brilliance Big Bore, 3 mm slices) and 4D-CBCT, totalling 347,330 DICOM images (183.04 GB) under a CC BY 3.0 licence; the anatomy is thorax/lung and the page makes no statement about spine coverage or field-of-view length. (https://doi.org/10.7937/K9/TCIA.2016.ELN8YGLE)
- The imaging device is a fixed radiotherapy kV CBCT imager, not a mobile C-arm: a Varian TrueBeam gantry with the RTI4343iL amorphous-silicon/CsI flat panel (42.7 x 42.7 cm, 0.139 mm pixel pitch), run at 125 kVp, 15 mA, 20 ms per projection in half-fan geometry, with acquisition binning 2x8 to 1424 x 268 pixels versus 2848 x 2144 unbinned. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12270334/)
- Projection intensities are stored as 16-bit raw XIM detector data normalized to their maximum, not log-compressed display images; the only stated processing is scatter, point-spread, air/blank and beam-hardening corrections applied later in TIGRE for reconstruction, so this device would document a raw-intensity (linear) convention if the data were obtainable. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12270334/)
- Pose ground truth is only the linac gantry angle: 576 LR/SR projection pairs were acquired at 5-degree intervals (72 per phantom) from Alderson-Rando head/thorax/pelvis sections, a LUNGMAN, a STEEVE, a CIRS Dynamic Thorax and a Catphan 604, with no fiducial or tracked-pose registration ground truth and no paired planning CT described for 2-D/3-D registration. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12270334/)
- The dataset creators require that users cite the Medical Physics outcome paper (DOI 10.1002/mp.13687, PMID 31247134, Med Phys Sept 2019), and the original challenge host page (https://image-x.sydney.edu.au/research/medical-imaging/spare-challenge) now returns HTTP 404, so the eScholarship record (hdl 2123/34378) is the only surviving access route. (https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/handle/2123/34378)
- Longitudinal paired CT/CBCT imaging exists for a subset: in 14 of the 20 subjects 4D-FBCTs were also acquired weekly during therapy, but a single radiation oncologist delineated targets and organs at risk only in the 4D-FBCTs and a limited number of 4D-CBCTs; no vertebral labels or pose/registration ground truth are described. (https://doi.org/10.7937/K9/TCIA.2016.ELN8YGLE)
- The dataset's primary reference is Hugo et al., Medical Physics 44(2):762-771 (2017), DOI 10.1002/mp.12059, and the TCIA DOI is 10.7937/K9/TCIA.2016.ELN8YGLE (last updated 2016-10-19); data citation is required. (https://doi.org/10.7937/K9/TCIA.2016.ELN8YGLE)
- The authors themselves state, without quantifying it, that anthropomorphic-phantom training data lack the fine anatomy of human subjects and may generalize poorly to clinical scans; their only patient check was three clinical thorax CBCTs (two thoracic, one spinal case) shown qualitatively, so the paper gives no measured phantom-to-patient appearance or scatter gap. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12270334/)
