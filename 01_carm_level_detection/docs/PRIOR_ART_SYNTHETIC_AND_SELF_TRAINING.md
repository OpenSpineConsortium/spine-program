# Prior art: synthetic radiographs, scaling, and self-training on rare classes

Deep-research run wf_78f61c94-3fe, 2026-09-17: 105 agents, five search angles, three-vote adversarial verification per claim. Only claims that survived are listed as findings; refuted and unverified ones are kept so nobody re-litigates them.

## Summary

Verified evidence exists only for problems 1-3, and it points the same way. For the DRR-to-radiograph gap, the one controlled head-to-head (SyntheX, Nat Mach Intell 2023, code public) found physics-style DRRs plus strong photometric domain randomization with zero real images tied CycleGAN/ADDA translation and matched-size real training (6.44 vs 6.62 / 6.41 / 6.46 mm on cadaveric hip fluoroscopy); FluoroSAM (MICCAI 2025) uses the same recipe with no generative translation; and the only style-transfer result that recovered synthetic-level real performance (Jecklin, Med Image Anal 2024) ran a paired Pix2Pix real-to-DRR at inference and tied plain fine-tuning. No surviving claim measured a downstream gain, or a label-preservation check, for SD/SDXL img2img, ControlNet, LoRA-on-real-radiographs or CUT, so that route is unmeasured for this task. For scaling, the chest-radiograph diffusion literature agrees that gains concentrate when real data is scarce, saturate at roughly 2-10 synthetic per real (RoentGen-v2 plateau at about 5x; Khosravi plateau from 300%; Sagers 10:1), synthetic-only trails mixed at every quantity and loses 0.08-0.13 AUROC on fine-detail findings, and synthetic-pretrain-then-real-finetune beat naive mixing on all five external sets (+6.5% vs +2.7% relative AUROC), so the honest plot is real+synthetic against a fixed real-only baseline on an external test set. For rare-class self-training, CReST (class-dependent pseudolabel admission rate, code public) raised CIFAR10-LT rare-class recall from 8.9/7.2% to 60.3/70.8% at a 2-7 point majority cost, and DebiasPL showed pseudolabel imbalance is intrinsic and that a higher global confidence threshold worsens it (more than 60 of 1,000 classes get zero pseudolabels at 0.95) while retained-label precision still matters, so balance must come from a separate mechanism; none of this is measured on radiographs or on ordinal sequence labels. Nothing survived on forced-schema relabelling, LSTV detection with tens of positives, or failure-analysis practice in vertebra labelling.

## Verified findings

### 1. Physics-style DRR rendering plus strong photometric domain randomization, with no real images at training time, matched both the best generative domain-adaptation baselines (CycleGAN and ADDA under strong DR) and matched-size real-data training on real cadaveric fluoroscopy; the authors argue GAN translation adds cost and failure modes rather than accuracy. FluoroSAM (MICCAI 2025) independently adopted the same recipe (DeepDRR-lineage rendering from 1,621 NMDID CTs with TotalSegmentator meshes, random plus near-standard geometries, heavy photometric DR) and uses no diffusion/GAN img2img.

*Confidence:* high · *vote:* 3-0 (SyntheX parity), 3-0 (FluoroSAM design)

**Evidence.** SyntheX Table 1, hip task, strong-DR column: SyntheX 6.44 +/- 7.05 mm / Dice 0.80; Realistic-CycleGAN 6.62 +/- 6.82 / 0.80; Realistic-ADDA 6.41 +/- 6.27 / 0.80; Real2Real 6.46 +/- 8.21 / 0.79 (CycleGAN/ADDA rows do consume unlabelled real X-rays; the SyntheX row does not). Qualifiers: 366 cadaveric C-arm images from 6 specimens, leave-one-specimen-out, with DRRs rendered from the same specimens' CTs at registered poses, the most favourable sim-to-real condition possible and not clinical patient films; parity holds only under strong DR (regular DR: sim-only 7.59 +/- 13.80 vs CycleGAN 6.57 +/- 8.22 vs Real2Real 6.90 +/- 10.69, i.e. translation helped before DR was strengthened); no significance test among the parity rows; authors state Sim2Real hardly matches Real2Real where abundant real data exist (COVID lesion segmentation 85.03% vs 93.95%); the 2022 arXiv v1 reports different hip numbers, so cite the 2023 NMI table. Label preservation is by construction: each DRR is rendered at the registered pose of a real X-ray so projected labels are exact. FluoroSAM v3 text has zero mentions of scatter, noise, Monte Carlo, polychromatic, physics, diffusion, GAN or style transfer; the physics lineage rests on DeepDRR/Unberath-2019 citations and v1 wording; no ablation isolates scatter/noise realism. Important: the companion claim that DeepDRR physics beats naive ray-cast DRRs (6.44 vs 10.50 mm strong DR; 7.59 vs 8.23 regular DR) was REFUTED 0-3, so the surviving evidence does not separate physics realism from domain randomization.

Sources: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10959504/> · <https://github.com/arcadelab/SyntheX> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08059>

### 2. Generative style transfer in the reverse direction, a paired Pix2Pix mapping each real fluoroscopy frame into the DRR domain at inference, recovered synthetic-level performance on real X-rays for a DRR-trained 3D spine reconstruction network; but in the deployable setting (estimated vertebra origins) it was indistinguishable from simply fine-tuning on the same paired real data, and neither code nor the paired dataset is released.

*Confidence:* medium · *vote:* 2-1

**Evidence.** Pix2Pix trained 400 epochs on 3,317 pixel-aligned real/DRR pairs from 10 cadaver specimens (fiducial DLT+RANSAC calibration, 1.06 +/- 0.86 px error); tested on 1,020 real images from 3 specimens on one C-arm (Siemens Cios Spin), effective n=3. With ground-truth vertebra origins: surface 0.73 +/- 0.10, F1 0.88 +/- 0.04, ASD 0.73 +/- 0.20 mm, HD95 3.01 +/- 2.21 mm; with estimated origins 0.62 / 0.84 / 0.97 mm / 3.68 mm; fine-tuning X23D on the same real data gave 0.63 +/- 0.10 / 0.84 +/- 0.04 / 0.98 +/- 0.23 / 3.45 +/- 0.90, no practical edge. Same-specimen DRR ceiling is surface 0.79 / F1 0.89. The paper's '11% and 4% boost' is GT-origin vs estimated-origin within the style-transfer arm, not style transfer vs fine-tuning. Cross-C-arm check on 6 specimens stated underpowered by the authors. Downstream task is 3D reconstruction, not level naming on clinical radiographs. The companion claim that naive physics-free DRRs 'essentially fail' on real fluoroscopy (almost no real sample above 0.6 surface score) was refuted 1-2, so the naive-baseline gap should be quoted cautiously.

Sources: <https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16027v2> · <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2024.103322>

### 3. In the one study that compared training schedules, using diffusion-synthesised radiographs for supervised pretraining and then fine-tuning on real data beat naively pooling real and synthetic in one training set on every one of five institutions' test sets (mean AUROC +6.5% relative vs +2.7% relative), and the naive-mix benefit plateaued at about 300k synthetic images, roughly 5x the 66,760 real images, even though 565k were generated. Code, weights and the synthetic set are public.

*Confidence:* high · *vote:* 3-0, 3-0

**Evidence.** DenseNet-121 real-only AUROC 0.772 (MIMIC-test) / 0.700 (CheXpert) / 0.661 (NIH) / 0.721 (PadChest) / 0.641 (VinDr); synthetic-pretrain-then-finetune 0.798 / 0.732 / 0.726 / 0.781 / 0.683; naive mix (66k real + 565k synthetic) 0.770 / 0.730 / 0.696 / 0.753 / 0.641. Naive mixing gives no in-distribution gain (0.770 vs 0.772) and none on VinDr (p=0.99); OOD gains +4.3 / +5.3 / +4.4 pp at p<0.05. Synthetic sizes tested 66k, 100k, 150k, 200k, 300k, 400k, 500k, 565k; the plateau is the authors' reading of Fig. 4 with no statistical test of the plateau itself, and refers to OOD gains from naive mixing. 6.5% and 2.7% are relative changes in mean AUROC (0.699 -> 0.744 = +4.5 pp; 0.718 = +1.9 pp). No control isolates the two-stage schedule from the roughly 9x more images seen. Task is 14-label CXR disease classification; generator is RoentGen-v2 (Stable Diffusion v2.1 fine-tuned on MIMIC-CXR), text-to-image and within-domain, not DRR. Preprint dated Aug 2025; a PMC record (PMC12676388) suggests a journal version exists.

Sources: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16783> · <https://github.com/StanfordMIMI/RoentGen-v2>

### 4. Synthetic radiographs substitute for real at roughly 2-3:1 (a classifier trained only on DDPM-synthesised CXRs matched real-only training once synthetic was 200% of real on the internal split, 300% on external sets), but synthetic-only trailed mixed real+synthetic at all ten quantities tested, and a matched-count comparison (5,000 synthetic vs 5,000 real) showed synthetic-only training loses 0.08-0.13 AUROC on findings that need fine anatomical detail. The honest benefit curve is real+synthetic vs real-only with real count held fixed, evaluated on an external test set.

*Confidence:* medium · *vote:* 2-1 (Khosravi), 3-0 (2601.12946)

**Evidence.** Khosravi et al. (Lancet eBioMedicine 2024, PMC11177083), Supplementary Table E4, CheXpert held-out patient split (n=56,448, CheXpert-labeler labels, not the 500-film radiologist test set), macro AUROC: real-only 0.783; real+synthetic at 100..1000% = 0.795 / 0.800 / 0.803 / 0.803 / 0.803 / 0.803 / 0.803 / 0.803 / 0.804 / 0.804 (plateau from 300%); synthetic-only = 0.766 / 0.783 / 0.789 / 0.793 / 0.795 / 0.796 / 0.797 / 0.798 / 0.796 / 0.799. 72,053 real training CXRs; the paper itself warns same-distribution test sets inflate gains (0.070 on CXP validation vs 0.043 on the held-out split). arXiv 2601.12946 v4 (Feb 2026): unconditional DDPM at 128x128 on 5,534 MIMIC-CXR images; 5,000 real vs 5,000 synthetic on a common 500-real test set; lung lesion -0.13, pleural effusion -0.08, fracture -0.08 AUROC; the paper does not state which generation of synthetic images was used for this experiment and code is 'available upon publication' with no repository link in any version. The stronger claim that Khosravi's supplementation 'raised AUROC incrementally with no saturation up to 10x' was refuted 0-3; Table E4 plateaus at 0.803 from 300%.

Sources: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09402> · <https://github.com/BardiaKh/SyntheticallyEnhanced> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12946>

### 5. Synthetic augmentation gains are concentrated in the scarce-real regime (1-10% of the real set) and saturate by roughly 10 synthetic per real; at full real data the gain is small and not statistically significant, and headline improvements are often measured against a nearly untrained baseline.

*Confidence:* high · *vote:* 3-0, 3-0

**Evidence.** Prakash et al. (ICCV 2025 workshop; ControlNet over the RoentGen latent diffusion model): RSNA Pneumonia F1 at 1% real 0.0104 -> 0.104 (2x, P=0.0101) / 0.142 (5x) / 0.147 (10x) / 0.161 (25x, P=0.0031, which is the abstract's 'maximum improvement of 0.15'); at 10% real 0.0950 -> 0.276 (10x, P=0.051, n.s.); at 100% real 0.326 -> 0.344 (2x, P=0.107, n.s.). CheXpert: 1% 0.209 -> 0.289 (P=0.0196); 10% 0.255 -> 0.323 (P=0.036); 100% 0.310 -> 0.354 (P=0.099, n.s.). Only two trials per setting, one-tailed t-tests; only 2x was run at 100% real, so plateau is confounded with multiplier; CheXpert's +0.044 at 100% is more than half its +0.080 at 1%, so 'near-plateau' is generous there. Sagers et al. (EfficientNet V2-M, Fitzpatrick 17k nine-class subset, 3,699 images): gains up to +13.2% at 32 real per class saturate at 10:1; 25:1, 50:1 and 75:1 'neither monotonic nor statistically significant'; at 228 real per class the effect was about 2% and non-significant; no ratio between 1:1 and 10:1 was tested; dermatology photographs only, no radiograph transfer. The companion claim that 'the optimal mixing ratio depends on how much real data is available' was refuted 0-3; only the regime statement survived.

Sources: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18602> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12453> · <https://github.com/manrai/synthetic-derm>

### 6. Class-rebalancing self-training (CReST) fixes the collapse of minority-class recall under pseudolabelling by admitting pseudolabels at a class-dependent rate mu_l = (N_{L+1-l}/N_1)^alpha (all predictions of the rarest class kept, only 0.01^alpha of the commonest at imbalance 100; alpha=0 recovers plain self-training), taking the most confident predictions within each class. The measured gain is almost entirely minority recall bought with a small majority loss, and over-rebalancing (alpha=1) flips the model into a reverse imbalance after about 10 generations. All benchmarks are natural images; code is public.

*Confidence:* high · *vote:* 3-0, 3-0, 3-0

**Evidence.** Table 1 (mean of 5 folds): CIFAR10-LT gamma=100, beta=10%: FixMatch 66.3 +/- 1.74 -> CReST 75.9 +/- 0.62 -> CReST+ 78.1 +/- 0.84; gamma=200: 59.7 -> 64.1 -> 67.7; CIFAR100-LT gamma=100: 28.3 -> 32.1 -> 34.6; ImageNet127 (gamma=286) 65.8 -> 73.7 averaged class recall over 3 generations. Table 5 per-class test recall: classes 9/10 go 8.9% / 7.2% -> 60.3% / 70.8% (+51.4 / +63.6) while classes 1-4 drop 4.9 / 1.8 / 2.7 / 6.6 points; balanced average 64.9 -> 79.6. alpha=1 ablation: after generation 10 the rarest-class recall rises 55.0 -> 71.1% but 7 of the other 9 classes lose recall and balanced accuracy drops 3.0 points. Setup: alpha=1/3 on FixMatch, 15 generations for CReST, 6 for CReST+ (Table 2 fair comparison uses 6/3; ImageNet127 uses 3 with alpha=0.7). CVPR 2021, outside the 2022-2026 window. Later imbalanced-SSL work (DASO 2022, ACR 2023) reports CReST+ underperforming when the unlabelled distribution differs from the labelled one. WARNING: the claim that FixMatch's rare-class pseudolabels are about 98% precise and therefore support confidence-gating anchored rare-class predictions was REFUTED 0-3; do not cite CReST as support for keeping anchored LSTV predictions on precision grounds.

Sources: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.09559> · <https://github.com/google-research/crest>

### 7. Pseudolabel imbalance is intrinsic to the classifier rather than inherited from an imbalanced label prior: it appears with balanced source and target (FixMatch on balanced CIFAR-10) and cross-domain (CLIP zero-shot on the balanced ImageNet-1K training set, where the most-predicted class receives more than 3,500 predictions, three times its true count). Raising a single global confidence threshold makes the imbalance worse (at tau=0.95 more than 60 of 1,000 ImageNet classes receive zero pseudolabels), yet once a separate debiasing mechanism is applied the highest threshold still gives the best accuracy, so retained-pseudolabel precision should stay high and balance be restored by a distinct mechanism, not by loosening the gate.

*Confidence:* medium · *vote:* 2-1 (imbalance origin), 3-0 (threshold result)

**Evidence.** DebiasPL (CVPR 2022, code with checkpoints and logs). Table 9, ImageNet-1K zero-shot, DebiasPL+CLIP top-1 at tau_clip 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.6 / 0.8 / 0.9 / 0.95 = 55.9 / 63.2 / 66.2 / 67.1 / 67.7 / 67.7 (0.9 and 0.95 tie, so a plateau, not a strict gain). Fig. 11 paragraph verbatim: larger threshold gives higher imbalance rate; at 0.95 more than 60 categories get zero predictions; smaller threshold lowers imbalance but also precision. Authors' own conclusion: high precision from a high threshold is essential and DebiasPL alleviates the imbalance the threshold causes; vanilla FixMatch+CLIP at 0.95 underperforms CLIP zero-shot, consistent with imbalance hurting absent debiasing. Table 9 has no non-debiased row across thresholds, so 'precision matters more than balance' is demonstrated only with DebiasPL active on one setting (ResNet-50 CLIP). Qualifiers on the origin claim: 'class 0' in Fig. 1c is the rank-0 most-predicted class on a sorted axis, not ImageNet index 0; CLIP's pretraining set is only approximately query-balanced; the causal attribution to inter-class similarity is hedged in the body ('we believe... largely'), and FlexMatch offers a complementary per-class-difficulty account. The imbalance-origin claim passed 2-1, the threshold claim 3-0. The stronger claim that self-training progressively amplifies the bias without self-correction was refuted 1-2.

Sources: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01490> · <https://github.com/frank-xwang/debiased-pseudo-labeling>

### 8. No claim survived verification for problems 4, 5 and 6 (correcting forced-schema annotations such as BUU-LSPINE's fixed five-lumbar template, partial-label or label-noise learning for ordinal sequence labels, human-in-the-loop relabelling at scale, transitional-vertebra detection on radiographs with tens of positives and its best published sensitivity/specificity, and failure-analysis tooling or reported failure modes from VERIDAH, SpineNet, Cina 2021 or VertXNet), nor for the generative side of problem 1 (SD/SDXL img2img, ControlNet with edge/depth conditioning, LoRA fine-tuned on unlabelled real radiographs, or CycleGAN/CUT as a forward real-ward translation with a measured downstream gain and a label-preservation check on radiographs).

*Confidence:* low · *vote:* n/a (no surviving claims)

**Evidence.** This is an absence in the retrieved-and-verified set, not proof the literature is empty. The only surviving evidence on generative translation is neutral to negative: SyntheX parity (translation buys nothing once DR is strong) and Jecklin's reverse-direction Pix2Pix tying fine-tuning. The only label-preservation mechanisms in surviving evidence are geometric by construction: SyntheX renders each DRR at the registered pose of a real X-ray so labels project exactly, and Jecklin's pairs are fiducial-calibrated to 1.06 +/- 0.86 px. No surviving source measured label drift or landmark displacement after diffusion or GAN translation of a radiograph. Every measured scaling curve in the surviving set is within-domain (diffusion CXR to real CXR, or dermatology photos), not cross-domain DRR to radiograph.

Sources: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10959504/> · <https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16027v2>
