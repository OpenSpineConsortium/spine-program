# Evidence: the time a fluoroscopic sweep would take, and what current localisation costs

Deep-research run 2026-09-19 (workflow wf_42f017f0-4cf); three adversarial verifiers per claim.

## Summary

The literature supports a rough but citable budget for a lateral fluoroscopic sweep. Beam-on time per spot exposure is small (about 0.5 s per shot on a phantom, PMC3248583; 0.06-0.35 s for an entire one-shot fiducial confirmation, PMC11476588), so exposures are not the cost; the cost is moving the C-arm, which runs 36-49 s per repositioning in the only time-motion measurements found (Matthews 2007, PMID 17986885; De Silva 2018, PMID 29487882), and physical manipulation rather than beam time dominates that figure. Per-image automated labelling latency for the only clinically translated system (LevelCheck) spans 4.7 s (2012 GPU, PMC3429949) to 7-26 s with an explicit runtime-accuracy trade-off (2015, PMC4433144) to 17-72 s in the 2018 OR deployment (PMID 30051244), and surgeons said they would wait 20-60 s. Combining these, a 4-exposure sweep from the sacrum plus one query image is on the order of 3-4 min of C-arm positioning (3-4 moves at 40-50 s each, plus one more move to the query site) and under 30 s of compute, against a published 15 min for standard fluoroscopic level counting in thoracic surgery (Mongardi 2021, PMID 33358493; Upadhyaya 2012, PMID 22054638) and 3 min with a preoperative CT-guided fiducial that itself costs ~14 min of CT-suite time (Keil 2024, PMC11476588). No source measured the specific manoeuvre the sweep needs (pure translation along the table between overlapping lateral views), no lumbar-specific localisation time was found, and no scaling of localisation time with distance from the sacrum or with BMI survived verification.

## Verified findings

### Beam-on time per single C-arm spot exposure is sub-second on a phantom (~0.5 s per AP spot in conventional mod…  (confidence high, votes 3-0 ([0]), 3-0 ([1]), 2-1 ([8]))

Beam-on time per single C-arm spot exposure is sub-second on a phantom (~0.5 s per AP spot in conventional mode on an OEC 9800 Plus; 0.2-0.3 s in pulsed/low-dose modes), and per-view fluoro-hunting in a pelvis phantom costs ~4.7 s and ~6.4 exposures when the view has to be found by trial and error. Clinical beam-on during lumbar pedicle screw insertion is ~20 s per screw (1.4 min per case), an upper bound on what a short spot-image sweep could consume. No verified dose-per-spot figure (mGy or cGy·cm²) for a lateral lumbar/thoracic spot image survived verification.

Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3248583/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10851103/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29487882/

Evidence: PMC3248583 (Korean J Pain 2011): five consecutive AP spot exposures on a 0.85 mm Pb apron phantom accumulated 2.7 ± 0.1 s conventional, 1.6 ± 0.5 s pulsed, 1.0 ± 0.2 s pulsed+low-dose, i.e. ~0.5 s/shot conventional. PMID 10851103 (Jones et al., Spine 2000): 140 consecutive lumbar pedicle-screw cases, 1.4 min fluoroscopy per case, 0.33 min per screw. PMID 29487882 (De Silva et al. 2018): conventional fluoro-hunting needed 6.4 ± 4.8 exposures and 4.7 ± 3.6 s per view vs 2.0 exposures and 0.5 s with virtual fluoroscopy. Caveats: phantom AP data, not lateral through a large patient (ABC will lengthen exposures); the 20 s/screw figure is continuous screening, not spot images; dose claims from PMC3248583 and PMC8150624 were refuted 0-3 and should not be cited.

### Repositioning a mobile C-arm takes roughly 36-49 s per move in the only time-motion measurements found: 46 s (…  (confidence high, votes 3-0 ([7]), 3-0 ([9]), 3-0 ([10]))

Repositioning a mobile C-arm takes roughly 36-49 s per move in the only time-motion measurements found: 46 s (95% CI 41-51) navigated and 49 s (95% CI 44-53) with scout images to retrieve a previously recorded projection on test rigs/phantoms (Matthews 2007), and 41.0 ± 30.9 s conventional vs 36.4 ± 33.2 s with virtual fluoroscopy to reach a target pelvic view (De Silva 2018, n.s.). Because removing scout exposures did not significantly change wall-clock positioning time, physical manipulation (~36 s), not beam-on time (~5 s), dominates the per-move cost. A 2026 self-driving 3D C-arm series (300 cases) reports aggregate C-arm operation time (31.9 ± 29.1 min per procedure) but no per-move or per-3D-scan duration.

Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17986885/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29487882/; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13267478/

Evidence: Matthews F et al., J Orthop Trauma 2007;21(10):687-92, doi 10.1097/BOT.0b013e318158fd42: positioning times 46 s navigated vs 49 s scout-based on 20 test-rig and 3 phantom setups. De Silva et al. 2018 (PMID 29487882, PMC5812884): positioning 36.4 ± 33.2 s vs 41.0 ± 30.9 s (p=0.39), fluoro 0.5 vs 4.7 s (p<0.05); beam time is ~11% of positioning time. PMC13267478: 'the distance of individual movements was not specifically collected'; only aggregate COT and movement counts are given. Verifier also noted Mandelka 2022 cadaver preprint (medRxiv 10.1101/2022.02.12.22270884): median 75.9 s and 7.1 shots to set a thoracolumbar standard projection manually. Caveat: none of these measured a pure table translation between overlapping lateral spine views in a draped patient; retrieving an arbitrary projection is likely slower than a one-axis slide, so 40-50 s is probably an upper bound for the sweep's per-move cost.

Surgeons (n=3) stated they would wait 20 s to 1 min for a result in all cases and >1 min in 60.6% of cases.

### Current thoracic level-localisation practice by fluoroscopic counting takes about 15 min of intraoperative tim…  (confidence high, votes 3-0 ([16]), 3-0 ([15]), 3-0 ([4]), 2-1 ([3]))

Current thoracic level-localisation practice by fluoroscopic counting takes about 15 min of intraoperative time (or fluoroscopy time), reduced to about 3 min when a preoperative CT-guided fiducial (radiopaque marker or 5 mm percutaneous screw) is in place. With a gold fiducial, confirmation needs one AP single-shot (plus lateral as required) and 0.06-0.35 s of beam-on time per case. The fiducial's own cost is an average 14.3 min of CT-intervention time (scout to control scan, range 10-20 min, n=10) under local anaesthesia 2-72 h preoperatively, excluding transport and turnover.

Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33358493/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22054638/; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11476588/

Evidence: Mongardi et al., J Clin Neurosci 2021;84:97-101 (PMID 33358493, doi 10.1016/j.jocn.2020.11.037): 'localization time with the fiducial was reduced dramatically (3 min vs 15 min of the standard technique)', n=19 fiducial vs n=11 fluoroscopy, thoracic; DAP higher in fiducial group (20 vs 16 Gy·cm²). Upadhyaya et al., J Neurosurg Spine 2012 (PMID 22054638): 26 fiducial-screw vs 26 historical fluoroscopy-only thoracic patients, 'intraoperative localization fluoroscopy time was greatly reduced'; the 2023 systematic review (PMC10193475) gives the numbers as 3 vs 15 min. Keil et al., J Clin Med 2024;13(19):5690 (PMC11476588, doi 10.3390/jcm13195690): 'average CT-intervention time between the planning scout and the final CT control scan was 14.3 min'; Table 2 intraoperative fluoroscopy times 0.06-0.35 s. Caveats: all thoracic, small non-randomised single-centre cohorts, no SD for the 15 min; Mongardi's 15 min is wall-clock localisation time whereas Upadhyaya's is fluoroscopy time, and the two are not the same quantity though both give 15 vs 3.

### Combined estimate: a lateral sweep of 3-5 overlapping spot exposures starting at the sacrum plus one query ima…  (confidence medium, votes derived from 3-0 and 2-1 claims)

Combined estimate: a lateral sweep of 3-5 overlapping spot exposures starting at the sacrum plus one query image costs on the order of 2-5 min, dominated by C-arm moves (3-5 moves at ~40-50 s each = 2-4 min; per-exposure beam time 0.5-5 s each is negligible) plus 5-30 s of per-image inference on current hardware (up to ~70 s on the 2018 LevelCheck deployment). This is comparable to the 3 min fiducial-assisted localisation without the 14 min CT-suite visit, and roughly a fifth of the 15 min published for thoracic fluoroscopic counting. If the sweep moves are pure table translations rather than re-found projections, the per-move figure could fall below 40 s, but no published measurement supports a specific lower number.

Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17986885/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29487882/; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3248583/; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4433144; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33358493/

Evidence: Arithmetic from the verified inputs: 4 moves × 46-49 s (Matthews) or 36-41 s (De Silva) = 2.4-3.3 min or 2.4-2.7 min; exposures 4-5 × 0.5-4.7 s = 2-24 s; inference 4.7-26 s (Otake 2012, Lo 2015) or 17-72 s (2018 OR). Comparator: 15 min counting vs 3 min fiducial (Mongardi 2021), 14.3 min CT intervention (Keil 2024). This is a synthesis, not a measured number; the move figures come from pelvis phantoms/rigs and arbitrary-projection retrieval, not from a translation-only spine sweep in a draped patient, and lumbar counting is usually faster than the thoracic 15 min, so the advantage is smaller in the lumbar spine.
