#### Addendum, 03:27 — two defects the SpineSAC bind tool would have shipped, found before the GPU queue cleared

The SPINEPS→body→project chain sits behind a three-hour GPU queue, so `tools/test_spinesac_bind_smoke.py`
(job 40416666/69/73, `slurm/spinesac_smoke.sh`) was written to exercise the mask construction and the overlay
renderer on synthetic volumes with known answers and on a real SPINEPS pair, rather than discover a bug on the
far side of a dependency chain. It found two, both of which would have produced finite, plausible numbers.

**1. SimpleITK cannot read SPINEPS output.** `sitk.ReadImage` on a SPINEPS mask raises *"ITK only supports
orthonormal direction cosines. No orthonormal definition found!"* — 1 of 25 masks in `~/bench_ts/spineps/native`
refused; nibabel opened 25 of 25. The bind tool was written on SimpleITK (the rest of the SpineSAC chain uses
it) and now reads with nibabel. It also **writes the body mask on the CT's own affine and header**, not on the
SPINEPS header, because a mask written on the SPINEPS header is likewise unreadable by the SimpleITK call in
`spinesac_truth.load_seg` — confirmed in the same test. Nothing is reoriented anywhere.

**2. "Instance × corpus 49/50" deletes the sacrum, and keeps the discs.** On a real pair (TS_s0001) every true
vertebra came back at a body fraction of **0.486–0.534** — about half the whole vertebra, which is what a
vertebral body should be, and an encouraging check on the definition — while **id 26, the sacrum, came back at
0.000**: SPINEPS assigns the sacrum no corpus sub-region, so the rule erases it. That matters here specifically,
because `spinesac_truth.py` uses the sacrum as its class 1 *and* as the structure that must land on the sacrum
for a projection convention to be accepted, and `spinesac_score.py` reports a sacrum-found rate. Had this
shipped, the convention search would have validated on vertebrae alone and the score would have read "sacrum
never found" — a wrong answer with no error. Separately, instance ids **≥ 100 are intervertebral discs**
(118–126 alongside 18–26) and also read 0.000. The tool now drops the discs, takes the corpus for the
vertebrae, and keeps the **sacrum whole**, recording which convention each level carries. The smoke test
asserts all three and passes (`SMOKE_OK failures=0`).

This is worth generalising beyond SpineSAC: the same 49/50 rule is what `tools/body_masks.py` applies to every
CT for the body-box convention (job 40416510). If the sacrum is a class in any render bank built that way, it
is being deleted there too, and any bank whose instance masks carry ids ≥ 100 is carrying discs as though they
were levels. Neither is checked anywhere at present.
