# Radiology: Artificial Intelligence — Instructions for Authors, as they bind this manuscript

Transcribed 2026-09-23 from the journal's Instructions for Authors page (pasted by Greg in full; the page is
https://pubs.rsna.org/page/ai/author-instructions). Every rule below is checked by `tools/check_limits.py`
where a machine can check it and by `FINAL_CHECK.md` where a human must. Article type: **Original Research**.
Submission is through ScholarOne (https://mc.manuscriptcentral.com/rad-ai); review is **double-anonymized**.

## Limits (Original Research)

| item | limit |
|---|---|
| Word count, Introduction through Discussion | **3,000** |
| Abstract | **structured, ≤ 250 words**, four sections: Purpose / Materials and Methods / Results / Conclusion |
| References | **35** |
| Figures (images, charts or graphs) | **6**; each figure has **no more than four components (a, b, c, d)** |
| Tables | **4** |
| Key Points | up to **3**, each a main point or conclusion **with summary data**; must not repeat the summary statement; **no vague language, no abbreviations** (CT and MRI are allowed) |
| Summary Statement | **1–2 sentences, ≤ 255 characters, boldface**, on the anonymized title page; **no abbreviations** |
| Abbreviations | **maximum 10**; spelled out at first use in the abstract and in the text; all abbreviations in figures and tables defined in the legend or footnote |
| Checklist | **CLAIM** (Checklist for AI in Medical Imaging, 2024 update) — required at first submission; the paper is returned without it |
| Supplemental material | ≤ 12 pages; one Word document for text, tables and figures with captions; not copy-edited |
| Text file | **.docx** (from January 2026 .doc is refused); double spaced; adequate margins; not right-justified; Arial, Calibri or Times New Roman 11 pt; no page numbers; equations embedded where cited; Greek letters and special characters from the symbol font |

## What the structured abstract must contain

- **Purpose**: one to two sentences.
- **Materials and Methods**: (1) retrospective or prospective; (2) date range; (3) number of patients or subjects with age and sex where appropriate; (4) the groups, including controls; (5) the procedures performed; (6) the evaluation, in the same order as the Results; (7) one sentence on statistical analysis.
- **Results**: findings parallel to the Methods, with indicators of statistical significance, **actual numbers and percentages**.
- **Conclusion**: derived directly from the results; addresses the purpose; **no** elaboration on significance or implications.

## Main body

Headings: Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion (subheadings allowed in Methods and Results).

**Introduction.** Background and the references that say why the study was done; no extensive literature review; brief and focused; **the final paragraph states the hypothesis and purpose**.

**Materials and Methods** must include everything the Results present, and:
- retrospective or prospective, stated plainly; **the date range of the study**;
- the subject group, with age and sex (mean and range, by sex where appropriate);
- sample selection: inclusion and exclusion criteria, consecutive or random, **the number excluded for each cause**;
- **how sample size was derived**;
- the number, training and expertise (years) of the people who executed and read the index tests; authors by initials, non-authors in the acknowledgments; residents marked "in-training";
- what was evaluated and how: anonymization of data, independent readings versus consensus, how disagreements were handled (single-observer studies and consensus reads are discouraged);
- the **pre-specified primary outcome measure(s)**, secondary and other variables;
- for human-subject studies, **in the first paragraph**: institutional review board approval and consent; HIPAA compliance for U.S. studies; clinical-trials registry name and number if any; **whether any of the population was previously reported, with the citation** (subject overlap is also declared in the cover letter);
- instruments and software with trade names, manufacturer and location; procedures in enough detail to reproduce;
- references for established methods; description and justification of new or modified ones;
- **the last paragraph: the statistical methods, the software with version and manufacturer, the P value used for significance, and a justification of sample size or a power calculation** (strongly recommended).

**Results.** **First paragraph: the demographics of the study population.** Logical sequence, tables and figures cited; results for every item mentioned in the Methods, in the same order with the same subheadings; statistical significance stated; results reported for **all** variables analysed, not only the significant ones; **numerators and denominators for every percentage**, including sensitivity, specificity, accuracy and predictive values; regression coefficients or group means given with their P values; "significant" reserved for statistical significance.

**Discussion.** Does not repeat the Results in detail; **does not cite tables or figures**. First paragraph: the problem studied and the primary results. Implications and limitations, with reference to modified methods; relation to other studies; conclusions tied to the study's goals without unqualified statements; no claim of priority; no results of ongoing or incomplete investigations; new hypotheses labelled as such. **The second-to-last paragraph: study limitations** (recommendations may follow). **The final paragraph: the summary of results and findings.**

**Style.** No laboratory slang or clinical jargon. **No self-evaluation ("novel", "unique", "ground-breaking").** Written so a radiologist outside the specialty can follow it. **No self-references that break anonymity** ("in our prior study"); **no mention of the authors' institution or funding in the manuscript**; author initials anonymized if they are readers. SI units.

## Anonymization (double-anonymized review)

The Abbreviated Title Page and the Acknowledgments are anonymized. Patient anonymity is preserved. Language that reveals the group's earlier work is avoided. Institution and funding appear only on the Full Title Page, which is submitted separately.

## Figures

- For a **new submission**, all figures go in **one text document in citation order, each legend immediately after its figure**; for a revision, production files are uploaded individually, named with the figure number (Figure 1.tif, Figure 2a.tif), legend in the caption box, multi-part figures as separate files per part.
- Number as figure 1a, 1b, 2 …; **no more than four components per figure**.
- Crop to the region of interest with enough anatomy for orientation; label every feature the legend describes with distinct labels; **no patient or subject identifying information**; same magnification for images of one type; tonal relations of the original radiograph (bone white on dark; patient's right to the observer's left; CT and MR viewed from below); no redundant figures.
- Enhancements explained and the original supplied alongside.
- Production files: photographic/halftone images as PSD, TIF, AI or EPS (**PNG is not accepted**), **300 dpi**, 3–7 inches, labels and arrows on a separate unflattened layer touching the feature (no equilateral-triangle arrowheads); graphs and illustrations as PSD, TIF, PPT, AI or EPS at **1,200 dpi**, layers kept, not upsampled; Excel or Word originals may be sent in native format.
- Collages accepted when arrows or tags carry the meaning; layers kept; 300 dpi where there is text; 3 inches per image; labels in a **bold sans-serif such as Arial at 12 pt or larger**.
- Colour is acceptable for charts and graphs; symbols, letters and numbers legible at column width; every symbol defined in the legend or on the graph itself.
- Previously published figures are discouraged (permission and possibly a fee).

**Legends**: one for every figure including drawings and graphs; all abbreviations defined; no duplication of the text; for human images the **age, sex and clinical history** where relevant; every label on the illustration described; the type of image, plane, contrast, sequence, and **the features the reader should observe**; scale markers where appropriate; for graphs, the important points to observe.

## Tables

Prepared in Word, attached at the end of the text document, **one per page, each with a title**, Arabic numerals; **numerators and denominators for every percentage** either in the table or in the Results; understandable without the text; every abbreviation defined in a footnote; **no merged cells** (use sub-headings).

## References

Numbered consecutively in order of first mention; periodical abbreviations in National Library of Medicine style; accuracy is the authors' responsibility (hyperlinks fail unless the bibliographic data match); include the DOI. Formats:

- Print article: `Tyler LN, Harville TO, … Elliott M. Multiple alloantibodies after transfusion in an infant treated with infliximab. N Engl J Med 2007;357:2092-2093. doi: 10.1056/NEJMc070741`
- Online article: as print, plus `Published November 1, 2004. Accessed November 10, 2004.`
- Book chapter: authors, chapter title, editors, book title, edition, city and state, publisher, year, full page range (1537-1544, not 1537-44).
- Web content: author(s) if any; title; site name or owner; URL; publication, update and access dates.

## What is submitted, and as separate files

1. **Cover letter**: title; complete author list; justification if two first authors; **subject-overlap statement** naming every prior publication, submission or in-press paper whose subjects overlap (with PDFs at submission, and the overlap also stated in the Methods); conflicts of interest and industry support; **confirmation of sole submission**; any fast-track request.
2. **Full Title Page**: title; every author's first and last name, middle initial, degrees and institution with superscripts in author order; the originating institution's name and street address; the corresponding author's telephone, e-mail and full postal address; funding; **manuscript type**; **word count of the text**; unanonymized acknowledgments; **data sharing statement** (per the Editorial Policies page).
3. **Point-by-point response letter** (revisions only).
4. **CLAIM checklist** (Original Research and Technical Developments).
5. **Figures document** (new submissions: one combined document with legends after each figure).
6. **Supplemental materials** (one Word document; multimedia separately, mp4 preferred, < 30 MB, each with a legend).
7. **The manuscript file**, one document, in this order: Abbreviated Title Page (anonymized: title, article type, the boldface summary statement ≤ 255 characters, the Key Points); Abstract; Main Body; Acknowledgments (anonymized); References; Figure Legends; Tables (embedded, one per page).

Also required at submission: an ORCID iD linked to the submitting author's ScholarOne account (every author of an accepted paper); one corresponding or senior author; at most two first authors; more than 40 authors needs the Editor's consent; **CRediT** contributor roles (since October 2025).

## Preprints and prior versions

Posting on arXiv before submission is allowed and is not prior publication. The submitted version, if posted on the authors' or institutional sites, carries on its first page: *"This work has been submitted to Radiology: Artificial Intelligence for possible publication. Copyright may be transferred without notice, after which this version may no longer be accessible."* The journal asks authors to **disclose where and when earlier versions were presented** (conference papers, preprints) and to give access to them; if a substantial portion was published before, describe in detail how the present work differs. iThenticate is run on every submission; material from the authors' own group must be quoted and referenced.

## Statistics

Follow the journal's "Top 10 list of statistical errors" (Radiology 2009;253:288–290): denominators everywhere, P values with the estimates, no "significant" without a test, sample-size justification, correct handling of clustered data. Statistical consultation before the study is recommended; the journal may have raw data recalculated.

## Ethics

EQUATOR Network practices; IRB and consent in the first Methods paragraph; HIPAA for U.S. studies; animal-care approval for animal work; previously reported subjects cited.
