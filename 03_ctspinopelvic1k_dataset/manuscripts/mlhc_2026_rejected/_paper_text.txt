Abstract 1
Machine learning models for spine segmentation face two compounding blind spots: a2
modality gap(predominance of MRI-derived benchmarks despite CT being the clinical3
standard for surgical planning) and ananomaly gap(near-complete absence of Lum-4
bosacral Transitional Vertebra (LSTV) annotation in existing CT datasets despite a 5–5
35% population prevalence). We introduceCTSpinoPelvic1K, a CT-native benchmark6
of 1,137 colonoscopy-protocol abdominal CT volumes across 804 patients with a unified7
10-class spinopelvic segmentation labelmap and dual-origin LSTV phenotype labels.8
The benchmark is assembled from CTSpine1K and CTPelvic1K—two independently cu-9
rated public datasets sharing a patient cohort but using incompatible NIfTI coordinate10
conventions—via a two-phase patient-anchored pipeline: canonical UID matching resolves11
identity with algebraic certainty, after which intrapatient exhaustive bone-HU maximiza-12
tion selects the source CT series for each annotation with no heuristic fallbacks. A key13
methodological contribution is apartial-annotation training protocol: only 338 of the14
1,137 cases carry fused spine-plus-pelvis ground truth, but we describe how the remaining15
799 spine-only and pelvic-only cases can be incorporated into training via nnU-Net v2’s16
ignore labelmechanism by rewriting unobserved background voxels, yielding a 3.36×17
increase in effective training data without biasing the model toward predicting background18
on unobserved structures. We further characterize systematic 0% cross-source agreement19
between two independent radiology teams at the sacralization boundary—not annotation20
noise but radiologically meaningful definitional indeterminacy between morphological and21
vertebral-counting operationalizations—and release both annotations with explicit arbitra-22
tion metadata. A zero-shot TotalSegmentator benchmark concretely quantifies the anomaly23
gap: junction Dice drops 19.6 points (0.770→0.574) from Normal to Any-LSTV cases,24
with lumbarization cases degrading to 0.421 junction Dice owing to TotalSegmentator’s25
structural inability to emit an L6 class. TheCTSpinoPelvic1Kdataset is publicly re-26
leased on HuggingFace; the preprocessing pipeline, conversion scripts, and nnU-Net training27
infrastructure will be released under a permissive license upon acceptance.28

1. Introduction39
The lumbosacral junction is among the most clinically consequential regions of the axial40
skeleton. Surgical procedures at this level—transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF),41
pedicle screw instrumentation, laminectomy, and sacropelvic fixation—require preoperative42
planning at millimeter accuracy, and errors at the lumbosacral level carry the highest medi-43
colegal burden in spine surgery (Carrino et al., 2011). Accurate automated segmentation of44
the lumbar vertebrae, sacrum, and pelvic girdle supports pedicle screw trajectory optimiza-45
tion, sacropelvic parameter measurement, intervertebral implant sizing, and intraoperative46
neuronavigation registration.47
The LSTV problem.A Lumbosacral Transitional Vertebra (LSTV) is a vertebra at48
the L5–S1 junction exhibiting morphological characteristics of the adjacent segment (?).49
Sacralizationoccurs when the fifth lumbar vertebra undergoes bony fusion with the sacrum,50
reducing the mobile lumbar spine to four segments.Lumbarizationoccurs when the first51
sacral segment detaches, mimicking an independent sixth lumbar vertebra. Prevalence52
estimates range from 5% to 35% depending on imaging modality and classification crite-53
rion (Sekharappa et al., 2014). Wrong-level surgery attributable to transitional anatomy54
misidentification occurs in an estimated 4.5–15% of cases where preoperative level counting55
relied on plain film or MRI alone (Konin and Walz, 2010).56
The modality gap.The most widely used spine segmentation benchmarks—SPIDER,57
Spine Generic, and TotalSpineSeg—are built entirely on MRI (Graaf et al.; Cohen-Adad58
et al., 2021; Warszawer et al., 2025). Castellvi morphology is defined by cortical bridging and59
transverse process ossification, features CT resolves at sub-millimeter resolution and MRI60
does not. A model trained on MRI and deployed on preoperative CT acquires an implicit61
domain shift that degrades lumbosacral boundary delineation at the junction where clinical62
decisions are made.63
The anomaly gap.CT-trained benchmarks including CTSpine1K (Deng et al., 2021),64
VerSe (Sekuboyina et al., 2020), TotalSegmentator-CT (Wasserthal et al., 2023), and CT-65
Pelvic1K (Liu et al., 2021) do not report or control for LSTV prevalence, do not stratify66
splits by transitional anatomy, and make no attempt to evaluate per-type performance at67
the lumbosacral junction. No prior ML benchmark has curated LSTV in CT with dense68
spinopelvic segmentation labels at scale, nor analyzed disagreement structure between in-69
dependent LSTV annotation operationalizations.70
The annotation uncertainty problem.Even when LSTV labels are attempted, inde-71
pendent radiologists applying different operationalizations of the same morphological con-72
cept can systematically disagree at the lumbosacral boundary. Existing datasets resolve this73
by publishing a single adjudicated label, discarding the disagreement signal. The medical74
machine learning community increasingly recognizes that inter-rater disagreement is signal,75
not noise (Dawid and Skene, 1979):CTSpinoPelvic1Kapplies this principle specifically76
to the LSTV confusion zone, where disagreement reflects genuine definitional indeterminacy77
rather than annotator error.78

3. Dataset Construction: Patient-Anchored Fusion of CTSpine1K and161
CTPelvic1K 162
CTSpine1K and CTPelvic1K share a common COLONOG patient cohort but were indepen-163
dently curated: masks are released as NIfTI files without theSeriesInstanceUIDlinkage164
to the primary DICOM data, and the two teams used differentdcm2niixinvocations, pro-165
ducing silently incompatible coordinate conventions (Section 5.3). Fusing these annotation166
sets therefore requires solving two problems: (i)identity—determining which pelvic mask167
belongs to which patient in which dataset—and (ii)alignment—determining which specific168
CT acquisition each mask was drawn from, in world coordinates. We solve both without169
spatial registration or heuristic metadata fallbacks. 170
Identity via canonical UID join.Both CTSpine1K and CTPelvic1K mask filenames171
encode the TCIAPatientIDwith zero-padded UID suffixes whose padding conventions dif-172
fer between dataset releases, causing naive string matching to fail. Canonical UID matching173
(Eq. 1) reduces the problem to anO(N) exact relational join, algebraically guaranteed cor-174
rect up to padding. On CTSpine1K∪CTPelvic1K this resolves 825/825 patients (100%)175
with zero heuristic fallbacks. 176
Alignment via intrapatient bone-HU maximization.Restricting candidate series177
to the patient’s own TCIA acquisitions reduces the search space from a global index (∼105–178
106 CT series) to|S p| ≈4 candidates. Within this bounded set, bone-HU maximization179
(Eq. 2) serves as a metadata-free oracle for mask-to-series assignment—useful because the180
DICOM metadata fields normally relied upon (SeriesDescription,BodyPartExamined)181
are frequently missing or mislabeled in TCIA releases. Placement succeeds on 789/792182
spine masks (99.6%) and 714/714 pelvic masks (100%); the failure modes are documented183
in Section 7. 184

CTSpine1K VerSe labels and CTPelvic1K labels are remapped into a unified 10-class243
scheme: 0 = background; 1–5 = L1–L5 (VerSe 20–24); 6 = L6 / lumbarized S1 (VerSe 25;244

9. Discussion349
Partial-annotation training unlocks the full data scale.For federated annotation350
sources—the normal case when fusing independently curated datasets—discarding partial351
cases imposes a silent two-thirds data penalty at theCTSpinoPelvic1Kscale. Ignore-label352
training lets every annotated voxel contribute supervision only where its class is observed.353
The pattern generalizes beyond spine/pelvis fusion: any multi-source dataset where different354
teams annotate different structures on the same underlying images can apply this mechanism355
directly.356
The confusion zone is a scientific contribution.The 0% cross-source sacralization357
agreement documents, at scale, that two legitimate expert-level annotation strategies for the358
same anatomical feature produce non-overlapping classifications at the Castellvi Type I–II359
boundary. No prior spine ML benchmark has reported this because no prior benchmark360
retained both annotation sources with origin. Thelstv agreementflag makes the361
ambiguity explicit, enabling uncertainty-aware training and calibration benchmarking on362
known-ambiguous cases.363
The anomaly gap is empirically measurable.The zero-shot TotalSegmentator bench-364
mark directly quantifies the clinical cost of the anomaly gap: the most widely deployed365
multi-organ CT segmentation model loses 19.6 junction-DSC points from Normal to Any-366
LSTV cases, and 34.9 points on lumbarization specifically (0.770→0.421). Of the lumbarization-367
case error, a mechanically identifiable fraction traces to a single architectural decision—the368
absence of an L6 output class—illustrating how label vocabulary choices made during bench-369
mark design propagate as hard failure modes years later in deployment.370
Implications for surgical planning AI.A segmentation model that outputs high un-371
certainty at L5/S1 for a Castellvi Type II case is more useful to a surgeon than one that372
outputs a hard incorrect label with high confidence.CTSpinoPelvic1Kprovides the an-373
notation structure necessary to train and evaluate such models.374
The coordinate convention problem is general.The Y-axis reflection between CT-375
Spine1K and CTPelvic1K is an instance of a broad failure class: whenever independent376
teams convert the same DICOM source using different toolchains, silently inverted NIfTI377
axes result. Any multi-source fusion pipeline must validate affine-derived world coordinates378
for representative landmarks.379
Limitations.CTSpinoPelvic1Kderives from colonoscopy-protocol CT (soft-tissue ker-380
nel), which may not generalize to bone-kernel protocols used in some surgical planning381
workflows. LSTV labels are inherited without independent re-reading; the 0% sacraliza-382
tion agreement highlights sensitivity to annotation protocol. Semi-sacralization has limited383
representation (n= 3). TotalSegmentator was evaluated in fast mode (3 mm isotropic384
resampling); full-resolution (1.5 mm) inference would be expected to recover 1–2 DSC385
points on standard anatomy, but the label-vocabulary-driven lumbarization failure mode386
is resolution-invariant. The present submission does not include a trained nnU-Net base-387
line; the infrastructure for partial-annotation training is described in Section 5.5 and the388
training pipeline is fully implemented, but empirical evaluation is deferred to future work.389
Consequently, the head-to-head comparison of the ignore-label protocol against fused-only390
