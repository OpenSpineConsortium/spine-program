We thank the reviewer for the detailed, constructive review and respond in order.

(1) Ignore-label validation and trained baseline. The nnU-Net v2 model is trained (5-fold cross-validation, ignore-label protocol). All code and the 5-fold checkpoints are public now, reachable from the README of the dataset cited in the paper; the trained-baseline evaluation and the fused-only versus all-cases ablation that directly tests the ignore-label claim follow at camera-ready.

(2) Figure 3 caption. Correct, this is an error: all scans are soft-tissue kernel. We will change "bone-protocol CT series" to "soft-tissue-kernel CT series."

(3) Generalizability. Agreed; the colonoscopy-protocol soft-tissue source is a stated limitation, and we will keep it explicit rather than overclaim surgical-CT transfer.

(4) PatientID vs bone-HU. Yes - the DICOM PatientID is the strongest identifier that survived into the masks, and it resolves the patient. But the scan-level SeriesInstanceUID did not survive the DICOM-to-NIfTI conversion. With 825 patients spanning 3,451 TCIA series, PatientID only bounds the search to a patient; bone-HU must still place each of the 1,498 masks.

(5) Motivating identity vs alignment (a paragraph we will add). Identity is trivial (the PatientID). The real problem: no mask carries a SeriesInstanceUID, so there is no unambiguous link from a mask to a specific TCIA scan, and CTPelvic1K's pelvic masks ship without their CTs entirely. The pipeline can only infer the source scan from weak proxies (a filename SeriesNumber, NIfTI affine geometry, slice count), which leaves most assignments only probable and 107 fully ambiguous (5 certain); bone-HU then resolves placement deterministically. Our reproducible pipeline re-anchors these orphaned, unusable-as-released masks into one unified, ML-ready benchmark on HuggingFace - that is the contribution.

(6) Related work. Agreed, the orthopedic-trauma and oncology benchmarks are not directly relevant to LSTV, spinopelvic fusion, or partial-annotation training; we will remove them.

(7) LSTV framing vs N=53. Fair, with a clarification: a volume is one labeled CT scan, and a separate-mode patient (spine and pelvic masks on different acquisitions) is two scans - so the 53 LSTV volumes come from 33 distinct patients. The primary contribution is the fused, fully-annotated spinopelvic dataset (1,153 cases) with LSTV as a radiologist-Castellvi-classified stratified subgroup, not a 53-case LSTV-only set; to our knowledge it is the largest CT LSTV set with dense labels and an independent Castellvi read, and the findings (0% cross-source agreement; the anomaly gap) do not depend on large N. We will reframe as an LSTV-aware spinopelvic benchmark and state the N limitation explicitly.
