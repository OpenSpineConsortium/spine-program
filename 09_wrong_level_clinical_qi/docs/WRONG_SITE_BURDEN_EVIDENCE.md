# Evidence: the annual burden of wrong-site and wrong-level spine surgery (what elimination would save)

Deep-research run 2026-09-19 (workflow wf_203d096e-7c0); three adversarial verifiers per claim. Extrapolations are labelled as such in the claims.

## Summary

The verified evidence does not support a headline figure in the billions for wrong-site surgery. The only published total-cost number, Mehtsun et al. (Surgery 2013), is $1.3 billion of NPDB malpractice payments for ALL surgical never events (retained objects, wrong site, wrong procedure, wrong patient) cumulated over 21 years (1990-2010), i.e. about $62M/yr, of which wrong-site is roughly a quarter (~$15M/yr) and wrong-site + wrong-procedure + wrong-patient together about half (~$31M/yr, 2010 dollars). Event counts are bounded below by voluntary reports (Joint Commission: 127 wrong surgeries in 2024, 86 wrong-site, 9 wrong-level of which 7 spinal; NHS England: 185 wrong-site Never Events in FY2024/25) and above by claims-scaled extrapolation (~2,000 US wrong-site/procedure/patient events per year, from Mehtsun's 4,082 never-event estimate). For spine, converting the two verified per-procedure rates (1 in 3,110 self-report; 0.12% Japanese registry) to a US volume of 1.3-1.6M procedures gives roughly 420-1,900 wrong-level operations a year, and applying the verified litigation (17%) and payment (81% of litigated) fractions and the ~$133-136k mean paid claim gives on the order of $8-36M/yr in indemnity alone. Hospital direct costs (reoperation, added stay), indirect costs and worldwide totals have no verified source in this run, so the defensible answer is: US indemnity savings in the tens of millions per year, total savings plausibly low hundreds of millions once unquantified hospital and disability costs are added, and no evidence-based worldwide figure exists.

US national event estimate (extrapolation): Mehtsun et al. found 9,744 paid NPDB claims for surgical never events 1990-2010 and, scaling by literature rates of adverse events that produce paid claims, estimated ~4,082 surgical never events per year; with wrong-site 24.8%, wrong-procedure 25.1% and wrong-patient 0.3% of paid claims, this implies roughly 1,010 wrong-site, ~1,025 wrong-procedure and ~12-33 wrong-patient events per year (about 2,050 wrong-site/procedure/patient events combined). Corroborating lower-bound series: Kwaan (malpractice insurer, 2.83M operations 1985-2004) 1 nonspine wrong-site event per 112,994 operations (95% CI 1/76,336-1/174,825); Colorado COPIC ~20 wrong-site/wrong-patient events per year (132 in 6.4 years).

Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23257079/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16618892/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20956767/

Evidence: Mehtsun abstract verbatim: 'we estimated that 4,082 surgical never event claims occur each year in the United States' (authors label it conservative; press release says 4,044). Category shares (2,413 wrong-site, 2,447 wrong-procedure, 27 wrong-patient of 9,744) from PMC10443983 citing Mehtsun; AHRQ NBK305567 independently states ~1,005 wrong-site, 1,020 wrong-procedure, 33 wrong-patient per year. Arithmetic: 4,082 x 0.248 = 1,012. Kwaan and Stahel numbers verified verbatim from PubMed abstracts. Marked medium because the annual number is a model-based extrapolation from paid claims, not a count, and Kwaan excludes spine and predates the Universal Protocol. Scaling Kwaan's rate or COPIC's ~20/yr to a national figure requires a US surgical-volume or population denominator that was NOT verified in this run. Merged claims [3], [5], [7], [9].

Spine-specific per-procedure rates: wrong-level spine surgery occurred in 0.12% (95% CI 0.09-0.16; 40 of 34,544) of degenerative spine operations in a 27-hospital Japanese registry 2012-2022 (thoracic 0.42%, lumbar 0.13%, cervical 0.05%), versus 1 in 3,110 (0.032%; 418 of an estimated 1.3M career procedures) by anonymous AANS surgeon self-report (415 respondents, 12% response). Almost 50% of Spine Section surgeons (569 responding, 54%) report having done wrong-level lumbar surgery at least once.

Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42061893/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18197106/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22480979/

Evidence: Furuya et al., Bone Joint J 2026;108-B(5):673-680: overall 0.12% (0.09-0.16), thoracic 0.42% (0.16-1.08), lumbar 0.13% (0.09-0.18), cervical 0.05% (0.02-0.12), verified against publisher page; degenerative disorders only, hospital-recognised events (lower bound). Mody et al., Spine 2008: 'From an estimated 1,300,000 spine procedures, 418 wrong level spine operations had been performed, with a prevalence of 1 in 3110' (arithmetic checks). Groff et al., World Neurosurg 2013: 'Almost 50% of reporting surgeons have performed wrong-level lumbar spine surgery at least once.' The registry rate is ~4x the self-report rate; the two bracket the plausible true rate. Merged claims [15], [16], [17], [21].

### Fraction of events that generate a paid claim: 17% of self-reported wrong-level spine surgeries (73 of 418) le…  (confidence medium, votes 3-0, 2-1, 3-0)

Fraction of events that generate a paid claim: 17% of self-reported wrong-level spine surgeries (73 of 418) led to legal action or monetary settlement; among litigated wrong-disc-level cases (Goodkin & Laska 2004, n=68, assembled largely from a malpractice verdict newsletter) 55 of 68 (81%) ended in payment (37 settled, 18 plaintiff verdicts, 13 defence verdicts = 42% of jury trials). Combined, roughly 14% of wrong-level events produce a paid claim (0.17 x 0.81); the 68-of-69-litigated figure is a sampling artifact and must not be read as a litigation rate.

Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18197106/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15031066/

Evidence: Mody abstract: '73 cases resulted in legal action or monetary settlement to the patient (17%)' (composite outcome, not a payment rate). Goodkin abstract: 'Thirty-seven cases were settled. A plaintiff verdict was rendered in 18 cases and a defense verdict in 13 cases (42% of the cases that were decided by a jury)'; 37+18+13 = 68; 55/68 = 80.9%. Methods show 65 of 69 cases came from Medical Malpractice Verdicts, Settlements and Experts, so the series is litigation-selected (claim [19] passed only 2-1 on this ground). Settled is equated with paid by convention. Merged claims [18], [19], [20].

Total annual US savings from elimination (derived, ranges): (a) Indemnity floor from NPDB: $1.3B / 21 yr = ~$62M/yr for all surgical never events; wrong-site share 24.8% = ~$15M/yr; wrong-site + procedure + patient ~50% = ~$31M/yr (2010 dollars, individual-practitioner payments only). (b) Event-based: ~2,050 wrong-site/procedure/patient events x 14% paid x ~$133-136k = ~$38-39M/yr; if every event were paid, an implausible ceiling of ~$275M/yr. (c) Wrong-level spine: 420-1,920 events x 14% x $133-136k = ~$8M-$37M/yr indemnity. (d) Hospital direct and indirect costs: no verified per-event figure; with 36-46% of events requiring additional surgery or causing permanent harm, a total 2-5x the indemnity figure is plausible but unsupported by a source here. Defensible statement: eliminating wrong-site surgery in the US would save tens of millions of dollars a year in malpractice indemnity, plausibly low hundreds of millions in total direct cost, and the frequently quoted $1.3 billion is a 21-year cumulative for all never events, not an annual wrong-site figure.
