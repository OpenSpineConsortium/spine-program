# Evidence for the introduction: the cost of wrong-level spine surgery and of level localisation

Deep-research run 2026-09-19 (workflow wf_7c2848b4-012): five search angles, 15 sources fetched, every claim put to three adversarial verifiers (2 of 3 refutations kill a claim). Each surviving finding below carries its vote and sources; refuted claims are listed at the end so they are not re-introduced by accident. ## Summary

Wrong-level spine surgery is common at the career level (about half of responding US neurosurgical spine surgeons in two independent surveys, PMID 18197106 and 22480979) and rare per procedure: 1 in 3,110 by self-report (3.2/10,000), 0.12% (12/10,000; 95% CI 0.09-0.16) in a 34,544-case Japanese multicentre registry, which also gives the first thoracic-specific rate, 0.42% (42/10,000; CI 0.16-1.08), three times lumbar and eight times cervical (PMID 42061893) - so the draft sentence that the thoracic spine "has no published rate of its own" must be revised. The widely quoted 0.09-4.5 per 10,000 band (PMID 20407349) is a wrong-SITE-surgery range across all specialties, not a whole-spine estimate, and should be reworded. Financially, 17% of self-reported wrong-level events led to legal action or settlement, nearly 20% of Spine Section respondents have been sued for wrong-level/wrong-side lumbar surgery, 81% of litigated wrong-disc-level cases (55/68) ended in a payment with surgeons winning only 42% of jury trials (Goodkin & Laska 2004), NPDB surgical never-event payments have a pooled median of $75,220 (IQR $28,847-$215,003; 2025 dollars) with no spine- or wrong-level-specific median retrievable, and 978 NHS spinal-surgery claims over five years were valued at £535.5M (~£107M/yr, ~£0.55M/claim), 26 of them (2.66%) never events including wrong-site surgery. Valued at the JAMA Surgery hospital OR-time cost of $33.78-$37.45/min (FY2014, before physician fees), the Mayo series' 51.9-minute gold-fiducial placement is worth ~$1,750-$1,950 per patient and ~155 staffed hours (about $350k in 2014 dollars) across the 179 patients over 38 months, a proxy because the procedure occurs in a fluoroscopy/CT suite rather than an OR. The standard intraoperative thoracic technique remains needles placed every few levels counting cephalad from the sacrum on lateral fluoroscopy, with surgeons disagreeing on the landmark (pedicle 67%, spinous process 49%), a 2012 systematic review found site-verification protocols failed to prevent about one-third of cases in the only study that assessed them, and the literature's remedy (DeVine 2010) is still post-exposure imaging against a marked fixed structure - i.e., counting.

DRAFT INTRODUCTION PARAGRAPH (figures tied to sources above): "Wrong-level spine surgery persists because intraoperative fluoroscopy shows a few vertebrae at a time and the surgeon counts from whatever landmark is visible. Half of responding neurosurgeons in two independent surveys report at least one wrong-level operation in their career [Mody 2008; Groff 2013]; self-reported incidence is 1 in 3,110 procedures [Mody 2008], and a prospectively collected registry of 34,544 degenerative spine operations recorded 0.12%, with the thoracic spine highest at 0.42% (95% CI 0.16-1.08), three times the lumbar and eight times the cervical rate [Furuya 2026]. The consequences are not confined to the patient: 17% of self-reported events led to legal action or settlement [Mody 2008], nearly one in five Spine Section respondents has been sued for a wrong-level or wrong-side lumbar operation [Groff 2013], 81% of litigated wrong-disc-level cases ended in a payment and surgeons prevailed in only 42% of those decided by a jury [Goodkin 2004]; the median indemnity for a surgical never event reported to the National Practitioner Data Bank is $75,220 (IQR $28,847-$215,003) [Bettis 2026], and in England 978 spinal-surgery claims over five years were valued at £535.5 million, 26 of them never events including wrong-site surgery [Machin 2018]. The thoracic spine also reveals the difficulty in the cost of avoiding it: one quaternary centre performed 179 preoperative percutaneous gold-fiducial placements over 38 months, 176 of them thoracic, at a mean of 51.9 minutes and 183 mGy each, purely to mark a level for the surgeon [AJNR 2023]. At a hospital operating-room cost of $34-37 per minute before physician fees [Childers 2018], each such placement represents roughly $1,750-$1,950 of staffed procedural time and the series about 155 hours - a second invasive procedure scheduled in advance because the level was judged un-nameable in theatre. In theatre, the standard technique is unchanged: needles are placed every few levels and counted cephalad from the sacrum on lateral fluoroscopy, with surgeons disagreeing even on which landmark to count (pedicle 67%, spinous process 49%) [Strong 2021; Mayer 2014]; the only study to assess site-verification protocols found they failed to prevent about one-third of cases [Longo 2012], and the literature's recommended safeguard, imaging after exposure against a marked fixed structure [DeVine 2010], is itself another count."

## Verified findings

### About half of responding US neurosurgical spine surgeons report having performed at least one wrong-level spin…  (confidence high, votes 3-0, 3-0)

About half of responding US neurosurgical spine surgeons report having performed at least one wrong-level spine surgery in their career: 207/415 (50%) of AANS respondents (12% response, n=3,505 surveyed) in 2008, and almost 50% of 569 AANS/CNS Spine Section respondents (54% response) for wrong-level lumbar surgery specifically in 2012/2013.

Sources: Mody MG et al. Spine 2008;33(2):194-8. PMID 18197106. DOI 10.1097/BRS.0b013e31816043d1; Groff MW et al. World Neurosurg 2013;79(3-4):585-92. PMID 22480979. DOI 10.1016/j.wneu.2012.03.017

Evidence: Mody abstract: 'Two hundred seven (50%) reported that they had done 1 or more wrong level surgeries during their career.' Groff abstract: 'Almost 50% of reporting surgeons have performed wrong-level lumbar spine surgery at least once; >10% have performed wrong-side lumbar spine surgery at least once.' Both are self-report career-lifetime figures; Mody's 12% response invites self-selection, Groff's 54% is more robust. Merged claims [0] and [3].

### Per-procedure incidence: self-reported 1 in 3,110 (3.2 per 10,000; 418 events against an estimated 1.3 million…  (confidence high, votes 3-0, 3-0)

Per-procedure incidence: self-reported 1 in 3,110 (3.2 per 10,000; 418 events against an estimated 1.3 million procedures, 71% lumbar/21% cervical/8% thoracic); prospectively collected 34,544-case Japanese multicentre degenerative-spine registry (27 hospitals, 2012-2022): 40 events, 0.12% (12 per 10,000; 95% CI 0.09-0.16), intraoperative disorientation the cause in 81.3%.

Sources: Mody MG et al. Spine 2008;33(2):194-8. PMID 18197106; Furuya M, Fujimori T, Takenaka S, et al. Bone Joint J 2026;108-B(5):673-680. PMID 42061893. DOI 10.1302/0301-620X.108B5.BJJ-2025-1129.R2

Evidence: Mody: '1 in 3110 procedures'. Furuya: 'A total of 40 cases of WLSS were identified, giving an overall incidence of 0.12% (95% CI 0.09 to 0.16).' The registry figure is roughly four times the survey figure, consistent with self-report undercounting. Merged claims [1] and [5].

### A thoracic-specific wrong-level rate now exists: 0.42% (42 per 10,000; 95% CI 0.16-1.08) versus lumbar 0.13% (…  (confidence high, votes 3-0)

A thoracic-specific wrong-level rate now exists: 0.42% (42 per 10,000; 95% CI 0.16-1.08) versus lumbar 0.13% (CI 0.09-0.18) and cervical 0.05% (CI 0.02-0.12), p=0.004, i.e. roughly three times lumbar and eight times cervical at the point estimate. The draft introduction's statement that the thoracic spine 'has no published rate of its own' is therefore false and must be revised.

Sources: Furuya M et al. Bone Joint J 2026;108-B(5):673-680. PMID 42061893

Evidence: Abstract verbatim: 'Regional incidence varied significantly: thoracic 0.42% (95% CI 0.16 to 1.08), lumbar 0.13% (95% CI 0.09 to 0.18), and cervical 0.05% (95% CI 0.02 to 0.12; p = 0.004)'. Thoracic CI is wide (small denominator, ~5 events implied) and overlaps lumbar; cohort is degenerative surgery in a Japanese network. Single source but primary, peer-reviewed, May 2026. Claim [6].

### The 0.09-4.5 per 10,000 range (literature 1990-Dec 2008) is a wrong-SITE-surgery range across all specialties…  (confidence high, votes 3-0, 3-0)

The 0.09-4.5 per 10,000 range (literature 1990-Dec 2008) is a wrong-SITE-surgery range across all specialties (its 0.09 floor is a general-surgery insurer dataset), not a whole-spine wrong-level estimate; the draft's 'published whole-spine estimates span 0.09 to 4.5 per 10,000' overstates the source and should read 'published wrong-site-surgery estimates'. A 2012 systematic review found only 12 incidence/prevalence studies of wrong-site/level spine surgery, ten lumbar-only and two mixed lumbar/thoracic/cervical, none thoracic-specific.

Sources: DeVine J, Chutkan N, Norvell DC, Dettori JR. Spine 2010;35(9 Suppl):S28-36. PMID 20407349. DOI 10.1097/BRS.0b013e3181d833ac; Longo UG et al. J Bone Joint Surg Br 2012;94-B(11):1546-50. PMID 23109638. DOI 10.1302/0301-620X.94B11.29553; Hempel S et al. AHRQ 2015 systematic review, PMID 26061125 (corroborating: spine wrong-level 0.62/10,000, orthopaedics 1.2/10,000)

Evidence: DeVine: 'The estimated rate of wrong site surgery varies widely ranging from 0.09 to 4.5 per 10,000 surgeries performed.' Longo: 'retrieved 12 studies ... ten studies were performed on patients undergoing lumbar spine surgery and two on patients undergoing lumbar, thoracic or cervical spine procedures.' Merged claims [7] and [9].

### Litigation frequency: 17% (73/418) of self-reported wrong-level events led to legal action or monetary settlem…  (confidence high, votes 3-0, 3-0, 3-0)

Litigation frequency: 17% (73/418) of self-reported wrong-level events led to legal action or monetary settlement and one caused permanent disability; nearly 20% of 569 Spine Section respondents have been the subject of at least one malpractice case arising from wrong-level or wrong-side lumbar surgery; in a legal-database series of 68 litigated wrong-disc-level cases (52 lumbar, 11 cervical, 3 thoracic), 37 settled, 18 plaintiff verdicts, 13 defence verdicts, so ~81% (55/68) resulted in a payment and surgeons prevailed in only 42% of jury-decided cases.

Sources: Mody MG et al. Spine 2008. PMID 18197106; Groff MW et al. World Neurosurg 2013. PMID 22480979; Goodkin R, Laska LL. Wrong disc space level surgery: medicolegal implications. Surg Neurol 2004;61:323-7. PMID 15031066. DOI 10.1016/j.surneu.2003.08.022

Evidence: Mody: '73 cases resulted in legal action or monetary settlement to the patient (17%)'. Groff: 'Nearly 20% of responding surgeons have been the subject of at least one malpractice case relating to these errors.' Goodkin: 'Thirty-seven cases were settled. A plaintiff verdict was rendered in 18 cases and a defense verdict in 13 cases (42% of the cases that were decided by a jury).' Goodkin's cases come from a verdicts newsletter (publicised lawsuits only) and are mostly 1990s; the 81% is a payment rate among reported lawsuits, not among all claims. Merged claims [2], [4], [13].

### Settlement size: the NPDB pooled median malpractice payment for a surgical never-event report (codes 322 retai…  (confidence high, votes 3-0, 3-0, 3-0)

Settlement size: the NPDB pooled median malpractice payment for a surgical never-event report (codes 322 retained foreign body, 330 wrong body part, 333 wrong patient, 334 wrong procedure; 1991-2025, 2025 dollars) is $75,220 (IQR $28,847-$215,003); wrong-procedure (334) has the highest median at $168,723. No wrong-body-part-specific or spine-specific median was retrievable. Sources: Bettis T, Tran Q, Glosser L, Maegawa F, Patel S. Patient Saf Surg 2026. DOI 10.1186/s13037-026-00508-9; Machin JT, Hardman J, Harrison W, Briggs TWR, Hutton M. Eur Spine J 2018;27(11):2693-2699. PMID 30151803. DOI 10.1007/s00586-018-5739-1

Evidence: Bettis abstract: 'The pooled median payment was $75,220 (IQR, $28,847-$215,003). Code 334 had the highest median payment ($168,723).' Machin abstract: 'Clinical negligence claims in spinal surgery were estimated to cost £535.5 million over this five-year period' and ''never events' including 'wrong site surgery' or 'retained instrument post-operation' (26 claims, 2.66%)'. NPDB captures only paid claims against individual practitioners. Merged claims [12], [14], [15].

### Operating-room time cost: across California hospitals in FY2014 the mean cost of one minute of OR time was $37…  (confidence high, votes 3-0, 2-1)

Operating-room time cost: across California hospitals in FY2014 the mean cost of one minute of OR time was $37.45 (SD $16.04) inpatient and $36.14 (SD $19.53) ambulatory, median inpatient $33.78 (IQR $26.12-$45.19), excluding surgeon and anaesthesiologist fees. A 52-minute procedure is therefore ~$1,750-$1,950 of OR time in 2014 dollars (roughly 30-40% more in 2026 dollars); the prior literature ranges $7 to >$100/min.

Sources: Childers CP, Maggard-Gibbons M. JAMA Surg 2018;153(4):e176233. PMC5875376. DOI 10.1001/jamasurg.2017.6233

Evidence: Verbatim: 'The mean (SD) cost for 1 minute of OR time across California hospitals was $37.45 ($16.04) in the inpatient setting and $36.14 ($19.53) in the ambulatory setting.' Table 2 median $33.78 (IQR $26.12-$45.19). 52 x 33.78 = $1,757; 52 x 37.45 = $1,947. Merged claims [16] and [17].

### Preoperative fiducial practice: Mayo Clinic placed preoperative gold fiducial markers in 179 patients between…  (confidence high, votes 3-0)

Preoperative fiducial practice: Mayo Clinic placed preoperative gold fiducial markers in 179 patients between 3 Jan 2019 and 16 Mar 2022 (38.4 months) solely for intraoperative level localization; 176 thoracic (T7 most common, T6-T8 peak), 2 lumbar, 1 cervical; 143 (80%) under fluoroscopy, 36 (20%) under CT; mean procedure time 51.9 min (range 20-114), mean dose 183.43 mGy (range 13-805); all 179 operations were at the correct level. Valued at Childers' OR-time cost, the series consumed ~9,290 minutes (~155 h), about $314k-$348k in 2014 dollars at the median/mean rate, as a proxy since the procedure occurs in a fluoroscopy/CT suite, not an OR.

Sources: AJNR Am J Neuroradiol 2023;44(5):618-622. PMID 37080723. PMC10171395. DOI 10.3174/ajnr.A7854; Childers CP, Maggard-Gibbons M. JAMA Surg 2018 (for the valuation)

Evidence: 'A total of 179 patients (104 female) underwent gold fiducial marker placement ... Most fiducial markers (143) were placed with fluoroscopy with the most common location at T6-T8.' Level table: thoracic 176 (T1 5, T2 5, T3 14, T4 14, T5 18, T6 21, T7 26, T8 21, T9 15, T10 19, T11 15, T12 7). Draft's 52 min / 183 mGy are correct roundings. Dollar valuation is derived arithmetic (179 x 51.9 = 9,290 min). Claim [18].

### Current intraoperative thoracic localization and why it fails: the classic technique places needles every few…  (confidence high, votes 2-1, 3-0, 3-0)

Current intraoperative thoracic localization and why it fails: the classic technique places needles every few levels counting cephalad from the sacrum on lateral fluoroscopy; surgeons disagree on the landmark counted (pedicle 67%, spinous process 49% in the thoracic spine; multiple answers allowed). A 2012 systematic review found the single study that assessed prevention showed site-verification protocols (time-out/checklists) failed to prevent about one-third of wrong-site spine cases and no published evidence supports protocol effectiveness; DeVine 2010 concluded NASS/Joint Commission checklists are insufficient alone and recommended intraoperative imaging after exposure with marking of a fixed anatomic structure compared against preoperative imaging - which is itself a count.

Sources: Strong MJ et al. Pre- and intraoperative thoracic spine localization techniques: a systematic review. J Neurosurg Spine 2021;36(5):792. PMC10193475; Mayer JE et al. Spine J 2014;14(5):741-748. DOI 10.1016/j.spinee.2013.06.068 (primary for the 67%/49% survey figures; 89% use fluoroscopy for thoracic); Longo UG et al. J Bone Joint Surg Br 2012;94-B(11):1546-50. DOI 10.1302/0301-620X.94B11.29553; DeVine J et al. Spine 2010;35(9 Suppl):S28-36. PMID 20407349

Evidence: Strong: 'Classically, a series of needles are placed every few levels counting from the sacrum in a cephalad fashion... most frequently performed with lateral fluoroscopic views' and 'variability as to which anatomical landmark to use for localization, either pedicle (67% in thoracic and 59% in lumbar) or the spinous process (49% in thoracic and 52% in lumbar)'. Longo: 'current site-verification protocols did not prevent about one-third of the cases.' DeVine: 'we recommend intraoperative imaging after exposure and marking of a fixed anatomic structure.' Merged claims [8], [10], [11]. Note the Strong-derived claims about 15-to-3-minute fluoroscopy time savings and the 41% rib-counting discrepancy / obesity / transitional-anatomy mechanism list were REFUTED at verification and must not be cited from that source without re-checking the original papers.

## Refuted (do not cite)

- (0-3) The literature provides no definitive estimate of the incidence of wrong-site spinal surgery and no published evidence that site-verification protocols are effective. — https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/0301-620X.94B11.29553
- (1-2) In a survey of spine surgeons, 68% admitted to wrong-level localization (some corrected intraoperatively) and roughly one in two has performed a wrong-level surgery. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10193475/
- (0-3) Preoperative gold fiducial placement reduced intraoperative fluoroscopic localization time from 15 minutes (traditional needle localization) to 3 minutes. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10193475/
- (0-3) Localization is confounded by anatomical variation: in one series the operative report differed from conventional rib counting by one level in 41% of cases, and 10% of patients have 4 (2.6%) or 6 (8.2%) lumbar segments; other cited factors are morbid obesity, prior surgery, osteoporosis, scapular shadowing, and variable numbers of rib-bearing vertebrae. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10193475/
- (1-2) Over 2004-2025 the NPDB recorded 15,196 malpractice payment reports coded to a surgical never event, of which 3,499 carried code 330 (Wrong Body Part), 7,646 code 322 (Surgical or Other Foreign Body Retained), 93 code 333 (Wrong Patient) and 3,983 code 334 (Wrong Procedure or Treatment). Code definitions are from the NPDB Public Use File format specifications, not the article text. — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13037-026-00508-9
- (0-3) Goodkin and Laska identified 69 cases of wrong disc space level surgery, of which 68 became the subject of lawsuits, indicating that nearly every recognised wrong-level disc operation in their series led to litigation. — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0090301903007092
