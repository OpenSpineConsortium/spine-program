# How much of each C-arm's learned distribution a styled frame reaches

Generated by `tools/device_spread_sweep.py` from the committed cards alone.

## The finding

`device_style.SPREAD = 0.2` clips every per-frame term to its central 60% of the fitted marginal. Because the clip is applied to all 8-11 terms at once, the share of the fitted JOINT distribution a styled frame can reach is between **1.7%** and **5.1%**, not 60%. The clip was added to stop draws that paired an extreme photon count with an extreme blur -- a joint implausibility -- and it is a marginal instrument, so it pays for that in joint coverage and the price compounds with the number of terms.

A typicality gate does the intended job without the price: draw from the full copula and reject only the draws whose Mahalanobis distance in copula normal scores exceeds the card's own 99th percentile -- the same statistic `device_fit.coverage` already validates each card against on held-out frames. Every marginal stays intact and 99 % of the joint distribution stays reachable.

| device | terms | frames fitted | joint mass at spread 0.2 | typical draws kept | joint mass, typicality gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `bv_pulsera` | 9 | 116 | 5.1% | 5.1% | 99.0% |
| `cios_fusion` | 9 | 38 | 3.3% | 3.3% | 99.0% |
| `elekta_xvi` | 10 | 335 | 2.4% | 2.4% | 99.0% |
| `pixium_4600` | 8 | 18 | 5.0% | 5.0% | 99.0% |
| `surgio_b51_dr` | 11 | 294 | 2.8% | 2.8% | 99.0% |
| `varian_halcyon` | 10 | 525 | 1.7% | 1.7% | 99.0% |

## The clip saturates; it does not truncate

`np.clip` pins an out-of-band draw to the boundary VALUE rather than rejecting it, so each clipped term
carries a point mass of weight 20% at each end of its range. Per frame, at the spread in code:

| device | frames with at least one pinned term | pinned terms per frame (of k) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `bv_pulsera` | 94.9% | 3.6 of 9 |
| `cios_fusion` | 96.7% | 3.6 of 9 |
| `elekta_xvi` | 97.6% | 4.0 of 10 |
| `pixium_4600` | 95.0% | 3.2 of 8 |
| `surgio_b51_dr` | 97.2% | 4.4 of 11 |
| `varian_halcyon` | 98.3% | 4.0 of 10 |

This is visible in `docs/figures/fig_device_params.png` as spikes in the blue histograms: the kilovoltage
of a clipped draw is not a narrowed distribution but a handful of stacked values.

Neither number settles what a detector should be trained on: a sampler that reaches more of the
distribution is not automatically better training data. That is measured on real frames in
`slurm/device_spread_arm.sh`, which trains the same detector on a corpus styled under each rule.
