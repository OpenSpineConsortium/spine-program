# Measured effect of synthetically oversampling a rare class (especially a rare anatomical variant) on rare-class recall versus common-class accuracy

Scope note. Every number below is quoted from the primary paper (PDF text extracted locally or the publisher/PMC page), with the oversampling factor where the paper states one. "Synthetic test set" and "non-medical" flags are given inline. Where a claim came only from a search-engine summary and could not be checked against the paper it is marked (unverified) or moved to Gaps.

## KQ1. Lumbosacral / thoracolumbar transitional vertebrae (L6, sacralised L5, T13, Castellvi): trained with augmented or oversampled variant cases and per-class results reported? What did VerSe do about L6/T13?

### Takeaway
No published spine paper synthesises (renders, GANs, diffuses) L6/T13/Castellvi cases and reports per-class recall before and after. The two quantified levers in the literature are (a) collecting more real variant cases at far above clinical prevalence (VerSe'20: L6 in 85 of 253 scans with a visible lumbosacral junction, ~34%), which moved L6 identification from zero methods in VerSe'19 to "almost all" in VerSe'20, and (b) VERIDAH's 4x epoch-level oversampling of real anomaly cases plus re-encoding T13/L6 as repeated T12/L5 labels, which reached 96.3% (T13-type) and 97.2% (L6-type) anomaly recall on CT with 99.18% perfectly labelled scans overall. Neither reports an ablation of the oversampling factor, and neither reports a cost to normal-anatomy accuracy beyond "marginally higher on normal cases".

### Cited Findings
- VerSe benchmark (Sekuboyina et al., Med Image Anal 2021, DOI 10.1016/j.media.2021.102166, PMID 34340104): "At a vertebra level, we observe a sudden performance drop in the case of transitional vertebrae (T13 and L6). Concerning L6, None of the methods in VerSe'19 identified the presence of L6. However, in VerSe'20, almost all algorithms identify at least a fraction of the L6 vertebrae. On the other hand, for T13, except for Xiangshang Z., the identification rate widely varies between the Public and Hidden phases for all teams." — [arXiv 2001.09193 (PDF, Sec. 4.4)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09193); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34340104/)
- VerSe attributes the change to data enrichment, not to method: "Supervised algorithms fail to generalise to out-of-distribution cases (L6 in VerSe'19) when their percentage of occurrence in the dataset is consistent with their low clinical prevalence. 2) With the availability of large, public data with an over-representation of out-of-distribution cases (as in VerSe'20), makes better algorithm design and learning feasible." — [arXiv 2001.09193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09193)
- Cross-year test: "Payer C. and Zhang A. succeed in identifying L6, while none of the methods in 2019 do, owing to the over-representation of L6 in VerSe'20." VerSe'19 methods run on VerSe'20 data: Payer C. 85.21 id-rate, Lessmann N. 66.96; "Lessmann N. depends on identifying the last vertebra. In cases with L6, this affects the entire scan ... In case of Payer C., the presence of L6 was not as detrimental." — [arXiv 2001.09193 (Sec. 4.6)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09193)
- Rare vs normal scans (VerSe Fig. 9, Dice): "we observe a superior performance on normal anatomy when compared to that on rare anatomy ... In Public, Yeah T., Zhang A., and Zeng C. have a small drop in performance, with the first two approaches showing a better performance on the rare cases compared to the two top performers, Payer C. and Chen D. In Hidden, Payer C. does not show any drop in performance, and outperforms the rest on the rare cases." The worst-case VerSe'20 scan is "due to the presence of a transitional L6 vertebra. Interestingly, the semi-automated approach of Jakubicek R. succeeds in identifying this anomaly correctly." — [arXiv 2001.09193 (Sec. 4.5)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09193)
- VerSe sizes: VerSe'19 160 scans (80/40/40 split), VerSe'20 319 scans (113/103/103); labels 25 = L6, 28 = T13. — [arXiv 2001.09193 (Table 2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09193)
- VerSe'20 variant counts (Liebl et al., Sci Data 2021, DOI 10.1038/s41597-021-01060-0): L6 present in 85 scans (28 training / 38 public validation / 29 private test) against 165 scans ending at L5 and 3 at L4; T13 in 6 scans (2/2/2) against 131 ending at T12 and 8 at T11 (of 145 complete thoracic spines); cervical ribs in 18 scans (6/7/5); stump ribs in 90 scans; Castellvi II–IV in 109 scans. Abstract: "enumeration abnormalities (n = 77) and transitional vertebrae (n = 161)"; "The first challenge (VerSe 2019) showed that segmentation algorithms perform well on normal anatomy, but fail in variants not frequently present in the training dataset." The descriptor does not report per-variant algorithm performance. — [PMC8553749](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8553749/)
- VERIDAH (anomaly-aware SPINEPS extension; Möller et al. 2026, arXiv 2601.14066). Training cohorts: T2w MRI 5,291 subjects with 286 thoracic-enumeration-anomaly (TEA, 5.4%) and 466 lumbar-enumeration-anomaly (LEA, 8.8%) cases; CT 1,171 subjects with 60 TEA (5.1%) and 83 LEA (7.1%). Oversampling: "each anomalous case in the training set [was sampled] four times during each epoch." Label re-encoding: T13 and L6 are not separate classes; "the existence of a T13 and L6 [is] indicated by two consecutive T12 or L5 labels", chosen because training on T13 directly "would create a huge class imbalance problem"; four classification heads (label, region, transition type, visibility). Test sets (real, deliberately enriched): T2w 82 TEA (8.2%) and 146 LEA (14.6%); CT 25 TEA (6.8%) and 29 LEA (7.9%). Results: TEA recall 87.80% (MRI) / 96.30% (CT); LEA recall 94.48% / 97.22%; scans with all labels correct 98.30% / 99.18%; perfect labelling on normal cases "marginally higher" than on anomalies. The CT cohort (1,536 scans) is in-house and unreleased. — [arXiv 2601.14066](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14066v1)
- A search-engine summary of VERIDAH states labelling accuracy on CT of 99% versus 77% "with prior labeling methods that did not account for enumeration anomalies" (unverified against the paper text). — [arXiv 2601.14066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14066)
- Transformer-enhanced vertebra segmentation with variant recognition (Sci Rep 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-16689-9): VerSe'19+'20, 374 scans / 355 subjects; no oversampling or synthesis, only affine (rotation +/-15 deg, scale 0.9–1.1) and Gaussian-noise augmentation plus an "anatomical variation attention module" and auxiliary BCE loss. Classification accuracy: normal 87%, L6 85%, T13 80%; AUC 0.96 (L6), 0.93 (T13), 0.95 (normal); mean Dice 0.953 over 26 labels; claims nnU-Net and SwinUNet "showed marked degradation" on L6/T13 while its model "remained stable". Classification evaluated on "300 samples with known variation annotations, evenly sampled across the three classes". — [PMC12491412](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12491412/)
- LSTV on lateral lumbar radiographs (Kwak, Ro, Kang, J Clin Med 2025, DOI 10.3390/jcm14217671, PMID 41227066): 3,116 radiographs, 1,941 LSTV-positive (62.3%) vs 1,175 normal — the variant is the majority class; no oversampling, only rotation/flip/CLAHE, label smoothing 0.1, dropout; binary only, no Castellvi subtypes. ResNet-50 sensitivity 85.1%, specificity 61.9%, AUC 0.84 (166/195 LSTV, 73/118 normal correct); DINOv2 90.8%/50.0%/0.80; CLIP 79.5%/56.8%/0.73; orthopaedic specialist 81.3%/87.3%. No ablation of imbalance handling. — [PMC12610351](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12610351/)
- Human-reader Castellvi grading (Hanhivaara et al., Acta Radiol 2024, PMID 39444351): CT had the highest diagnostic performance; radiography and MRI "fairly poor sensitivity and accuracy" for Castellvi typing (search summary; not a deep-learning paper). — [PubMed 39444351](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39444351/)

### Inferences
- The only spine-specific "before/after" for a rare variant is dataset-level: L6 went from ~clinical prevalence (VerSe'19) to roughly one scan in three (VerSe'20) using additional real cases, and detection went from 0 methods to nearly all. That is an enrichment factor of roughly 5–10x in prevalence achieved with distinct real cases, not duplication or synthesis; the effect is confounded with one year of method development, and VerSe never reports an L6 or T13 identification rate as a number in the text (it is a bar in Fig. 7).
- VERIDAH's recipe (4x epoch oversampling of ~60–80 distinct real anomaly cases per type on CT, plus removing the rare class from the label space) is the closest published analogue to the oversampling question. Its 96–97% anomaly recall with 99.18% overall perfect labelling implies that, at that oversampling factor, there is no measurable cost to normal spines; but with no ablation the factor 4 is a design choice, not a measured optimum.
- The Sci Rep 2025 transformer paper's balanced 300-sample evaluation (100 per class) cannot be scan-level for T13, because only 6 VerSe scans contain a T13; treat its 80% T13 accuracy as a vertebra- or crop-level number on a heavily re-used handful of cases.
- Kwak et al. show the trade-off in reverse: with LSTV as the majority class and no rebalancing, sensitivity for the variant is high (85–91%) and specificity for normal anatomy is low (50–62%).

### Gaps
- No paper found that renders, simulates or generates L6, T13, sacralised L5 or Castellvi-type cases as training data and reports per-class results; the search terms tried included "synthetic", "simulated anomaly", "augmentation", "oversampling", combined with L6/T13/Castellvi/transitional.
- No deep-learning paper found that reports per-Castellvi-type sensitivity at all; Kwak et al. is binary.
- The VerSe per-vertebra identification rates for L6 and T13 (Fig. 7) and the Dice gap in Fig. 9 are figures; their numeric values were not extracted.
- Meng et al. 2023 (graph optimisation with detection of T13/L6/absent T12; CMIG, S0895611123000538) returned HTTP 403 and was not read.
- A search-engine summary gave "47 instances of L6 and 6 instances of T13" for VerSe'20; Liebl's descriptor says 85 scans with L6. The 47 could not be traced to a source and the descriptor is taken as primary.
- VERIDAH's 4x oversampling has no ablation; the effect of 1x vs 4x vs more is unmeasured.

## KQ2. Adjacent rare-variant work (rib anomalies, accessory bones, rare fractures, rare lesions) augmented with GAN / diffusion / rendering: what ratio, what rare-recall gain, what common-class cost?

### Takeaway
Nothing was found for rib anomalies or accessory bones. For rare lesions and fracture types the measured pattern is: GAN/diffusion samples added on top of saturated classical augmentation raise the harder or rarer classes' sensitivity by 6–13 points (Frid-Adar: metastases 68.7 to 81.2%, haemangioma 72.3 to 78.5%) with specificity also rising, i.e. no common-class cost in those small, near-balanced sets; label-free synthetic tumour rendering matches real-tumour training on Dice (59.8% vs 57.5%) while specifically targeting the rare small-tumour regime where real-trained sensitivity is 52.0%. The published spine-fracture GAN paper adds "20%" synthetic to the small AO classes and reports only aggregate accuracy (89.98 to 93.68%).

### Cited Findings
- Frid-Adar et al. (ISBI 2018, arXiv 1801.02385; extended in Neurocomputing 321:321–331, 2018, DOI 10.1016/j.neucom.2018.09.013, arXiv 1803.01229). 182 liver-lesion CT ROIs (53 cysts, 64 metastases, 65 haemangiomas), 3-fold CV. Classical augmentation: 480 augmented images per ROI (30 rotations x 3 flips x 7 translations x 5 scales), "overall ~30,000 examples per folder"; accuracy rose from 57% with no augmentation ("~63 samples per fold") to saturation at 78.6% at 5,000 samples per fold, "where adding more augmented data examples failed to improve the classification results". GAN (DCGAN per class) samples then added on top of that optimum in balanced groups of {100x3, 500x3, 1000x3, 2000x3, 3000x3, 4000x3} per fold; best at 5,000 + 3,000 = 8,000 samples per fold: 85.7%. Per class, classic-only vs classic+GAN: cyst sensitivity 98.1% to 100%, specificity 98.4% to 97.7%; metastases sensitivity 68.7% to 81.2%, specificity 83.9% to 89.0%; haemangioma sensitivity 72.3% to 78.5%, specificity 84.6% to 91.4%; weighted sensitivity 78.6% to 85.7%, specificity 88.4% to 92.4%. Test folds are real. — [arXiv 1801.02385](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.02385); [arXiv 1803.01229](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01229)
- Spine fracture AO-type classification with GAN-generated CT (Sci Rep 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-98518-7): 2,820 CT images C3–L5, classes A0–A4, B1–B3, C; "added 20% synthetic fracture images to the class with less CT images like A2, B1, B2, C" using extended DCGAN and PGGAN; VGG16-ResNet50-ViT ensemble accuracy 89.98% (geometric augmentation only) to 90.28% (DCGAN) and 93.68% (PGGAN). Per-class counts and per-class before/after are not given, and the paper does not state whether the 80:20 test split excludes synthetic images (flag: possibly contaminated). — [PMC12022092](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12022092/)
- EndoRare, one-shot diffusion synthesis of four rare GI lesions (calcifying fibrous tumour CFT, juvenile polyposis JPS, familial adenomatous polyposis FAP, Peutz–Jeghers PJS; arXiv 2512.24278, Dec 2025). Classifier with EndoRare augmentation: macro PR-AUC 0.708 vs 0.612 for the strongest generative baseline (Textual Inversion), p<0.001; macro ROC-AUC 0.978; TPR at 20% FPR 0.988, "an absolute gain of 8.8% over traditional augmentation (p < 0.001)"; CFT PR-AUC 0.498 vs 0.398; macro partial AUC (0–20% FPR) 0.175, +5.8% over DATUM; JPS TPR at 20% FPR 0.95 vs 0.80 for zero-shot. Novice reader study: recall +0.400, precision +0.267 after exposure to synthetic cases. Synthetic:real ratio not stated in the extracted text; the paper mentions a "Mixed test set" (flag: test composition unverified). — [arXiv 2512.24278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24278)
- Label-free liver tumour segmentation (Hu et al., CVPR 2023, arXiv 2303.14869): 101 real tumour CTs vs synthetic tumours rendered on-the-fly into 116 healthy liver CTs; real-trained U-Net DSC 57.51%, synthetic-trained 59.77% on real tumours. Real-trained sensitivity by size: 52.0% for tumours of radius <5 mm vs 91.6% for larger; the synthetic-trained model "could detect tumors as small as 2mm" and showed "much higher Specificity" on external healthy scans. — [ar5iv 2303.14869](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.14869)
- Ktena et al. (Nat Med 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-02838-6, PMID 38600282, arXiv 2304.09218): dermatology high-risk conditions (BCC, melanoma, SCC/SCCIS) — high-risk sensitivity improved 27.3% in-distribution and 63.5% out-of-distribution with a 75% synthetic / 25% real training mix; chest X-ray (atelectasis, consolidation, cardiomegaly, effusion, oedema) — a purely synthetic training set "led to the best accuracy and fairness trade-off", cardiomegaly AUC +21.1%, average OOD AUC +5.2% relative; histopathology (100 labelled samples) +48.5% relative with a 50:50 mix. The paper says gaps were closed "without penalizing" the overrepresented group; no majority-group degradation is reported. (Whether the derm percentages are relative or absolute was not verified.) — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-02838-6)
- Cervical rib (prevalence <1%) is listed as an ACR AI use case for X-ray, but no published detector with augmentation results was found. — [ACR use case](https://www.acr.org/Data-Science-and-Informatics/AI-in-Your-Practice/AI-Use-Cases/Use-Cases/Cervical-Rib-Detection-X-Ray)
- Rare-disease augmentation scoping review (118 studies 2018–2025; PMID 41283261): imaging led the field; classical geometric/photometric augmentation most frequent; deep generative models expanding since 2021; no pooled effect sizes for rare-class recall. — [PMC12641889](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12641889/)

### Inferences
- In Frid-Adar the generative gain arrived only after classical augmentation of the same 182 lesions had saturated, and it fell on the two confusable classes (metastasis/haemangioma) rather than the easy one (cyst) — the mechanism is added variability, not more copies. Specificity rose alongside sensitivity, so no common-class cost was measurable at 3,000 synthetic per class per fold; at 4,000 the curve did not improve further (Fig. 8, values not extracted).
- The label-free tumour result is the closest published analogue to "render the rare sub-class": the rare regime (small tumours) is synthesised without limit, and the payoff is on that regime plus specificity, not on aggregate Dice.
- The spine-fracture GAN paper is the only bone-specific result and is too coarse to answer the question: the 20% addition is not a per-class ratio and no per-class recall is reported.

### Gaps
- No published detector/classifier for rib anomalies (cervical rib, stump rib, supernumerary rib), accessory ossicles, or other skeletal variants with synthetic or oversampled training was found on PubMed/arXiv-indexed search; only case reports and RadioGraphics reviews exist.
- EndoRare's synthetic:real ratio and test-set composition were not extractable from the PDF text.
- Hu et al.'s synthetic-trained small-tumour sensitivity (the counterpart to the 52.0% real-trained figure) was not extracted numerically.
- Ktena et al.'s per-condition tables and whether their "improvement" figures are relative were not verified.

## KQ3. Class-imbalance methods with measured trade-offs (class-balanced loss, focal loss, repeat-factor sampling, oversampling 5x–50x): any curve of rare recall vs oversampling factor, ideally medical?

### Takeaway
The rare-up / frequent-down trade-off is quantified in LVIS (RFS at t=0.001: APr 3.2 to 13.4, APf −0.6; class-aware sampling: APr to 8.5 but APc and APf both fall) and in ISIC 2019 (aggressive inverse-frequency oversampling: DF/VASC sensitivity 0.92/1.00 but nevus sensitivity 0.35; replacing it with CB-focal + sqrt sampling: nevus 0.929, DF 0.880, VASC 0.875). Two medical dose-response curves exist for synthetic oversampling: Sagers (dermatology, 0/10/25/50/75 synthetic per real; gains up to +13.3% absolute at 10:1, saturating above 10:1) and Dushenev (ChestX-ray14 rare pathology, 0.5x–20x; recall 0.130 to 0.744 at 4x, then falling to 0.540 at 20x, while PR-AUC drops from 0.846 to 0.677). Khosravi (CheXpert, 1x–10x synthetic) shows a monotone but small aggregate gain (0.782 to 0.804 AUROC) with the largest gains in labels under 5% prevalence.

### Cited Findings
- LVIS repeat-factor sampling (Gupta, Dollar, Girshick 2019; non-medical): r_c = max(1, sqrt(t / f_c)), image-level r_i = max over categories. "Comparing with the baseline (equivalent to t = 0), there is a large improvement in APr from 3.2% to 13.4% at t = 0.001. This threshold oversamples categories appearing in less than 0.1% of images (829 of the 1230 categories). There is a slight penalty in lower APf (−0.6%), but overall AP improves (+2.2%)." Table 3b/3c at t=0.001: AP 23.2, APr 13.4 +/- 0.80, APc 23.2, APf 27.1; the extracted row labelled 0.1000 (t=0.1) reads AP 21.3, APr 9.6, APc 21.7, APf 25.5 (stronger oversampling lowered every group; row label inferred from PDF text). Class-aware sampling: "CAS improves APr over the baseline as expected (from 3.2% to 8.5%), however both APc and APf decrease leading to a worse overall result" (AP 18.7, APc 19.0, APf 22.3). Bigger backbones raise AP but "APr does not improve with the largest backbone" (15.8 to 15.6). — [arXiv 1908.03195 (Appendix B.2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03195)
- Chang et al. 2021 (ICML; LVIS v0.5, Mask R-CNN R-50, cosine classifier; non-medical). Box AP / APr / APc / APf: baseline 22.0 / 5.3 / 20.9 / 30.1; RFS 24.3 / 13.1 / 23.9 / 29.3 (frequent −0.8); class-aware sampling 18.4 overall; class-balanced loss 21.0; EQL 23.3; RIO (image + object resampling) 25.7 / 17.2 / 25.1 / 29.8. Mask AP: baseline 22.9 / 6.7 / 23.1 / 29.0; class-aware sampling 18.5 / 7.3 / 19.3 / 21.9 (frequent −7.1); class-balanced loss 20.9 / 8.2 / 21.2 / 25.7; EQL 22.8 / 11.3 / 24.7 / 25.1; RFS 25.1 / 15.1 / 25.4 / 28.6; RIO 26.0 / 18.9 / 26.2 / 28.5. — [arXiv 2104.05702 (Table 1)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05702)
- Class imbalance in object detection, COCO-ZIPF with YOLOv5s (technical report, non-medical): "the mAP trajectories of both the CAS and RFS strategies closely mirror that of the baseline, with a marginal decrease observed towards the final epochs, suggesting that CAS and RFS do not outperform the baseline"; class-aware sampling "improved detection in some minority classes, it inadvertently reduced the model's accuracy for the majority class ... overall decrease in mAP"; weighted BCE caused "significant performance degradation" until the classification-loss weight was cut from 0.5 to 0.05, and still fell short of baseline; mosaic and mixup augmentation improved performance "uniformly across" classes. — [arXiv 2403.07113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07113)
- Buda, Maki, Mazurowski (Neural Networks 2018, DOI 10.1016/j.neunet.2018.07.011, PMID 30092410; MNIST/CIFAR-10/ImageNet, non-medical): imbalance ratios up to 5,000 (MNIST) and 50 (CIFAR-10); "oversampling should be applied to the level that completely eliminates the imbalance"; on MNIST at rho=1,000 partial oversampling was tested and "the default version of oversampling was always the best"; "as opposed to some classical machine learning models, oversampling does not cause overfitting of CNNs" (train–test gap stable, Fig. 8). - Sagers et al. 2023 (arXiv 2308.12453; Fitzpatrick17k 9-condition subset F17k-9, 3,699 images; Stanford DDI; Stable Diffusion inpaint / in-then-outpaint / DreamBooth text-to-image; 458,920 synthetic images; real held-out tests). Dose-response: with 16, 32 or 64 real images per class and 0/10/25/50/75 synthetic per real, "With 16 or 32 real images per class, adding 10 synthetic images per real image led to significant increases in accuracy, up to an absolute increase of 13.3% (BH adjusted p = 5.3e-4). - Sensors 2026 review of class-imbalanced learning in medical imaging claims "data-centric strategies can improve minority class recall by 12–35% in datasets with imbalance ratios ... >= 10:1", but the range is not tied to a citation in the text; the cited primaries report AUC or accuracy, not before/after minority recall. — [PMC13029843](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13029843/)
- Eur Radiol 2024 evaluation-practices paper (DOI 10.1007/s00330-024-10834-0; CheXpert and ChestX-ray14) recommends AUC-PR, adjusted AUC-PR and balanced Brier score for imbalanced classifiers; full text paywalled, no numbers retrieved. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00330-024-10834-0)
- Long-tailed medical image classification (arXiv 2607.23883) combines class-aware sampling (alpha=0.5), asymmetric loss and mixing augmentations into one model and reports only aggregate AUROC/F1/AP, no head-vs-tail split. — [arXiv 2607.23883](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23883)

### Inferences
- RFS at its default t=0.001 is a mild oversampler: for a category in 0.01% of images the repeat factor is sqrt(0.001/0.0001) = 3.2, and 10x is reached only at f_c = 1e-5. That mildness is why the frequent-class cost is 0.6–0.8 AP. Class-aware sampling, which is effectively full rebalancing, buys less rare AP and costs 5–7 AP on frequent classes (Chang, mask APf 29.0 to 21.9). The LVIS t=0.1 row shows that pushing the factor up loses on every group.
- Buda's "oversample to full balance, no overfitting" holds for balanced-accuracy-type metrics on MNIST/CIFAR; on ImageNet the same recipe did not beat the baseline on ROC AUC, and the ISIC 2019 paper shows the concrete price of full inverse-frequency oversampling in a medical set: majority sensitivity 0.35.
- The two medical synthetic dose-response curves agree on shape: gains up to roughly 4x–10x synthetic per real, then flat or falling. Dushenev's PR-AUC falling from 0.846 to 0.677 while recall rises from 0.13 to 0.74 says much of that "gain" is an operating-point shift toward the rare class rather than better ranking; Sagers' gains are absolute accuracy on a balanced test and are largest when there are 16–32 real images per class.

### Gaps
- No medical paper was found that reports rare-class recall as a function of a plain duplication factor (5x–50x) with common-class accuracy alongside; the curves above are for synthetic images (Sagers, Dushenev, Khosravi) or non-medical (LVIS).
- Khosravi's per-pathology tables (E3–E9), which carry the "<5% prevalence" result, are supplementary and were not retrieved.
- Sagers Fig. S-2 (1/3/7 synthetic per real vs duplication) is supplementary and not in the arXiv PDF text.
- Focal loss in medical imaging: only qualitative statements were found (focal loss raises F1 more than accuracy; cross-entropy yields higher accuracy but lower F1); no per-class before/after table with an imbalance ratio was located in this pass.

## KQ4. Evidence that duplicating too few distinct examples fails (memorisation) while generating distinct views of the same examples succeeds: any measured comparison?

### Takeaway
The direct evidence is mixed and thin. Against the "duplication fails" thesis: Buda et al. found duplication-oversampling to full balance the best method for CNNs with no overfitting, and VERIDAH reached 96–97% anomaly recall with 4x duplication of a few dozen real cases. For it: in ISIC 2016 random oversampling gave malignant recall 0.63 / macro-F1 0.72 vs 0.74–0.75 / 0.90 for SMOTE/ADASYN; in Frid-Adar, 480 geometric views per lesion plateaued at 78.6% and only GAN samples moved it to 85.7%; in ISIC 2019, aggressive oversampling inflated rare sensitivity while collapsing majority sensitivity to 0.35. The one paper that designed the exact comparison (Sagers: duplication vs 1/3/7 synthetic per real for a downsampled class) puts the answer in a supplementary figure that was not retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- "oversampling does not cause overfitting of CNNs" and full-balance oversampling was "always the best" among partial levels on MNIST at rho=1,000; but on ImageNet oversampling scored below baseline (88.38 vs 90.74 ROC AUC at rho=100). — [Buda et al., ar5iv 1710.05381](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.05381)
- VERIDAH: 4x epoch-level duplication of 60 TEA and 83 LEA real CT training cases, with T13/L6 re-encoded as repeated T12/L5, gave 96.30% / 97.22% anomaly recall and 99.18% perfectly labelled scans on a real enriched CT test set (25 TEA, 29 LEA). — [arXiv 2601.14066](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14066v1)
- Frid-Adar: classical geometric augmentation (480 views per lesion, ~30,000 per fold available) saturated at 78.6% by 5,000 samples per fold, "where adding more augmented data examples failed to improve the classification results"; adding 3,000 GAN samples per class per fold on top raised accuracy to 85.7%, with the gain on metastases (+12.5 sensitivity) and haemangiomas (+6.2). — [arXiv 1801.02385](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.02385)
- ISIC 2016: random oversampling (duplication) macro-F1 0.72, malignant recall 0.63, benign recall 0.80; SMOTE 0.90 / 0.74 / 1.00; ADASYN 0.90 / 0.75 / 0.99. The authors state ROS "can result in overfitting" and SMOTE/ADASYN "may lead to overfitting due to redundant synthetic samples" (validation oscillations). — [arXiv 2512.15837](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15837)
- ISIC 2019: the baseline that used "aggressive inverse-frequency oversampling" reached DF sensitivity 0.920 and VASC 1.00 with NV sensitivity 0.350; removing it (CB focal + sqrt effective-number sampling) gave NV 0.929, DF 0.880, VASC 0.875, with AUC up for every class. — [PMC13337696](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13337696/)
- Sagers: for a class downsampled to 25/50/100 real images, "We added 1, 3, or 7 synthetic images per real image of the rare disease class to achieve parity ... As a control, we also tested an upsampling strategy of simple duplication for the rare disease images" (Fig. S-2, not in main text); overall, "the primary driver of performance was the number of real images" and synthetic gains "are substantially smaller than the gains obtained from adding real images". — [arXiv 2308.12453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12453)
- VerSe: over-representation with distinct real L6 cases (VerSe'20: 85 L6 scans) moved L6 from "None of the methods in VerSe'19 identified the presence of L6" to "almost all algorithms identify at least a fraction of the L6 vertebrae"; T13, with only 6 scans (2 in training), still "widely varies between the Public and Hidden phases for all teams". — [arXiv 2001.09193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09193); [PMC8553749](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8553749/)
- Dushenev: synthetic views generated from ~50 real positives improved recall from 0.130 to 0.744 at 4x on real test folds, while PR-AUC fell from 0.846 to 0.677; a synthetic-only classifier "achieves reasonable recall, [but] overall performance remains lower than mixed real+synthetic training"; "too many generated samples may distort the training distribution". — [arXiv 2605.11898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11898)
- Pediatric rare genetic syndromes from faces, synthetic-only vs real-only training (arXiv 2605.22767): Top-1 FaceNet 13.27% synthetic-only vs 9.91% real-only; CLIP 10.62% vs 3.10%; DenseNet 11.50% vs 15.93%; performance "peaks at intermediate scales" and "performance degradation at larger scales suggests redundancy or noise in current generation and selection". — [arXiv 2605.22767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22767)
- Khosravi: synthetic replicas at 200% matched real-only on the CheXpert test (0.783 both), and 300% matched on MIMIC and Emory, but every synthetic-only model trailed the matching real+synthetic model. — [PMC11177083](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11177083/)
- Generative models trained on small medical sets can emit near-copies: unconditional latent diffusion models "memorize patient imaging data" (Dar et al., arXiv 2402.01054); a search summary reports that training-time augmentation reduced the memorised fraction (PCCTA and MRNet cohorts) — numbers not verified against the paper. — [arXiv 2402.01054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01054)

### Inferences
- The evidence separates by what "fails" means. Duplication does not appear to overfit in the classical sense (Buda; VERIDAH's 4x works), but it moves the decision boundary: full inverse-frequency duplication maximises rare recall and pays for it in majority sensitivity (ISIC 2019, NV 0.35), and stronger RFS lowers every group (LVIS t=0.1). Distinct views (geometric or generative) add variability that duplication cannot; Frid-Adar's plateau under 480 geometric views per lesion, broken only by GAN samples, is the cleanest medical measurement of that, though the GAN was itself trained on the same lesions.
- For a variant with a handful of distinct examples (T13: 6 scans in VerSe'20), neither year of VerSe produced stable identification, whereas L6 with 85 scans did — consistent with the number of distinct cases, not the sampling weight, being the binding constraint. The project's own unpublished observation (L6 Dice 0.000 despite 12x duplication with too few distinct cases, per the working notes) is in the same direction but is not a citable source.
- Synthetic "distinct views" from generators fitted to ~50 examples can be near-duplicates (Dar et al.), so the duplication-vs-generation dichotomy is partly a continuum; Dushenev's falling PR-AUC at high ratios is what near-duplicate synthetic positives would be expected to produce.

### Gaps
- No medical paper was found that trains the same model on (a) k distinct rare cases duplicated n-fold and (b) the same k cases with n generated/rendered views, and reports rare recall and common accuracy for both; Sagers designed this (duplication control vs 1/3/7 synthetic) but the result is in Fig. S-2 of the supplement, which was not accessible in this pass.
- No measured threshold for "too few distinct examples" (a minimum k) was found in any of the sources.
- Dar et al.'s memorisation percentages were only seen in a search summary and were not checked against the paper.
