# Simulated-to-real mixing ratios for X-ray / fluoroscopy detectors and landmark models

Scope: published experiments in surgical X-ray, fluoroscopy or radiography (plus the closest medical sim-to-real work) that swept or ablated the synthetic:real training ratio, the number of real images added to a synthetic corpus, or fine-tuned a synthetic-pretrained model on N real images, with reported numbers. Everything below was checked against the paper's full text unless marked "(abstract only)". Numbers are quoted as printed; where a fetched summary looked internally inconsistent this is flagged rather than smoothed over.

Method note on how this was gathered: full texts were read for SyntheX (arXiv PDF), Unberath 2019 (PMC), Gherardini 2020 (PMC), i3PosNet (PMC), Kausch 2020 (PMC), Pelphix (arXiv PDF), Toth 2019 (MIDL PDF), Zhou 2021 (arXiv PDF), Jecklin 2024 (arXiv HTML), Rapuri 2026 (arXiv HTML), Mo 2025 (arXiv HTML), FleXray 2026 (arXiv HTML), Barr 2026 (PMC), Moroianu 2025 (PMC), RoentGen (ar5iv), Ktena 2024 (PMC), Salehinejad 2018 (arXiv PDF), Pan 2024 (arXiv HTML), Tang 2026 VDSB-GWSyn (arXiv HTML), Yi 2018 (arXiv HTML). Abstract-only: Bier 2019, FluoroSAM, XraySyn, DiffPose, Esfandiari 2018, Grupp 2020, Arrabi 2025, Jung 2026, Viviers 2024.

## Key question 1 — Which of the flagship sim-to-real X-ray papers (DeepDRR, SyntheX, Pelphix, XraySyn, ProST/DiffDRR-line, pelvis/vertebra landmark, catheter/needle/guidewire) actually report a synthetic:real ratio sweep or a real-image-count curve, and what were the numbers?

### Takeaway
None of the physics-simulation (DRR/DeepDRR) flagship papers — DeepDRR 2018/2019, Bier 2019, SyntheX 2023, Pelphix 2023, FluoroSAM, Kausch 2020, Toth 2019, i3PosNet, Zhou 2021, Rapuri 2026 — sweeps the synthetic:real ratio or the number of real images; they train on synthetic only and test on real (SyntheX varies only the *synthetic* count, 366 vs 10,000). The only ratio/count sweeps with tables are in the guidewire-synthesis (VDSB-GWSyn: 0/1k/2k/3k synthetic on 182 real), the cervical-spine radiograph paper (Barr 2026: 100–700 real with/without synthetic pretraining), and the chest-radiograph generative-augmentation line (RoentGen 2022, Moroianu 2025: 0–565k synthetic on 66k real and 3k–66k real fine-tuning).

### Cited Findings

#### Physics-simulated (DRR / DeepDRR / DiffDRR) papers — what each actually did

- **DeepDRR, Unberath et al. 2019 IJCARS (extended MICCAI 2018), DOI 10.1007/s11548-019-02011-2.** Pelvis landmark detection: 20 CTs (18/1/1), 1,000 DRRs per CT = 20,000 synthetic; tested on 78 real X-rays of two cadaveric lower-torso specimens; **no real images in training or fine-tuning**. Mean L2 landmark error on real data: naive-DRR-trained 78.2 ± 26.5 mm vs DeepDRR-trained 24.7 ± 21.3 mm (p < 0.01). Robotic end-effector: 10,000 synthetic images from 5 CTs, tested on 87 real X-rays; Dice 88.7 ± 7.7 % (naive) vs 91.5 ± 6.3 % (DeepDRR); keypoint L2 26 ± 19 mm vs 2.5 ± 1.0 mm. Only synthetic-realism ablations; no synthetic:real ratio — [Unberath 2019, PMC7297499](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7297499/); [MICCAI 2018 arXiv 1803.08606](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08606)
- **Bier et al. 2019 IJCARS, DOI 10.1007/s11548-019-01975-5 (abstract/summary only).** Training data "synthetically generated from full body CTs" (20 CTs, 23 landmarks); no real-image training reported in the abstract — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11548-019-01975-5); [JHU pure](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/learning-to-detect-anatomical-landmarks-of-the-pelvis-in-x-rays-f/)
- **SyntheX, Gao et al. 2023 Nature Machine Intelligence, DOI 10.1038/s42256-023-00629-1 (arXiv 2206.06127).** Hip task: 366 real fluoroscopic images from six cadaveric specimens with a *precisely matched* synthetic twin set (366 DRRs at the registered poses), plus a "scaled-up" set of **10,000 synthetic images from 20 hip CTs**. Real2Real (train on real, leave-one-specimen-out) vs Sim2Real (train on synthetic only). **No mixed synthetic+real training and no real-image-count sweep**; the only size variable is synthetic count (366 matched vs 10,000 scaled). Table 1 (366 real test images): Sim2Real (scaled, strong DR) landmark error 6.29 ± 6.29 mm, Dice 0.818 ± 0.221; Real2Real 8.15 ± 15.30 mm, Dice 0.759 ± 0.248; landmark difference p = 0.032. Table 5 at 360×360, strong domain randomization: Realistic (DeepDRR, 366 matched synthetic) 9.05 ± 19.37 mm; Realistic-Scaled (10,000 synthetic) 6.29 ± 6.29 mm; Real2Real 8.15 ± 15.30 mm; Realistic-CycleGAN-Scaled 6.59 ± 7.25 mm. Table 4 Dice, same column: Realistic 0.760, Realistic-Scaled 0.818, Real 0.759. Surgical tool (264 real images): Sim2Real 2.13 ± 2.27 mm / Dice 0.860 vs Real2Real 1.90 ± 5.49 mm / Dice 0.406. COVID lesion (2,951 real CXR): Sim2Real accuracy 85.03 % vs Real2Real 93.95 %; F1 53.26 vs 73.48 — [SyntheX arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127); [Nature MI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-023-00629-1)
- **Pelphix, Killeen et al. MICCAI 2023, arXiv 2304.09285.** Training set 726 simulated sequences totalling 229,488 images, 11 validation sequences (3,916 images); cadaver test 257 images from five wire/screw insertions. Accuracy 93.8 % on simulated vs 67.57 % in cadaver across granularity levels; target corridor up to 88 %. Trained "despite having no access to real image sequences"; fine-tuning on real sequences is named as future work. No sweep — [Pelphix arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09285)
- **FluoroSAM, Killeen et al. (arXiv 2403.08059; MICCAI 2025 chapter) (abstract only).** Trained "from scratch on 3M synthetic X-ray images"; the abstract reports qualitative/quantitative transfer to real images but gives no synthetic:real ratio experiment — [FluoroSAM arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08059)
- **Kausch et al. 2020 IJCARS, DOI 10.1007/s11548-020-02204-0.** C-arm standard-projection pose regression trained exclusively on DRRs (proximal femur 81/109 cases AP/lateral; lumbar spine 47 patients); 3 usable cadaver studies; real X-rays evaluated only qualitatively; no real training data — [Kausch 2020, PMC8286958](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8286958/)
- **Toth et al. MIDL 2019, "Training deep networks on domain randomized synthetic X-ray data for cardiac interventions".** Trained "purely with CT volumes, without any real X-rays from the target domain"; domain randomization only. Table 2 (clinical X-ray robustness, mean translational error ef): standard network 5.77 / 14.07 / 7.30 / 10.78 / 5.05 mm across five weight seeds vs domain-randomized network 1.38 / 1.01 / 1.15 / 1.34 / 1.71 mm. No real fine-tuning, no ratio — [Toth 2019 MIDL PDF](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v102/toth19a/toth19a.pdf)
- **i3PosNet, Kügler et al. 2020 IJCARS, DOI 10.1007/s11548-020-02157-4.** 10,000 synthetic training images (200k patches); real test: 25 of 540 phantom C-arm images. Position error 0.024 ± 0.016 mm (synthetic) vs 0.746 ± 0.818 mm (real); forward-angle error −0.024 ± 1.215° vs 6.59 ± 10.36°. "No fine-tuning or mixed training experiments"; authors state large annotated datasets "mixed into the training" are future work — [i3PosNet, PMC7316684](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7316684/)
- **Zhou et al. 2021 Comput Biol Med 138:104923 (arXiv 2108.06466).** Skull landmark ResNet trained on 9,751 DRR–landmark pairs (9,251 train / 500 test) from one subject's CT; CycleGAN trained with 6,716 DRRs and real dual-fluoroscopy frames; 48 pairs of real dual-fluoro images evaluated. No real landmark labels in training, no real-count sweep. Registration error 3.9 ± 2.1° / 4.6 ± 2.2 mm during walking — [Zhou 2021 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06466)
- **Rapuri, Ko, Killeen, Taylor, Unberath 2026, "2D Versus 3D Diffusion for In Silico Training of Interventional X-ray AI Models", arXiv 2606.21414.** DeepFluoro real set (366 images, 6 specimens). Six-fold, precisely matched views, landmark error at 0.60 NCC threshold: RealXray 4.89 ± 6.42 mm; RealCT-DRR 7.83 ± 25.67; DiffCT-DRR 5.82 ± 13.72; DiffXray 4.40 ± 1.87. "Cloud" augmentation (500 synthetic views per patient, ±2.5° perturbations): RealCT-DRR-Cloud 4.87 ± 1.75 mm; DiffXray-Cloud 4.47 ± 1.49 mm. **No synthetic:real ratio experiments and no real-count reduction** — [Rapuri 2026 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21414v1)
- **Mo, Suh, Martin, Moyer 2025, arXiv 2511.21575 (pelvic fluoroscopy landmarks).** Trained *only* on 8,000 DiffDRR images from 90 CTPEL CTs (80 train); tested on 1,600 novel-view and 1,000 novel-subject synthetic images; **no real fluoroscopy at all**. The "fine-tuned" model (RMSE 8.45 vs 8.58 px internal; 5.09 vs 5.58 px external) refers to one epoch of a pose loss, not real data — beware this when the paper turns up in searches for "fine-tuning on real" — [Mo 2025 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21575v1)
- **Arrabi et al. 2025 (arXiv 2510.16160) (abstract only).** Conformal landmark localization for C-arm positioning validated "on a synthetic X-ray dataset generated from DeepDRR"; no real data reported — [Arrabi 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16160). **Jung et al. 2026 (arXiv 2604.18740) (abstract only)** fine-tunes MLLMs on "both synthetic and real X-ray datasets" but the abstract gives neither counts nor a ratio — [Jung 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18740)
- **XraySyn, Peng et al. AAAI 2021 (arXiv 2012.02407) (abstract only):** view synthesis from a single radiograph with CT priors; no downstream detector trained with a synthetic:real sweep — [XraySyn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02407). **DiffPose, Gopalakrishnan, Dey, Golland CVPR 2024 (arXiv 2312.06358) (abstract only):** patient-specific self-supervised registration from differentiable rendering; no real-count or ratio experiment reported in the abstract — [DiffPose](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06358)
- **Grupp et al. 2020 IJCARS (arXiv 1911.07042) (abstract only)** — often grouped with the sim-to-real hip work but trained on **real** cadaver fluoroscopy (366 images, 6 specimens, labels from 2D/3D registration); Dice 0.86/0.87 hemipelves, 0.90/0.84 femurs; 2D landmark error 5.0 mm. Not a synthetic:real study — [Grupp 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07042)

#### Sim-to-real spine reconstruction with real fine-tuning (one point, no sweep)

- **Jecklin et al. 2024 Medical Image Analysis (arXiv 2401.16027; PMID 39197301)**, lumbar-spine 3D reconstruction from sparse fluoroscopy. Synthetic: 120,148 DRRs from 863 CTs (84,000 train / 24,136 val / 12,012 test). Real: 4,337 paired synthetic/real ex-vivo images from 13 specimens (3,317 train / 1,020 test), Siemens Cios Spin. Transfer learning = full fine-tune (~40 epochs, LR reset to 1e-4; freezing modules was worse). Table 2 (estimated origins): base model on DRR input (upper bound) F1 0.86, surface 0.69; transfer-learned on real X-rays F1 0.84, surface 0.63; style-transfer + base model F1 0.84, surface 0.62. Six unseen specimens on three C-arms: F1 0.84–0.88. "The paper did not report experiments varying the fraction of real versus synthetic training data, or mixing datasets during training." — [Jecklin 2024 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16027); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841524002470)

#### Catheter / guidewire papers

- **Gherardini, Mazomenos, Menciassi, Stoyanov 2020 CMPB, DOI 10.1016/j.cmpb.2020.105420 (PMID 32171151).** Three datasets: 9,000 synthetic (catheter composited on real fluoro backgrounds), 2,000 phantom fluoroscopy, 1,207 in-vivo frames from six procedures T1–T6. Two-stage: end-to-end on synthetic (Experiment-1) or phantom (Experiment-2), then fine-tune the deepest 7 U-Net layers on a *small* in-vivo set. Table 3: fine-tune/test frames per procedure T1 55/231, T2 30/120, T3 40/160, T4 40/160, T5 30/113, T6 45/183; split **S1 = 240 fine-tune frames** drawn across all six procedures; S2 = T1+T4+T6 (714 frames, tested on the other three); S3 = T1+T2+T5 (579 frames). Table 5 (S1, Experiment-1): Dice 0.58–0.78 on T1–T4, 0.13–0.29 on T5–T6, average 0.55; Experiment-2 comparable (0.57–0.77, then 0.18–0.29). Table 4 (S2, S3): Dice 0.02–0.36 per procedure (averages ≈ 0.15–0.26). **Leave-one-out training on in-vivo frames only, no synthetic: average Dice ≈ 0.1 (range 1e-4 to 0.29)**. Note the "more real frames → worse Dice" ordering (240 → 0.55; 579/714 → ≈ 0.17–0.26) is a procedure-shift effect (S2/S3 test on procedures never seen in fine-tuning; T5/T6 are low-contrast and T6 has black borders), not a monotonic count curve — [Gherardini 2020, PMC7903142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7903142/)
- **Yi et al. 2019 J Digit Imaging, DOI 10.1007/s10278-019-00201-7 (arXiv 1806.00921).** 2,515 adult CXRs with B-spline-composited synthetic catheters for training; 35 fully labelled neonatal pediatric X-rays for testing; Fβ ≈ 0.8. "The paper does not include experiments comparing training with only synthetic data vs. only real data vs. mixtures" — [Yi 2018 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.00921v1)
- **Pan et al. 2024, "Label-Efficient Data Augmentation with Video Diffusion Models for Guidewire Segmentation in Cardiac Fluoroscopy", arXiv 2412.16050.** 400 fully annotated 16-frame videos (+409 partially annotated videos: 4,000 annotated / 10,000 unannotated frames); 1,200 synthesized videos added to 80 % of the F-video set. Despite the title, "the document does NOT contain label-efficiency curves ... sweeps over synthetic-to-real ratios ... [or] data ablations varying the quantity of real labeled data" — [Pan 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16050)
- **Tang et al. 2026, VDSB-GWSyn (guidewire synthesis in coronary angiography), arXiv 2606.00109.** Real: 283 annotated frames (182 train / 50 val / 51 test). Synthetic: 7,148 candidate masks → 3,506 after filtering → 3,000 used for pre-training. **Sweep of synthetic pre-training set size (Table 2), endpoint localization on the real test set:** 0 synthetic: MPE 16.01 px, PCK@3px 52.63 %; 1,000: 9.72 px, 77.45 %; 2,000: 7.71 px, 86.27 %; 3,000: 7.78 px, 85.29 %. Gains saturate at 2,000 synthetic (≈ 11× the 182 real training frames); 3,000 is marginally worse — [Tang 2026 arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00109)
- **Sim-to-real SAM guidewire, arXiv 2410.07460:** unsupervised adaptation with no target-domain labels; not a ratio study — [arXiv 2410.07460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07460)
- **Xi et al. 2025, catheter detection/segmentation via multi-task learning (arXiv 2503.02717; IJCARS DOI 10.1007/s11548-025-03461-7):** real data only (UCL 3,000 + 2,450 private); "no ablation studies vary synthetic-to-real ratios" — [Xi 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02717)

#### Radiography with generative (not physics) synthetic data — the papers that do sweep

- **Barr et al. 2026 BMC Med Inform Decis Mak, DOI 10.1186/s12911-026-03479-0 (PMID 41965677), cervical-spine lateral radiograph landmarks.** DDPM trained on 4,963 CSXA radiographs; landmark model pretrained on synthetic then fine-tuned on the external CLX-34 set (1,248 usable images; 874 development / 374 test). **Real-count sweep, test MSE, fine-tuned-from-synthetic vs de novo:** n=0 (zero-shot) 124.73 / —; n=100 31.78 / 84.33; n=200 12.97 / 55.84; n=300 10.10 / 34.30; n=400 9.74 / 27.21; n=500 7.49 / 14.17; n=600 8.56 / 7.42; n=700 4.37 / 7.42. Fine-tuning from the synthetic backbone won at 6 of 7 sizes; it lost at n=600. (The fetched summary lists 7.42 for de novo at both 600 and 700 — treat the 700-point de novo value as needing verification against the paper's table.) — [Barr 2026, PMC13185266](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13185266/)
- **RoentGen, Chambon et al. 2022, arXiv 2211.12737, Table 5 (6-finding CXR classification, AUROC).** Real 1.1k only: 0.73. Real 1.1k + 1.1k synthetic: 0.77 (+0.04). Synthetic 1.1k only: 0.69 (−0.04). Real 1.1k + 5.5k synthetic: 0.76 (+0.03, i.e. below the 1:1 mix). Real 30k: 0.82 (+0.09). Synthetic 30k only: 0.80 (+0.07). Real 30k + synthetic 30k: 0.84 (+0.11) — [RoentGen ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.12737); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12737)
- **Moroianu, Bluethgen, Chambon et al. 2025 (preprint; Research Square DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-7687810/v1; arXiv 2508.16783; PMC12676388), RoentGen-v2 on MIMIC-CXR (66k real).** (a) Real 66k + synthetic 0 / 100k / 200k / 300k / 400k / 500k / 565k: OOD AUROC CheXpert 0.700 → 0.730, NIH 0.661 → 0.696, PadChest 0.721 → 0.753, VinDr 0.641 → 0.641; in-distribution MIMIC 0.772 → 0.770; **plateau at 300k synthetic ≈ 5× the real count**. (b) Synthetic-only 66k–565k: at 565k, MIMIC in-distribution 0.756 vs 0.772 real (p < 0.05) but OOD better (NIH 0.702 vs 0.661). (c) Synthetic pretraining (565k) + real fine-tuning at 3k / 10k / 30k / 66k: "by fine-tuning on less than 10k real samples, we match or exceed" the 66k real-only baseline; at 66k real: MIMIC 0.798, CheXpert 0.732, NIH 0.726, PadChest 0.781, VinDr 0.683 — [Moroianu 2025, PMC12676388](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12676388/)
- **Salehinejad et al. ICASSP 2018 (arXiv 1712.01636) / IEEE TMI 2019 (DOI 10.1109/TMI.2018.2881415, PMID 30442603), DCGAN chest X-ray augmentation.** One fixed configuration, no sweep: DS1 real imbalanced, DS2 real balanced, DS3 real + DCGAN images balanced to 30,196 per class (2× the largest real class; DCGAN trained on 2,000 real per class; 1,000 real held out each for validation and test). Table 1 accuracy DS1 / DS2 / DS3: cardiomegaly 79.15 / 71.73 / 95.31; normal 77.75 / 72.53 / 95.02; pleural effusion 73.64 / 51.23 / 91.19; mean 92.10 % with DS3, ">20 % higher" than real-only — [Salehinejad ICASSP arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01636); [TMI PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30442603/)
- **Ktena et al. 2024 Nature Medicine, DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-02838-6.** CXR arm trained on 201,055 CheXpert images; best configuration was synthetic-only; +5.2 % in-distribution AUC and 44.6 % lower fairness gap. "The radiology section lacks a dedicated label-efficiency curve" (the histopathology and dermatology arms vary labelled-data amounts, radiology does not) — [Ktena 2024, PMC11031395](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11031395/)
- **FleXray, Butoi et al. 2026, arXiv 2609.26756.** Universal X-ray segmentation trained on online DRRs from 2,159 CT subjects + generatively edited DRRs + 764 real radiographs (hand/foot/forearm/humerus). Ablations on rendering mode and generative enhancement (equal-dataset mean Dice 0.870 with and without enhancement; ribs 5–10 +2.2 to +7.4 Dice with it); real X-rays "provided training coverage for peripheral radiographs"; overall 0.875 vs dataset-specific nnU-Nets 0.914. **No synthetic:real ratio sweep** — [FleXray arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26756)

### Inferences
- The fluoroscopy DRR/DeepDRR literature has standardised on a "synthetic-only train, real test" protocol; the question the caller is asking (how many real frames to add to a DeepDRR corpus, and at what ratio) has not been answered with a table by any of the flagship groups. SyntheX's Table 5 is the closest thing: it is a *synthetic*-count curve (366 → 10,000 synthetic moved landmark error 9.05 → 6.29 mm at 90 % activation, crossing below the 8.15 mm real-only baseline).
- The three papers that do sweep something (VDSB-GWSyn, Barr 2026, Moroianu 2025) all use *generative* synthetic data (Schrödinger-bridge / DDPM / latent diffusion), not physics simulation, so the ratios they report are not directly transferable to DRR corpora.
- Where a real-fine-tune point exists in fluoroscopy (Gherardini 240 frames; Jecklin 3,317 frames) it is a single point, and in Gherardini the apparent inverse relation between fine-tune count and Dice is confounded by which procedures were held out.

### Gaps
- No DeepDRR- or DiffDRR-based paper found gives a synthetic:real ratio table or a real-image-count curve for pelvis or vertebra landmark detection. Searches on "DRR fine-tuning real fluoroscopy number of real images", "sim-to-real X-ray landmark real images added" and "fine-tuned synthetic DRR pretrained real X-ray images pelvis" returned only synthetic-only papers.
- Esfandiari et al. 2018 IJCARS (pedicle-screw segmentation/pose; DOI 10.1007/s11548-018-1776-9) reports 93 % accuracy on synthetic vs 83 % on "clinically realistic" X-rays (abstract), but the full text (training mix, counts) was behind the Springer wall and the Semantic Scholar/Europe PMC abstracts are truncated — cannot say whether real images were mixed in.
- ProST (Gao et al. MICCAI 2020) and DiffDRR (Gopalakrishnan 2022) were not fetched; from their abstracts they are registration/rendering methods, not ratio studies, but this is unverified here.
- FluoroSAM full text was not read; if it contains a real-data fine-tuning experiment it is not in the abstract.
- The Viviers et al. 2024 TIP paper on 6-DoF instrument pose (arXiv 2405.11677; 92.41 % ADD-S 0.1) could not be read past the abstract (PDF > 10 MB); training-mix details unknown.

## Key question 2 — At how many real labelled images does real-image performance saturate when a large synthetic set is present? Any paper giving the curve?

### Takeaway
No fluoroscopy/DRR paper gives this curve. The only medical X-ray curves are Barr 2026 (cervical-spine landmarks: synthetic-pretrained and de novo converge at ≈ 600–700 real radiographs, with the synthetic advantage largest at 100–500) and Moroianu 2025 (CXR classification: < 10k real fine-tuning samples on a 565k-synthetic backbone match 66k real). The only "synthetic-side" saturation numbers are VDSB-GWSyn (2,000 synthetic on 182 real, ≈ 11:1) and Moroianu (300k synthetic on 66k real, ≈ 5:1).

### Cited Findings
- Barr 2026: fine-tuned-from-synthetic MSE falls 124.73 → 31.78 → 12.97 → 10.10 → 9.74 → 7.49 → 8.56 → 4.37 at n = 0/100/200/300/400/500/600/700 real; de novo 84.33 → 55.84 → 34.30 → 27.21 → 14.17 → 7.42 → 7.42 at n = 100…700; the gap closes between 500 and 700 — [Barr 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13185266/)
- Moroianu 2025: with 565k synthetic pretraining, fine-tuning on < 10k real MIMIC-CXR images matched or exceeded the 66k real-only baseline; mixing plateaued at 300k synthetic ("about 5× the number of real data samples") — [Moroianu 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12676388/)
- VDSB-GWSyn: with 182 real training frames, synthetic pre-training gain saturates at 2,000 synthetic (PCK@3px 86.27 %) and is flat/slightly down at 3,000 (85.29 %) — [Tang 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00109)
- Gherardini 2020: 240 real in-vivo frames sampled across six procedures were enough to lift a synthetic-pretrained light U-Net to Dice 0.58–0.78 on the four well-exposed procedures (average 0.55 over all six); in-vivo-only training on the same frames without synthetic pretraining gave ≈ 0.1 — [Gherardini 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7903142/)
- SyntheX: the real-only baseline (leave-one-specimen-out on 366 real images, i.e. ≈ 300 real training images per fold) was beaten by 10,000 synthetic images with no real data (6.29 vs 8.15 mm, p = 0.032), but the paper does not add real images to the synthetic set — [SyntheX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- RoentGen: at 1.1k real, adding 1.1k synthetic gave +0.04 AUROC and adding 5.5k gave +0.03; at 30k real, adding 30k synthetic gave +0.02 over 30k real alone (0.84 vs 0.82) — [RoentGen](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.12737)

### Inferences
- For a landmark task with a strong synthetic prior, Barr's curve puts the point at which real data alone catches up in the mid-hundreds of labelled radiographs; below ≈ 300 real images the synthetic backbone is worth a 3–4× reduction in error. That is the only medical X-ray number of this kind found; it is for radiographs with a DDPM backbone, not fluoroscopy with DRRs.
- For fluoroscopy specifically, the evidence is one point (Gherardini: 240 frames) and one implicit comparison (SyntheX: ≈ 300 real frames per fold lost to 10,000 synthetic). Nobody has shown what 10, 50 or 100 real frames buy on top of a DeepDRR corpus.

### Gaps
- No paper gives a real-image-count curve for DRR-trained fluoroscopy detectors; "tens vs low hundreds" cannot be answered from the literature found.
- Barr 2026's numbers came from a fetched summary; the exact table (and whether the de novo n = 700 value really equals the n = 600 value) should be confirmed in the PDF before quoting.

## Key question 3 — Fine-tuning a synthetic-pretrained detector on N real frames: measured gains vs N

### Takeaway
Measured fine-tuning gains exist at single N values (Gherardini N = 240: Dice ≈ 0.1 → 0.55; Jecklin N = 3,317: F1 0.84 on real, vs 0.86 for the same model on synthetic input) and as a curve only in Barr 2026 (radiographs) and Moroianu 2025 (CXR classification). i3PosNet and Pelphix explicitly defer real-data fine-tuning to future work.

### Cited Findings
- Gherardini 2020, N = 240 in-vivo frames (fine-tuning only the deepest 7 layers): synthetic-pretrained + fine-tuned Dice 0.55 average (0.58–0.78 on T1–T4) vs ≈ 0.1 for the same architecture trained on in-vivo frames alone; N = 579 and N = 714 (different procedures held out) gave 0.02–0.36 per procedure — [Gherardini 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7903142/)
- Jecklin 2024, N = 3,317 paired real ex-vivo images, full fine-tune for ~40 epochs: transfer-learned model on real X-rays F1 0.84 / surface 0.63 (estimated origins), 0.85 / 0.66 (ground-truth origins); base model on DRRs 0.86 / 0.69; freezing modules "showed inferior results compared to full fine-tuning" — [Jecklin 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16027)
- Barr 2026, N = 100…700 real radiographs: fine-tuned MSE 31.78 at N = 100 vs 84.33 de novo (2.7× lower); 7.49 vs 14.17 at N = 500; 4.37 vs 7.42 at N = 700 — [Barr 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13185266/)
- Moroianu 2025, N = 3k / 10k / 30k / 66k real CXR on a 565k-synthetic backbone: at 66k, MIMIC AUROC 0.772 → 0.798, NIH 0.661 → 0.726, PadChest 0.721 → 0.781; < 10k real already matched the 66k real-only model — [Moroianu 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12676388/)
- i3PosNet: no fine-tuning run; "i3PosNet closely misses the required tracking accuracy for temporal bone surgery on real X-ray data" (0.746 mm vs 0.024 mm synthetic) — [i3PosNet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7316684/)
- Pelphix: "truly performative SPR algorithms for X-ray may rely on Pelphix-style simulation for pretraining, before fine-tuning on real image sequences" — stated, not done — [Pelphix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09285)
- Zhou 2021: no real landmark labels used; the domain gap was handled by CycleGAN style translation of real X-rays to DRR style, after which registration with landmarks from "fake DRRs" was "at least two-fold superior" to landmarks predicted on raw real X-rays — [Zhou 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06466)

### Inferences
- The fluoroscopy papers that fine-tune do so with hundreds to thousands of frames and one N each; the "small-N" regime (≤ 100 real frames) that matters for a C-arm product has no published measurement in this modality.
- Gherardini's fine-tuning of only the deepest 7 layers versus Jecklin's finding that full fine-tuning beat frozen modules suggests the right fine-tuning depth is task-dependent; neither paper varied N to separate depth from data volume.

### Gaps
- No fluoroscopy paper varies N across the tens-to-hundreds range.
- Whether a synthetic-pretrained DeepDRR landmark detector fine-tuned on tens of real DeepFluoro frames beats SyntheX's 6.29 mm is unmeasured.

## Key question 4 — Any result where adding synthetic data HURT real-image performance, and at what ratio?

### Takeaway
Documented harms are of three kinds: (i) poorly simulated synthetic data is worse than realistic synthetic data (Unberath: naive DRR 78.2 vs 24.7 mm; SyntheX: naive + strong DR 20.55 mm vs real 8.15 mm), (ii) more synthetic data can over-fit the synthetic domain (Toth 2019 states this; VDSB-GWSyn 3,000 < 2,000; RoentGen 5:1 < 1:1), and (iii) synthetic-only or heavily synthetic training under-performs real in-distribution while helping out-of-distribution (Moroianu 565k synthetic-only 0.756 vs 0.772; SyntheX COVID 85.0 % vs 94.0 %). No fluoroscopy paper reports a mixed synthetic+real run that was worse than real-only.

### Cited Findings
- Unberath 2019: naive-DRR-trained landmark network 78.2 ± 26.5 mm vs DeepDRR-trained 24.7 ± 21.3 mm on real cadaver X-rays (p < 0.01) — [Unberath 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7297499/)
- SyntheX Table 5 (360×360): naive simulation with strong domain randomization 20.55 ± 35.92 mm vs 16.22 ± 39.71 mm with regular DR, vs Real2Real 8.15 ± 15.30 mm; the authors write that strong DR on naive images "results in much worse performance"; ADDA with strong DR "shows deteriorated performance than regular DR"; Naive-ADDA Dice 0.491 ± 0.405 at 180×180 regular DR (Table 4) — [SyntheX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- SyntheX COVID lesion segmentation: synthetic-only accuracy 85.03 % vs real-trained 93.95 %; precision 48.46 vs 73.67 %; F1 53.26 vs 73.48 % — [SyntheX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- Toth 2019: "the clinical data results are slightly worse than in (Toth et al., 2018)... This is due to training on significantly more data, causing the network to overfit more to the synthetic domain" (claim supported by Figure 3, not a ratio table); domain randomization then cut mean clinical error from 5–18 mm to 0.75–2.26 mm across seeds (Table 2) — [Toth 2019](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v102/toth19a/toth19a.pdf)
- RoentGen Table 5: 1.1k real + 5.5k synthetic (5:1) AUROC 0.76 < 1.1k real + 1.1k synthetic (1:1) 0.77; 1.1k synthetic only 0.69 < 1.1k real only 0.73 — [RoentGen](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.12737)
- Moroianu 2025: synthetic-only at 565k gave MIMIC in-distribution AUROC 0.756 vs 0.772 real (p < 0.05); mixing 565k synthetic into 66k real left in-distribution unchanged (0.770 vs 0.772) while VinDr OOD stayed at 0.641 — [Moroianu 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12676388/)
- VDSB-GWSyn: 3,000 synthetic pre-training images (MPE 7.78 px, PCK 85.29 %) marginally worse than 2,000 (7.71 px, 86.27 %) — [Tang 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00109)
- Barr 2026: at n = 600 real, fine-tuned-from-synthetic MSE 8.56 was worse than de novo 7.42 (the only such point of seven) — [Barr 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13185266/)
- Rapuri 2026: "No harm from synthetic data" in the cloud-augmentation experiment; DiffXray-Cloud 4.47 ± 1.49 mm — [Rapuri 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21414v1)
- FleXray: "No case of synthetic data degradation is reported" — [FleXray](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26756)
- Out of scope but often surfaced by the same searches: a 2026 Frontiers Bioeng Biotechnol paper (DOI 10.3389/fbioe.2026.1852405) on distal-upper-limb landmarks adds 900 MetaHuman renders to 2,963 **RGB photographs** (23 % synthetic, single ratio) and reports per-landmark degradation for YOLO26l-pose (+8.5 % error at one landmark) — it is not radiography and tests one ratio only — [Frontiers 2026, PMC13314767](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13314767/)

### Inferences
- In every X-ray case where synthetic data hurt, the mechanism named by the authors is realism/over-fitting to the synthetic appearance, not the ratio per se; the ratio at which harm appears is only pinned down in the generative-CXR line (RoentGen: 5:1 worse than 1:1 at 1.1k real; Moroianu: no in-distribution gain beyond ≈ 5:1) and in one guidewire paper (≈ 11:1 to 16:1).
- Because the DRR papers never mix, "adding synthetic hurt" in fluoroscopy is only ever observed as "synthetic-only is worse than real-only on this task" (SyntheX COVID) or "bad synthetic is worse than good synthetic" (Unberath, SyntheX naive), never as a mixed-corpus ratio.

### Gaps
- No fluoroscopy or DRR paper reports a mixed synthetic+real training run that under-performed real-only, so the harmful ratio for physics-simulated corpora is unknown.
- General-domain object-detection ratio results turned up by the searches (Rozantsev et al. 2015, arXiv 1411.7911: best ratio 100:1 for AdaBoost, 50:1 for DPM, 15–20:1 for CNN; arXiv 2211.16066: gains up to 5:1) were **not** found cited by any medical X-ray paper for the ratio question, so per the brief they are listed here only as a pointer, not as findings.
- The Salehinejad TMI 2019 extension may contain a synthetic-count table that the ICASSP version lacks; the TMI full text was not accessible.
