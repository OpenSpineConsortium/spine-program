# Does synthetic-X-ray fidelity (physics or style) change the real data needed or the best synthetic:real ratio?

Scope note for the writer. Every number below was read from the primary text (arXiv/PMC full text, or a PDF extracted locally with PyMuPDF) unless marked "abstract only". One fetched summary of the SyntheX PDF returned numbers that do not exist in the paper ("127 annotated clinical radiographs", "8.2 ± 4.1 mm", "500 synthetic + 50 real matched 200 real"); those were discarded after the full text was extracted and are listed here only so they are not re-introduced from another note. Test sets are labelled REAL or SYNTHETIC in each finding.

The short version, before the per-question detail:

- Physics fidelity (DeepDRR vs naive DRR) is measured only in the zero-real-data transfer setting. At precisely matched poses on 366 real cadaver fluoroscopy images, the realistic simulation roughly halves the landmark error of the naive one (9.05 vs 20.55 mm at 360 px with strong domain randomisation), and with strong randomisation plus a scaled-up synthetic set it beats the real-data-trained model (6.29 vs 8.15 mm, p = 0.032). No paper adds real images on top of physics-rendered data and sweeps the ratio.
- Style transfer (CycleGAN) applied to naive DRRs recovered the entire fidelity gap in that same benchmark (naive 16.22 mm, naive+CycleGAN 7.63 mm, real-trained 8.93 mm at 360 px, regular DR) but gave nothing over physics + strong randomisation at scale (6.59 vs 6.29 mm), and it needs real images at training time. One group abandoned CycleGAN on 250k DRR/250k radiograph pairs because it altered content.
- The only real-data learning curves with synthetic pretraining are in-domain generative (DDPM from real films) or compositing synthetic, not physics DRRs; they show 2.6-4.3x lower error at <=400 real images and roughly 100-200 fewer real images for the same error at the 500-700 range.
- Mixing synthetic DRRs into a large real training set (8 % or 37 % synthetic) did not improve the real clean test Dice for chest bone segmentation (92.58 real-only vs 91.96 / 92.30 mixed); the gain was only robustness to poses the real set lacked.
- No study was found that measures physics-calibrated noise (Poisson before detector blur, scatter) as augmentation of REAL radiographs for a detection task. The nearest is CT nodule detection, where NPS-matched noise augmentation beat no augmentation on real low-dose scans (CPM 0.677 vs 0.586, significant) but not white-Gaussian augmentation (0.677 vs 0.633, n.s.).

## Key question 1: DeepDRR (MICCAI 2018; IJCARS 2019) and ablations: what was the measured delta of physics-based rendering over naive DRR on real images, and with what real data?

### Takeaway
The DeepDRR papers themselves report the naive-vs-physics delta on real cadaver images qualitatively (MICCAI 2018) or as "significantly outperformed (p<0.01)" with the ablation tables behind a paywall (IJCARS 2019); the only fully tabulated naive/heuristic/realistic comparison on real images is SyntheX's precisely matched benchmark, where realistic rendering halves the landmark error of naive rendering but only when combined with strong domain randomisation and, above all, with more synthetic data.

### Inferences
- The only quantitative naive-vs-physics delta on real images that is publicly readable is SyntheX Table 5, not the DeepDRR papers. At the best matched setting (strong DR, 180 px) realistic 8.41 mm vs naive 13.03 mm (about 35 % lower); at 360 px strong DR the ratio is 9.05 vs 20.55 mm (56 % lower).
- The fidelity gain is not monotone across settings: with regular DR at 180 px naive (13.12) actually beat realistic (14.33), and at 360 px regular DR the gap was 16.22 vs 12.62. Physics rendering paid off reliably only with strong randomisation, which suggests the physics term mainly makes the images tolerant of strong appearance randomisation rather than being sufficient on its own.
- Scatter, the most expensive term, was the one the IJCARS ablation could not show to be significant (per indexed text; table not verified).

### Gaps
- The IJCARS 2019 ablation table (Ablations 1-4, mm and detection rates on real cadaver images; robot end-effector Dice) could not be read: Springer and PubMed pages blocked the fetch, and no arXiv or PMC copy exists. The "not significant" statement about scatter comes from an indexed snippet of the Springer page, not the paper text.
- No DeepDRR paper reports a count of real cadaver images or any experiment adding real images to the synthetic training set.

## Key question 2: SyntheX (Gao et al. 2023): domain randomisation, the claim that synthetic can match or exceed real, exact numbers, and the amounts of real data compared

### Takeaway
SyntheX's "matches or exceeds real" claim rests on 366 real cadaver fluoroscopy images (leave-one-specimen-out, six specimens) against synthetic sets of 18,000-20,000 images from 20 CTs; the win comes from strong domain randomisation plus scale (6.29 vs 8.15 mm, p = 0.032), and there is no experiment mixing real into synthetic or reducing the real set.

### Cited Findings
- Primary hip result, Table 1: Sim2Real (realistic simulation, strong DR, scaled-up synthetic set) landmark error 6.29 ± 6.29 mm and Dice 0.818 ± 0.221 vs Real2Real 8.15 ± 15.30 mm and 0.759 ± 0.248, on 366 REAL hip X-rays; "This is significantly better than the Real2Real baseline (p=0.032), despite no real data being used for model training." — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127), [PMC10959504](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10959504/)
- Real data used by the real baseline: "366 real hip fluoroscopic images and corresponding high-resolution CT scans of six lower torso cadaveric specimens"; Real2Real "in a leave-one-specimen-out fashion. For each experiment, the training and validation data consisted of all labeled images from all but one specimen". So each real fold trained on roughly five specimens (about 300 images). — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- Synthetic data used: methods say "Synthetic hip X-rays were created using 20 CT scans from the New Mexico Decedent Image Database ... We generated 18,000 images for training and 2,000 images for validation"; the discussion says "we performed experiments based on 10,000 synthetic images from 20 hip CT scans" (internal inconsistency in the paper). The precisely matched ablations (Tables 4-5, non-scaled rows) used synthetic images rendered at the 366 real poses only. — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- Scaled-up rows of Table 5 (REAL test): Realistic-Scaled 5.91±8.43 / 6.06±7.10 (180 px), 6.80±9.53 / 6.29±6.29 (360 px), 8.12±19.35 / 5.99±12.19 (480 px); Realistic-CycleGAN-Scaled 6.79±9.70 / 6.87±9.58 / 6.87±13.19 (first three cells; row truncated in the extracted text). "With more training data and geometric variety, we found that all scaled-up experiments outperform the Real2Real baseline". — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- Domain randomisation: "networks trained with strong domain randomization consistently achieved better performance than those with regular domain randomization", except on naive DRRs (see Q1). Domain randomisation "does not assume knowledge or sampling of the target domain at training time". — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- Surgical tool (continuum manipulator) task, Table 2, 264 REAL cadaver X-rays, 5-fold: Sim2Real 2.13 ± 2.27 mm, Dice 0.860 ± 0.115; Real2Real 1.90 ± 5.49 mm, Dice 0.406 ± 0.194. Synthetic set: 100 voxelised CM models, 28,000 training / 2,800 validation images. — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127), [PMC10959504](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10959504/)
- COVID-19 lesion segmentation, Table 3, 2,951 REAL chest X-rays (QaTa-COV19), 5-fold: Sim2Real overall accuracy 85.03 % vs Real2Real 93.95 %; the authors flag this as the case where soft-tissue simulation falls short. — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- What SyntheX did not do: no experiment mixes real with synthetic images, and no experiment reduces the real training set; the paper positions synthetic data as "a viable alternative to real data training, or at a minimum, a strong candidate for pre-training". — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- Domain adaptation rows of Table 5 (REAL test, matched poses; regular/strong DR at 180/360/480 px): Realistic-CycleGAN 8.65±19.84 / 8.19±13.55; 8.57±18.69 / 8.90±17.47; 10.78±26.9 / 8.75±17.4. Naive-CycleGAN 8.56±18.46 / 9.05±10.75; 7.63±16.10 / 9.29±18.73; 9.14±21.82 / 11.73±25.06. Realistic-ADDA 11.19±24.80 / 11.43±25.83; 11.01±24.69 / 12.92±23.76; 16.42±37.61 / 9.24±17.53. Naive-ADDA 7.90±17.56 / 11.84±25.63; 10.42±25.05 / 14.17±30.89; 16.62±40.12 / 22.88±41.53. — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)

### Inferences
- The "exceeds real" result is a quantity-and-variety result as much as a fidelity result: the same realistic renderer at matched poses (9.05 mm) loses to the real baseline (8.15 mm); the win appears only when the synthetic set is scaled to 18,000-20,000 images with sampled poses (6.29 mm). SyntheX therefore does not show that fidelity reduces the need for real data; it shows that with enough fidelity plus randomisation, ~300 real training images per fold can be replaced by ~20k synthetic ones for this task.
- The real baseline is small (six specimens) and its error distribution is heavy-tailed (SD 15.30 mm); the "significant" advantage is partly a variance reduction, which the authors attribute to the synthetic set's richer anatomy and geometry.

### Gaps
- The paper's synthetic-set size is stated two ways (10,000 vs 18,000+2,000); which one produced the 6.29 mm row cannot be resolved from the text.
- No SyntheX experiment reports a synthetic:real mixture or a reduced-real curve, so nothing here answers the ratio question directly.

## Key question 3: Style transfer / CycleGAN / diffusion DRR-to-fluoro translation for training detectors: measured gains, losses, and failures on real frames

### Takeaway
Measured on real images, CycleGAN closes the naive-DRR gap completely (naive 16.22 to 7.63 mm; real-trained 8.93 mm) but adds nothing on top of physics rendering with strong randomisation at scale (6.59 vs 6.29 mm) and requires real images to train; ADDA mostly hurt; one group abandoned CycleGAN outright because it altered image content; the one spine fluoroscopy study that ran style transfer against fine-tuning found them equal (F1 0.84 both).

### Cited Findings
- SyntheX, matched poses, REAL test: Naive-CycleGAN 7.63 ± 16.10 mm vs Naive 16.22 ± 39.71 mm vs Real2Real 8.93 ± 19.76 mm (360 px, regular DR); Realistic-CycleGAN 8.57 ± 18.69 vs Realistic 12.62 ± 27.68 (same column). "both Realistic-CycleGAN and Naive-CycleGAN achieve comparable performance ... images via CycleGAN have similar appearance" regardless of the input renderer. — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- SyntheX at scale, REAL test: "Training on large-scale data processed by CycleGAN achieved comparable performance (6.59±7.25 mm) as pure realistic simulation with domain randomization [6.29±6.29 mm], but comes at the disadvantage that real data with sufficient variability must be available at training time to enable CycleGAN training." — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- SyntheX on ADDA (feature-level adaptation), REAL test: "ADDA with strong DR shows deteriorated performance than regular DR"; Realistic-ADDA rows (11.01-16.42 mm) are worse than Realistic with strong DR (8.41-13.06 mm) in five of six cells. — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- RadRotator (Mayo, 2024): "A dataset of 250,000 DRRs ... and 250,000 AP radiographs from a Total Hip Arthroplasty Radiography Registry was compiled, and a CycleGAN was trained ... The performance of the CycleGAN proved to be inconsistent during inference, where in some cases the style transfer occurred without noticeable issues, yet in others, the content of the original image was significantly altered ... the CycleGAN was replaced with the inclusion of the RandHistogramShift transformation". No downstream numbers were reported for the CycleGAN arm. — [arXiv 2404.13000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13000)
- Jecklin et al. 2024 (X23D lumbar spine 3D reconstruction from fluoroscopy, Med Image Anal, PMID 39197301): paired dataset of 4,337 real/synthetic fluoroscopy pairs from 13 ex-vivo specimens (10 specimens / 3,317 images training, 3 specimens / 1,020 images test); synthetic training set 120,148 DRRs from 863 CTs; base in-silico model on real X-rays: "almost no sample achieved a surface score greater than 0.6"; transfer learning (fine-tuning on real) F1 0.84 ± 0.04 (0.85 ± 0.08 with ground-truth origins); style transfer real-to-synthetic F1 0.84 ± 0.03 (0.88 ± 0.04 with ground-truth origins); further validation on six unseen specimens on three C-arm systems gave surface scores 0.64-0.73. Direction was real-to-synthetic only, chosen so that "existing and future synthetically trained networks" can be applied. Test set REAL. No sweep of the real data amount. — [arXiv 2401.16027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16027), [DOI 10.1016/j.media.2024.103322](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361841524002470)
- Rapuri et al. 2026 (JHU, 2D vs 3D diffusion for in-silico training), pelvic landmark detection on DeepFluoro (366 REAL images, six cadavers), 6-fold precisely matched; L2 error in mm at NCC thresholds 0.60 / 0.40 / 0.20: RealXray 4.89±6.42 / 9.50±19.61 / 18.94±35.57; RealCT-DRR 7.83±25.67 / 12.32±27.33 / 17.92±30.53; DiffCT-DRR (DRRs of 3D-diffusion CTs) 5.82±13.72 / 12.36±25.82 / 23.64±38.73; DiffXray (2D diffusion conditioned on segmentations) 4.40±1.87 / 12.17±26.88 / 25.40±37.93. "Cloud" experiments adding 500 views per patient with ±2.5° perturbations: RealCT-DRR-Cloud 4.87±1.75 / 6.02±2.40 / 9.73±13.16; DiffXray-Cloud 4.47±1.49 / 10.12±22.11 / 15.09±26.55. The DRR renderer is not named in the text. — [arXiv 2606.21414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21414)
- Toth et al. MIDL 2019 (cardiac LV model-to-X-ray registration, trained on DRRs from 1,711 CTs of 799 patients with no real X-rays, evaluated on 21 clinical patients x 169 perturbations, REAL): with no domain randomisation mean translational robustness error 18.45 mm, 90th percentile 85.79 mm, 342 outliers, 69.57 % within 5 mm; collimation DR alone 4.03 mm / 7.81 mm / 169 / 84.76 %; intensity DR alone 2.42 / 1.42 / 77 / 97.49 %; both 0.75 mm / 1.41 mm / 30 / 99.94 %. The randomisation was deliberately unrealistic ("adds unrealistic perturbations ... such that the target domain becomes just another variation"). On the SYNTHETIC test set the two networks were equal (mean 2.80 vs 2.65 mm). — [MIDL 2019 PMLR v102](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v102/toth19a/toth19a.pdf)
- Pelphix (MICCAI 2023, surgical phase recognition trained on DeepDRR-simulated sequences only): "average accuracy of 99.2% on simulated sequences and 71.7% in cadaver across all granularity levels, with up to 84% accuracy for the target corridor in real data" (257 REAL cadaver images). — [arXiv 2304.09285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09285), [PMC11016332](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11016332/)
- FluoroSAM (MICCAI 2025, trained from scratch on 3M synthetic X-rays): on 464 REAL cadaver fluoroscopy images (Brainlab Loop-X) text-only prompting Dice 0.51, with point refinement 0.79; no real-trained comparator. — [arXiv 2403.08059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08059), [PMC12822567](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12822567/)

### Inferences
- Across SyntheX Table 5 the CycleGAN rows are nearly renderer-independent (naive and realistic inputs land within ~1 mm of each other after translation), so for a detector the "style" a translation network imposes carries most of what physics rendering carries; the physics term becomes decisive only when no real images are available to learn the style from.
- Rapuri's cloud result is the cleanest evidence that pose variety, a lever synthetic data has and real data does not, matters more than the rendering source: at matched poses the plain DRR arm was worst (7.83 mm at 0.60 NCC), and with 500 extra views per patient it became best at every lower threshold (6.02 and 9.73 mm at 0.40 / 0.20 NCC vs 9.50 / 18.94 for real-trained).
- Two independent failure modes of translation are documented: content alteration (RadRotator) and no gain over randomisation at scale (SyntheX); no paper found reports a case where style-translated data was worse than untranslated naive DRRs on a real test set.

### Gaps
- No paper found trains a vertebra/level detector on DRR-to-fluoro translated images and reports real-frame recall against untranslated DRRs; the closest are pelvic landmarks (SyntheX, Rapuri) and lumbar 3D reconstruction (Jecklin).
- The Jecklin paper's transfer-learning arm and its train/test specimen counts were read from an HTML summary; the number of real images used to fine-tune (3,317 vs 1,020) was reported ambiguously and should be checked against the PDF before quoting.
- Diffusion-based DRR-to-fluoro translation for detector training: nothing measured on real frames was found beyond Rapuri (which conditions diffusion on segmentations, not on DRRs).

## Key question 4: Papers that sweep fidelity AND ratio together, or show calibrated synthetic data lets fewer real images reach the same performance

### Takeaway
No paper sweeps rendering fidelity and synthetic:real ratio jointly. Learning-curve evidence that synthetic pretraining reduces the real images needed exists only for in-domain generative or compositing synthetic data (2.6-4.3x lower error at <=400 real cervical films; Dice 0.55 vs 0.10 for catheters), and the one study that mixed physics-free DRRs into a large real set found no gain on the real clean test.

### Cited Findings
- Synthetic cervical spine radiographs (BMC Med Inform Decis Mak 2026, PMID 41965677): synthetic images are DDPM samples trained on 4,963 real CSXA lateral films (not DRRs), 20,063 released; task is 23-keypoint landmark regression (SCN); REAL held-out test CLX-34, 374 images. Learning curve, MSE, fine-tuning from the synthetic-pretrained backbone vs de novo: n=100 31.78 vs 84.33; n=200 12.97 vs 55.84; n=300 10.10 vs 34.30; n=400 9.74 vs 27.21; n=500 7.49 vs 14.17; n=600 8.56 vs 7.42; n=700 4.37 vs 7.42. Synthetic-only zero-shot on CLX-34: MSE 124.73. "Fine-tuning from the synthetic backbone outperformed de novo training at 6 of 7 matched training sizes." — [PMC13185266](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13185266/), [DOI 10.1186/s12911-026-03479-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12911-026-03479-0)
- Gherardini et al. 2020 (catheter segmentation in fluoroscopy, Comput Methods Programs Biomed, PMID 32171151): synthetic set of 9,000 images made by pasting transformed catheter masks onto 32 real fluoroscopy background patches (compositing, not DRR); phantom set 2,000; in-vivo 1,207 (836 TAVI + 371 diagnostic). REAL in-vivo test Dice: synthetic-pretrained + fine-tuned on 240 in-vivo images 0.55; phantom-pretrained + fine-tuned 0.54; in-vivo-only leave-one-out with no pretraining 0.10. Further splits with 493 and 579 fine-tuning images gave 0.26 and 0.17 (synthetic) and 0.19 (phantom) on their own test sequences. — [PMC7903142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7903142/)
- Hadzic et al. 2024 (DDPM augmentation for hand X-ray landmarks; 895 real images, 37 landmarks; REAL test 298 images): full data (597 real) PE 0.76 ± 0.79 mm real-only, 0.75 ± 0.77 mm real + DDPM (outliers >4 mm 92 vs 84), 0.77 ± 0.79 real + VAE (worse); reduced data (10 % = 60 real) PE 0.82 ± 0.84 real-only vs 0.84 ± 0.81 real + DDPM, outliers >10 mm 3 vs 1, >20 mm 1 vs 0; VAE images had 0 % acceptance at 10 %. Synthetic images capped at 400; MRF/SSM acceptance 40 % (full) and 26 % (10 %). — [arXiv 2410.12489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12489)
- Chest X-ray bone segmentation with CT-derived synthetic X-rays (2024, PMC11519576): DRRs by cone-beam ray tracing from 116 photon-counting CTs, "attenuation and scattering" modelled, standard post-processing; real training 3,434 CXR, REAL test 350. Gains appeared only on the SYNTHETIC rotated test set (+10-12 % Dice at ±12-20°, +3-7 % at ±4-10°). — [PMC11519576](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11519576/)
- Patient-agnostic synthetic pretraining for 2D/3D registration (2026): Siddon DRRs with a segmentation-free domain-randomisation scheme (intensity, projection-physics and occlusion pools); REAL test on DeepFluoro (366 images) and Ljubljana (10 CBCT/X-ray pairs). No fidelity comparison. — [arXiv 2607.23343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23343)
- AnyCXR (2025): 100,000+ DiffDRR chest DRRs from 2,982 CTs with three-stage domain randomisation; ablation (Dice PA / lateral) on the zero-shot evaluation set: plain 91.62 / 85.98; post-projection 2D augmentation only 93.75 / 89.16; 3D pre-projection randomisation only 94.03 / 87.60; both 94.53 / 91.35. No real-only baseline and no mixing; expert-revised REAL samples (n=40) gave DSC 0.926-0.978 PA and 0.795-0.982 lateral. — [arXiv 2512.17263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17263)
- Agentic C-arm landmark localisation (2026): 51,200 DeepDRR image-answer pairs from 50 NMDID CTs (10 held out); a REAL multi-region X-ray set of 1,738 images, 1,564 used for fine-tuning and 174 for test; DL baseline Hit@2 0.92 on synthetic, 0.95 on real after real fine-tuning; Gemma-3 27B fine-tuned 0.85 / 0.93; base Gemma-3 27B 0.42 / 0.90. No synthetic-only-on-real arm. — [arXiv 2604.18740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18740)
- Enhanced pelvic landmark detection with a 2D/3D registration loss (2025): trained on DiffDRR renders only (100 per CT, 80 CTs); tested only on SYNTHETIC DiffDRR views (novel view RMSE 8.58 vs 8.45 px; novel subject 5.58 vs 5.09 px); "The effectiveness of our method on clinical images has yet to be confirmed." Included as a warning that several recent "landmark detection in fluoroscopy" papers have no real test set. — [arXiv 2511.21575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21575)

### Inferences
- Where a real-data learning curve exists (cervical DDPM), synthetic pretraining bought roughly 100-200 real images at the 500-700 range (fine-tune at 500 real, MSE 7.49, sits between de novo at 600 and 700, both 7.42) and a 2.6-4.3x error reduction below 400 real images; but the synthetic images were generated from the same real corpus, so this measures in-domain generative augmentation, not physics fidelity.
- The bone-segmentation mixing result is the one direct test of "add DRRs to plenty of real data": at 8 % and 37 % synthetic the real-test Dice did not move (92.58 to 91.96 / 92.30). With a large real set, synthetic DRRs bought robustness to unseen poses, not accuracy on the real distribution.
- Combining SyntheX (fidelity x quantity, zero real) with the cervical curve (quantity of real, one synthetic type), the consistent pattern is that synthetic quantity and pose/appearance variety, not renderer physics, are what move real-image performance once some floor of realism is reached; no measurement exists of whether a higher-fidelity floor shifts the crossover point with real data.

### Gaps
- No study varies rendering fidelity (naive / physics / style-matched) and the synthetic:real ratio in one design; the question "does calibrated synthetic data shift the best ratio" has no measured answer in the literature found.
- No physics-DRR (DeepDRR-class) learning curve over the number of real fine-tuning images was found for any X-ray detection task.
- A Frontiers 2026 landmark study (PMC13314767) that reported real-only vs real + synthetic (3.00 vs 2.81 mm, HRNet) used Unreal Engine RGB hand photographs, not radiographs, and was excluded.

## Key question 5: Physics-calibrated noise (Poisson before detector blur, scatter) as augmentation of REAL images: measured effect on detection

### Takeaway
No measured X-ray detection result was found for physics-calibrated noise augmentation of real radiographs; the X-ray literature uses calibrated noise only for test-time robustness evaluation or for denoiser training, and the nearest measured detection result is in CT, where NPS-matched noise augmentation beat no augmentation on real low-dose scans but was not significantly better than white Gaussian noise.

### Cited Findings
- Physics-informed data augmentation for low-dose CT nodule detection (2026, PMC13366036): noise synthesised in the image domain from the scanner's noise power spectrum with random phase (not projection-domain Poisson), SD 1-5 HU emulating ~20-80 mAs; trained on 187 high-dose LIDC-IDRI scans, tested on 318 REAL clinical low-dose scans (<100 mAs). CPM on low-dose: baseline 0.586, white-Gaussian augmentation 0.633, physics-informed 0.677; across all doses 0.621 / 0.651 / 0.679. Physics vs baseline ΔCPM 0.09 (95 % CI 0.02-0.17); physics vs Gaussian ΔCPM 0.04 (95 % CI -0.04 to 0.12), not significant. — [PMC13366036](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13366036/)
- Radiographic noise injection framework for chest X-ray (2025): quantum (Poisson) and electronic (Gaussian) noise applied at test time only; lung segmentation Dice fell by 0.843 under severe electronic noise; pneumothorax-vs-atelectasis AUROC fell by 0.355 under quantum noise while being robust to electronic noise; no noise-augmented training arm. — [arXiv 2509.25265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25265)
- "Pre-trained Under Noise" fracture detection (2025): Poisson, Gaussian and mixed Poisson-Gaussian noise chosen because "X-ray radiographs were found to be most prone to Poisson and Gaussian noise distributions"; noise applied only at test time on 10,580 REAL radiographs; no augmentation comparison. — [arXiv 2507.09731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09731)
- Scaphoid fracture degradation study (2025): Gaussian blur, Gaussian noise, JPEG, CLAHE, resizing; clean accuracy 92.03 %; the authors note prior recommendations to inject noise in training but did not do it ("the addition of many possible noises to a training dataset is both computationally expensive and impractical"). — [PMC12826633](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12826633/)
- Realistic low-dose fluoroscopy simulation (IJCARS 2019, PMID 30779022): an image-formation-model noise simulator (gain, electronic noise) whose "simulated low-dose images ... are almost indistinguishable from real low-dose images", intended to train denoisers; no detection result. — [DOI 10.1007/s11548-019-01912-6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11548-019-01912-6)
- SyntheX's own noise handling on synthetic data: DeepDRR "realistic" simulation (polyenergetic attenuation, scatter, quantum and readout noise) vs xreg "heuristic" and naive; the realistic renderer's advantage on real images is in Q1 above, but the noise term was not ablated separately from attenuation and scatter. — [SyntheX arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)

### Inferences
- The CT result is the only measured analogue: calibrated noise texture helped versus nothing (ΔCPM 0.09) but its edge over generic Gaussian noise (0.04) was inside the confidence interval, which is the same shape as the DeepDRR scatter finding (Q1): the calibrated term is not demonstrably better than a cruder one once some noise is present.
- In the X-ray literature, calibrated Poisson/electronic noise is used to break models at test time, and the measured drops (Dice -0.843, AUROC -0.355) are large enough that noise augmentation of real training films is a plausible lever, but no one has reported it for detection.

### Gaps
- No paper found measures detection recall or landmark error on real radiographs/fluoroscopy after augmenting real training images with projection-domain Poisson noise applied before detector blur, with or without scatter. This is an open measurement, not a settled one.
- Whether noise ordering (Poisson before vs after blur) matters for a downstream detector has not been tested anywhere found; the DeepDRR papers describe correlating quantum noise by convolving with a crosstalk kernel but do not ablate that step.
