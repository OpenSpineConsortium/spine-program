# Corpus-size scaling and synthetic/real curricula for radiograph detectors and classifiers

Scope: measured data-scaling curves for YOLO-family detectors and ResNet-scale classifiers on radiographs (and nearest CT/3-D analogues), and measured schemes for mixing synthetic (DRR/CT-derived) and real X-ray data. Every number below is quoted from the primary source linked; resolution and model family are stated where the source states them, and flagged when it does not. The user's setting is 1024-px frames, YOLO11m detector, ResNet-18 classifier — note at the outset that **no source found measures a learning curve for a YOLO detector on radiographs at that resolution**; the closest measured curves are (a) CXR classification at 224–512 px with ResNet-50/DenseNet-121/EfficientNet, (b) a YOLOv5s carpal-bone detector re-trained at 16–200 radiographs, and (c) detector-scaling curves on street scenes.

## Key question 1 — Data-scaling / learning-curve studies in medical imaging detection and classification: where do YOLO-type detectors and ResNet-type classifiers show diminishing returns?

### Takeaway
For chest-radiograph classification with ResNet/DenseNet/EfficientNet backbones, two independent large studies place the knee of the curve at roughly 20,000 training images: AUC climbs steeply from ~2k to ~20k and then gains only 0.01–0.09 AUC over the next 10× (to 200k–291k). For detector re-training with transfer learning on radiographs, the only measured curve found (YOLOv5s, carpal bones) reached precision/recall/mAP > 0.95 at 96–100 radiographs; no YOLO learning curve at ~1024 px on radiographs with N on a log axis was found. Medical segmentation follows a three-parameter power law E(N) = aN^-b + c with early, task-dependent saturation, but per-task exponents for X-ray were not extractable.

### Cited Findings

**Classification, chest radiographs (ResNet/DenseNet/EfficientNet)**

- Pneumothorax classification, 291,454 merged open-source CXRs, tested on 525 emergency-department radiographs: "Model AUCs and accuracy increased rapidly from 2 × 10³ to 20 × 10³ training samples, with more gradual increase until the maximum training dataset size." ResNet-50 AUC 0.86 (20k) → 0.95 (291k); DenseNet-121 0.85 (20k) → 0.93 (291k); EfficientNet 0.92 (20k) → 0.98 (291k). Authors state learning curves "suggest a point of diminishing performance returns for increasing training data volumes". Image resolution not stated in the abstract. — [Effect of Training Data Volume on Performance of CNN Pneumothorax Classifiers, J Digit Imaging 2022, PMID 35239091, DOI 10.1007/s10278-022-00594-y](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35239091/) (abstract retrieved via [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:35239091%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json))
- Normal/abnormal CXR classification, 216,431 frontal radiographs, held-out test of 533 expert-labelled images: AUC 0.84 with 2,000 training images, 0.95 with 20,000, 0.96 with 200,000; the 200k result "was not significantly different from that observed when the model was trained with 20 000 images (AUC = 0.95, P > .05)". Architecture and resolution not given in the abstract (the paper assessed "network architecture" as a factor). — [Dunnmon et al., Radiology 2019, PMID 30422093, DOI 10.1148/radiol.2018181422](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/radiol.2018181422) (abstract via [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:30422093%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json))
- CXR-specific pretraining (SupCon on 821,544 CXRs) vs ImageNet pretraining, 10 tasks, five datasets (684,955 CXRs), training-set sizes "from eight to 8⁵" (= 32,768): "SupCon reduced label requirements up to 688-fold and improved the AUC at matching data set sizes". 45 CXRs → AUC 0.95 for microbiology-confirmed TB in external validation; 528 CXRs → AUC 0.75 for severe COVID-19 outcomes. Three setups (linear, nonlinear, full fine-tune). — [Sellergren et al., Simplified Transfer Learning for Chest Radiography Models Using Less Data, Radiology 2022, DOI 10.1148/radiol.212482](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/radiol.212482) (abstract via [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Simplified%20Transfer%20Learning%20for%20Chest%20Radiography%20Models%20Using%20Less%20Data%22&resultType=core&format=json))
- Fine-tuning depth × dataset size, 5,856 CXRs, fixed ResNet-50: "Linear probing remains competitive in low-data settings... The gap between shallow and deeper fine-tuning narrows in larger regimes, indicating diminishing returns from additional unfreezing". No per-size AUCs in the abstract. — [An Empirical Study of Dataset Size Effects on Fine-Tuning Depth, PMID 42077689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42077689/) (abstract via [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:42077689%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json))
- Resolution dependence for CXR classification (ResNet34, DenseNet121; 32×32 to 600×600): "Maximum AUCs were achieved at image resolutions between 256 × 256 and 448 × 448 pixels". Nodules: AUC 0.689 at 64×64 vs 0.854 at 320×320; masses 0.767 vs 0.886. Largest resolution gains for emphysema, cardiomegaly, hernia, nodules. — [Sabottke & Spieler, The Effect of Image Resolution on Deep Learning in Radiography, Radiology: AI 2020, DOI 10.1148/ryai.2019190015](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/ryai.2019190015) (abstract via [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22The%20Effect%20of%20Image%20Resolution%20on%20Deep%20Learning%20in%20Radiography%22&resultType=core&format=json))

**Classification, CT (learning-curve fit)**

- Six-class axial CT body-part classification, GoogLeNet, tested on 6,000 CT images (1,000 per class). Mean accuracy vs training images per class: 5 → 8.01 %, 10 → 17.37 %, 20 → 51.54 %, 50 → 77.15 %, 100 → 89.68 %, 200 → 95.67 %. Fitted learning curve predicted 98 % at 1,000 per class and 4,092 per class for 99.5 %. — [Cho et al. 2015, arXiv 1511.06348](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06348v2) (full text via [r.jina.ai](https://r.jina.ai/https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.06348v2))

**Detection, radiographs (YOLO)**

- Re-training YOLOv5s (transfer learning) to localise carpal bones on DMPLO carpal radiographs, leave-one-out cross-validation over training-set sizes: "Models trained using 96 radiographs or more achieved precision, recall and mAP above 0.95, including for the first carpal bone, when trained for 32 epochs." The smaller first carpal bone needed twice the epochs of the other structures. Recommendation: ~100 radiographs suffice for this task. Input resolution not stated in the abstract. — [How many radiographs are needed to re-train a deep learning system for object detection?, arXiv 2210.08734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08734)
- Fracture detection with YOLO on radiographs reports training-iteration convergence, not corpus-size curves: tibial-plateau study, 1,489 knee X-rays (727 fracture / 762 non-fracture), mAP stabilised at 99.9 % (AP view) and 97.8 % (lateral) after ~1,200 iterations; YOLOv9 AUC 0.99 on AP. — [Diagnostics 2026, DOI 10.3390/diagnostics16020182](https://doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics16020182). GRAZPEDWRI-DX (20,327 paediatric wrist radiographs, 6,091 patients): YOLOv10-M mAP@50 94 %, fracture sensitivity 92.5 % — [arXiv 2407.15689](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15689v1). Neither varies N.

**Segmentation scaling laws (medical)**

- 15 anatomical segmentation tasks across four modalities (X-ray, CT, MRI, retinal), training sizes in powers of two, images at 512×512, nnUNet (1.96 M params) and Swin-UNet (1.59 M params), batch 8, ≤50 epochs: error follows E(N) = aN^-b + c with "earlier and task-dependent performance saturation" and "a persistent error floor"; CXR tasks include lung, heart, facies diaphragmatica and spine segmentation. Per-task a, b, c are shown in Figure 3 but not tabulated in the text. — [Revisiting Data Scaling Law for Medical Segmentation, arXiv 2511.13883](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13883)
- 3-D medical MAE pretraining (ViT, 96³ crops at 1.5 mm), loss L(T) = E + C/T^β, fitted β by domain: BRAIN-T1 0.209, BRAIN-T2 0.243, ABD-CT 0.407, HN-CT 0.436, ABD-MRI 0.487, HN-PET 0.724 ("3.5×" spread). Transfer-aware allocation reached 0.0451 mean MAE loss at a 50 K-volume budget vs 0.0712 for data-proportional sampling (the "58 %" claim; a reconstruction-loss metric, not a downstream task). — [Knowledge Transfer Scaling Laws for 3D Medical Imaging, arXiv 2605.06859](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06859v1)

### Inferences
- The two large CXR classification curves agree on the order of magnitude: the first 10× (2k → 20k) buys ~0.1 AUC; the next 10× buys 0.01 (Dunnmon) to 0.06–0.09 (pneumothorax, ResNet-50/EfficientNet). For a ResNet-18 classifier on frames, expect the knee well below 10⁵ images and a floor that is set by label noise/anatomy rather than N — consistent with the segmentation power-law's "persistent error floor".
- The YOLOv5s carpal result (>0.95 mAP at ~100 radiographs) is for large, high-contrast, always-present bones with transfer learning; it should be read as a lower bound on required N for easy anatomy, not as evidence that detection saturates at 100 images in general — the same paper found small objects needed 2× the training.
- The Sabottke/Spieler result (peak 256–448 px for CXR classification at ResNet34/DenseNet121) implies that at 1024 px a ResNet-18 classifier is unlikely to be resolution-limited; the resolution cost is borne by the detector, where no medical learning curve at 1024 px was found.

### Gaps
- No paper found that gives a YOLO-family detector learning curve on radiographs (accuracy vs N, log axis) at 512–1024 px. Searches for VinDr-CXR / fracture / nodule detection with 10 %/25 %/50 %/100 % training-fraction ablations returned no such ablation.
- Per-task fitted exponents (b) and floors (c) for the X-ray segmentation tasks in arXiv 2511.13883 are only in a figure and could not be extracted.
- Input resolution is not stated in the abstracts of the pneumothorax, Dunnmon, Sellergren and carpal-YOLOv5s studies; the pneumothorax and Dunnmon full texts were behind cookie/redirect walls.

## Key question 2 — Real-image upweighting, progressive mixing, domain-randomisation schedules, or synthetic-then-real fine-tuning vs joint training: which papers measured these against each other, and with what differences?

### Takeaway
No X-ray paper was found that measures real-image upweighting, a progressive synthetic→real ramp, or a mixing schedule against alternatives. The medical X-ray sim-to-real literature (SyntheX, Toth 2019, Pelphix, FluoroSAM, AnyCXR) trains on synthetic data only and measures the *strength of domain randomisation*, not the real/synthetic mixing rule. The head-to-head measurements of fine-tune vs joint training come from general object detection: one study (YOLOv3, Cityscapes/Synscapes) found no significant difference (mAP50 0.371 ± 0.008 vs 0.384 ± 0.013) and an optimal real share of 5–20 %; another (Faster R-CNN, 160 real images) found two-stage pretrain-then-fine-tune generally beat mixing in the low-real regime; Tremblay et al. found DR-pretraining + fine-tuning on 6,000 real images beat real-only (AP 98.5 vs 96.5) and that freezing early layers hurt by up to 13.5 %.

### Cited Findings

**X-ray / DRR sim-to-real (synthetic only; DR strength is the measured variable)**

- SyntheX (hip landmark detection + segmentation, TransUNet at 360×360, 480×480 high-res variant; 20 CTs → 18,000 train + 2,000 val synthetic; real test 366 Siemens + 60 BrainLab LoopX radiographs): Real2Real landmark error 6.46 ± 8.21 mm / Dice 0.79 ± 0.25; Sim2Real (realistic simulation + strong DR) 6.44 ± 7.05 mm / 0.80 ± 0.23; Realistic + CycleGAN 6.62 ± 6.82 mm / 0.80 ± 0.23. "Networks trained with strong domain randomization consistently achieved better performance than those with regular domain randomization", except on naive (non-physics) simulations where strong DR hurt. Regular DR = Gaussian noise, gamma, random crop; strong DR added inversion, salt-and-pepper, affine, contrast, blur, box corruption, dropout, sharpen, pooling, brightness, distortion. No real-data fine-tuning or mixing in the main results; domain adaptation (CycleGAN, ADDA) was tested as an alternative and matched but did not beat SyntheX. Robotic tool: Sim2Real 1.10 ± 0.88 mm / Dice 0.92 vs Real2Real 1.19 ± 2.49 mm / Dice 0.41. COVID lesion segmentation (224×224, 81 + 62 CTs): Sim2Real accuracy 85.22 % vs Real2Real 94.05 %, gap attributed to annotation mismatch. — [Gao et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2023, DOI 10.1038/s42256-023-00629-1, PMC10959504](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10959504/); preprint [arXiv 2206.06127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06127)
- Toth et al. 2019 (3-D/2-D cardiac model-to-X-ray registration by imitation learning, CNN agent): 1,711 CTs from 799 patients; 10,000 training DRRs = 1,000 CTs × 10 perturbations; synthetic test 1,000 DRRs from 100 other patients; real test 21 patients × 169 perturbations = 3,549 X-rays. On DRRs both networks ~2.7 mm mean error; on real X-rays the non-randomised network's mean error ranged 5.77–18.45 mm across training seeds vs 0.75–2.26 mm with DR; outliers 98–342 vs 28–114. Intensity randomisation alone gave the largest gain; intensity + collimation randomisation was best. Resolution not stated. — [Training Deep Networks on Domain Randomized Synthetic X-ray Data for Cardiac Interventions, MIDL 2019, PMLR v102](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v102/toth19a.html) (full text via [r.jina.ai](https://r.jina.ai/http://proceedings.mlr.press/v102/toth19a/toth19a.pdf))
- Patient-agnostic synthetic pretraining for 2-D/3-D registration (256×256; 500 k regressor + 200 k similarity synthetic samples for pretraining; 20 k patient-specific DRRs): sub-millimetre success rate on DeepFluoro (6 CTs) / Ljubljana (10 CBCTs): no DR 76.2 / 77.0 %; + intensity/contrast DR 79.6 / 80.0 %; + projection-physics DR 83.2 / 83.8 %; full 84.7 / 85.0 %. Patient-specific fine-tuning from the synthetic-pretrained model: 0.47 h vs 20.70 h from scratch (≈44×) at 84.7 % vs 86.1 % SMSR. — [arXiv 2607.23343](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23343)
- Pelphix (surgical phase recognition from X-ray, 384×384, transformer over U-Net features): 337 CTs (327 train / 10 val) → 677 simulated sequences, 279,709 training images; simulated test accuracy corridor 99.7 %, activity 98.2 %, view 99.8 %, frame 99.0 %; real cadaver X-rays (257 images) corridor 84 %, activity 60 %, view 65 %, frame 77 %. No real fine-tuning performed; authors propose "Pelphix-style simulation for pretraining, before fine-tuning on real image sequences". — [Killeen et al., MICCAI 2023, PMC11016332](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11016332/); [arXiv 2304.09285](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09285)
- FluoroSAM (language-promptable X-ray segmentation): 1,621 real CTs → 3 M synthetic X-rays, 128 organ types + 464 tools; real chest X-ray Dice ~0.6, comparable to MedSAM, with "no public X-ray datasets ... introduced in addition"; no real fine-tuning reported. — [MICCAI 2025 open-access page](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2025/0344-Paper5042.html); [arXiv 2403.08059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08059)
- AnyCXR: multi-stage domain randomisation (pre- and post-projection) from CT volumes, >100,000 synthetic radiographs, 54 structures, trained on synthetic only, "strong zero-shot generalization on multiple real-world datasets" in PA, lateral and oblique views. Numbers not in the abstract. — [arXiv 2512.17263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17263)
- FleXray (universal X-ray segmentation): 2,159 CT subjects across six CT datasets plus 764 real radiographs from four X-ray datasets (peripheral anatomy gaps) and MURA annotations; dataset construction treated "as an iterative coverage problem"; the single-network variant used "equal proportions of standard and generatively enhanced X-rays" and the ensemble of five networks used different proportions; generatively enhanced DRRs gave +2.2 to +5.8 Dice on VinDr-Rib ribs 5–10; "mixing online and offline rendering performs best" (Table 8). — [arXiv 2609.26756](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26756). A search snippet of the same paper reports online-MOOSE + enhanced-DRR proportions summing to 75 % with the remaining sources at 25 %; this was not confirmed in the fetched text.
- Autonomous skeletal landmark localisation for C-arm control: 50 training / 10 test NMDID CTs, 1,024 DeepDRR projections per CT (51,200 train / 10,240 test synthetic image–answer pairs); real set 1,738 X-rays with 22 landmarks (1,564 for fine-tuning, 174 test). DL baseline (21 M params) Hit@2 0.95 / Recall@2 0.94 on real; fine-tuned Gemma-3 27B Hit@2 0.93 / Recall@2 0.91. Synthetic-only vs real-fine-tuned conditions are not reported separately. — [arXiv 2604.18740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18740)

**Mixed real + rendered (non-DRR) synthetic for a YOLO-pose model on radiographs**

- Distal-upper-limb landmark localisation: MetaHuman-rendered synthetic images added to real (real-only 2,963 vs mixed 3,863 images, ≈30 % more); mixed training reduced mean localisation error for all six models, significant for YOLO26x-pose (−17.8 %, p = 0.002), YOLO26s-pose (−14.5 %, p = 0.001) and HRNet-W48 (−6.3 %, p = 0.020); all models < 3.1 mm under mixed training. Caveat from the authors: the mixed set has ~30 % more images and gradient updates, so synthetic content and extra optimisation cannot be disentangled. — [PMID 42382666](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42382666/) (abstract via [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:42382666%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json))

**General object detection: fine-tune vs joint training, mixing ratios (nearest measured comparisons)**

- YOLOv3 (COCO-pretrained), Cityscapes real (2,727 images) + Synscapes synthetic (25,000); real ratios 0/5/10/20/50/100 % across 10 log-spaced subset sizes. Pretrain-on-synthetic-then-fine-tune vs mixed training: "no significant difference", mAP50 0.3706 ± 0.0082 vs 0.3841 ± 0.0132 (Table I). At a 10 % real ratio, 778 real images matched the all-real (2,727) model, ≈70 % real-data reduction. "The best ratio of real data in a mixed training dataset is between 5 % and 20 %." Input resolution not stated. — [Reducing the Amount of Real World Data for Object Detector Training with Synthetic Data, arXiv 2202.00632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00632) (full text via [r.jina.ai](https://r.jina.ai/https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00632))
- Faster R-CNN at 1200×800, 160 real training images, 500 synthetic images per variant (realistic / half-realistic / random): real-only mAP@0.5:0.95 0.643 ID / 0.466 OOD at 10 % real, 0.789 / 0.581 at 50 %, 0.795 / 0.585 at 100 %; adding 50 % synthetic improved both across ratios; two-stage "Bridged Transfer Learning" (synthetic pretrain → real fine-tune) "generally outperforms the mixed approach ... especially in low-real-data regimes"; realistic synthetic alone was "far worse than their real-data-only counterparts on in-distribution test data". — [arXiv 2510.12208](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12208v1)
- Domain randomisation for car detection (Faster R-CNN, 100,000 DR images, KITTI): DR-only AP@0.5 78.1, VKITTI-only 79.7, real-only (6,000 KITTI) 96.5; DR pretraining + fine-tuning: 100 real → 84.2, 1,000 real → 88.9, 6,000 real → 98.5. "Freezing the weights of the early network layers ... actually decreased rather than increased performance" (up to 13.5 %). — [Tremblay et al. 2018, arXiv 1804.06516](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06516) (full text via [r.jina.ai](https://r.jina.ai/https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06516))

### Inferences
- In every X-ray sim-to-real paper found, the lever that was measured and that mattered was the randomisation applied to the synthetic images (intensity/contrast first, then projection physics/collimation), not how real images were weighted or scheduled. The only X-ray-domain numbers on "real after synthetic" are Tremblay-style fine-tuning in the registration paper (44× faster to the same accuracy) — there is no X-ray measurement of a ramped or upweighted mix.
- Where the fine-tune vs joint comparison was measured (street-scene detectors), the difference was within noise at moderate real counts and favoured two-stage fine-tuning only when real data were very scarce (tens to low hundreds of images). The 5–20 % real share result is the nearest thing to a measured "ratio" for a YOLO detector.
- The 2510.12208 warning that realistic-looking synthetic data alone underperforms real on in-distribution tests, while random-appearance synthetic data buys OOD robustness at ID cost, matches the SyntheX finding that strong DR helps physics-based renders but hurts naive ones.

### Gaps
- No X-ray or medical paper found that measures real-image upweighting (loss or sampling weights), progressive synthetic→real ramps, or curriculum schedules against each other. This appears to be unmeasured in the domain.
- The BMC Med Inform Decis Mak 2026 paper "Synthetic cervical spine radiographs: expert validation and transfer learning for low-data settings" (DOI 10.1186/s12911-026-03479-0) could not be fetched (Springer cookie redirect loop); a search snippet says a synthetic-pretrained model improved landmark localisation on external data in low-data settings, but no numbers were obtained.
- The search-result summary attributed "with fine-tuning on all 6000 real images, a domain randomization-based approach achieved AP 98.5, better than real-only by 2.1 %" to the DR literature; the fetched Tremblay text gives 98.5 vs 96.5 (2.0 points), so use the fetched numbers.

## Key question 3 — Practical limits: a reported ceiling where more synthetic renders of the same CTs stopped helping (distinct patients/CTs mattering more than renders)?

### Takeaway
No paper found ablates the number of distinct CTs against the number of renders per CT in X-ray sim-to-real. The single measured render-scaling curve is SyntheX's: at a fixed 20 CTs, going from 50 to 500 renders per CT (1k → 10k images) lowered hip landmark error from 6.61 to 5.82 mm by 5k images and then gave 5.95 mm at 10k, while Dice rose 0.82 → 0.86 — i.e., landmark error flattened between 250 and 500 renders per CT, segmentation was still improving. Renders-per-CT in the published pipelines range from 10 (Toth) to ~1,850 (FluoroSAM) with no comparison across that axis.

### Cited Findings
- SyntheX scaled-up experiment: "500 synthetic X-ray images for every CT scan", datasets of 1,000 / 2,000 / 5,000 / 10,000 images from the same 20 CTs. Landmark error 6.61 ± 7.27 (1k), 6.56 ± 4.78 (2k), 5.82 ± 4.52 (5k), 5.95 ± 3.52 mm (10k); Dice 0.82 ± 0.23, 0.81 ± 0.24, 0.82 ± 0.24, 0.86 ± 0.21. Authors: "Sim2Real performances consistently improve as the number of training data increases", and the 10k model "outperform[s] real data-trained models" (Real2Real 6.46 mm / 0.79). — [Gao et al. 2023, PMC10959504](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10959504/)
- Render multiplicities actually used (no ablation across CT count in any): Toth 2019 — 1,000 CTs × 10 perturbations ([PMLR v102](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v102/toth19a.html)); Pelphix — 337 CTs → 279,709 images (≈830 per CT) ([PMC11016332](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11016332/)); FluoroSAM — 1,621 CTs → 3 M images (≈1,850 per CT) ([MICCAI 2025](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2025/0344-Paper5042.html)); agentic C-arm landmarks — 50 CTs × 1,024 projections ([arXiv 2604.18740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18740)); AnyCXR — >100,000 renders, CT count not in abstract ([arXiv 2512.17263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17263)); DRR-RATE — 25,692 CTs / 21,304 patients → 50,188 frontal DRRs (≈2 per CT) at 512×512, CheXnet AUCs cardiomegaly 0.92, effusion 0.95, atelectasis 0.72, consolidation 0.74, nodule 0.66, opacity 0.67; no training-size or mixing experiment ([PMC12478416](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12478416/)).
- Medical segmentation scaling law: "once a model captures the fundamental geometry of an organ, additional data of the same distribution provides diminishing returns" (early floor) — [arXiv 2511.13883](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13883). This is stated for same-distribution real data, not renders of the same CTs.

### Inferences
- SyntheX's curve is the only quantitative evidence on this axis, and it points the same direction as the user's hypothesis: with the patient count fixed at 20, landmark error stopped improving between 250 and 500 renders per CT, and the residual spread (SD 3.5 mm) remained. Whether that plateau is a render-diversity ceiling or a 20-patient ceiling was not tested — the experiment confounds the two because CT count was never varied.
- The 3.5× spread of scaling exponents across modalities in the 3-D MAE study (β 0.21–0.72) is a warning that an exponent measured on one anatomy/modality does not transfer; a spine-fluoroscopy exponent would need to be measured, not borrowed.

### Gaps
- No measured "distinct CTs vs renders per CT" ablation exists in the sources found; the closest, SyntheX, holds CTs fixed at 20. Anyone claiming a distinct-source ceiling for DRR training would be the first to measure it.
- No X-ray paper reports the number of distinct patients at which a synthetic-only model's real-data accuracy saturates.

## Key question 4 — Scaling-law-style results in medical imaging giving exponents or saturation points

### Takeaway
Two recent papers fit explicit power laws: medical segmentation follows E(N) = aN^-b + c with early, task-dependent saturation and a persistent floor (per-task coefficients only in a figure), and 3-D MAE pretraining loss follows L = E + C/T^β with β from 0.209 (brain T1 MRI) to 0.724 (head-and-neck PET), CT at 0.41–0.44. Older learning-curve fits on classification (Cho 2015 CT body part; the CXR curves above) give saturation points rather than exponents: ~20,000 images for CXR binary/pathology classification, ~1,000 per class predicted for 98 % on a six-class CT task.

### Cited Findings
- E(N) = aN^-b + c; a = reducible error scale, b = decay exponent, c = irreducible floor; 15 tasks, four modalities, powers-of-two training sizes, 512×512, nnUNet 1.96 M / Swin-UNet 1.59 M; "earlier and task-dependent performance saturation" and "a persistent error floor"; scaling "is not purely data-constrained but is influenced by intrinsic geometric and anatomical structure"; generative deformation augmentation "surpass[es] standard power law scaling trends without requiring additional data". — [Revisiting Data Scaling Law for Medical Segmentation, arXiv 2511.13883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13883)
- L_i(T) = E_i + C_i / T^β_i; β: BRAIN-T1 0.209, BRAIN-T2 0.243, ABD-CT 0.407, HN-CT 0.436, ABD-MRI 0.487, HN-PET 0.724; pretraining pools of 500 volumes (high-availability domains) / 150 volumes (ABD-MRI, HN-PET); budgets 10 K–200 K volume draws; ViT-B/16³ at 96³ voxels, 1.5 mm isotropic. — [Knowledge Transfer Scaling Laws for 3D Medical Imaging, arXiv 2605.06859](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06859v1)
- Learning-curve extrapolation on CT classification: measured 95.67 % at 200/class; fitted curve predicts 98 % at 1,000/class and 99.5 % at 4,092/class (GoogLeNet). — [Cho et al. 2015, arXiv 1511.06348](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06348v2)
- CXR classification saturation points: 20k images (AUC 0.95 vs 0.96 at 200k, n.s.) — [Dunnmon 2019](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/radiol.2018181422); "rapidly from 2 × 10³ to 20 × 10³ ... more gradual increase" to 291k — [J Digit Imaging 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35239091/); label requirements reduced up to 688-fold by CXR-specific pretraining, sizes 8–32,768 — [Sellergren 2022](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/radiol.212482).

### Inferences
- With b in the 0.2–0.7 range (3-D MAE) and CXR classification gaining ~0.01–0.09 AUC per decade past 20k, a ResNet-18 classifier on radiograph frames should be planned for a floor, and the marginal value of more same-distribution data past ~10⁴ should be estimated from a fitted curve on the user's own subsets rather than from these exponents, which differ 3.5× across anatomies.
- Pretraining choice moved the curve far more than N did in the Sellergren study (688-fold label reduction), which is the same lever as synthetic pretraining in the DR papers; for a fixed real-data budget the published evidence favours spending effort on the pretraining source before on the mixing schedule.

### Gaps
- No detection-specific (box mAP) scaling law with a fitted exponent exists in medical imaging in the sources found; both fitted laws are segmentation/reconstruction.
- No scaling law reported at 1024 px; the segmentation law is at 512×512 and the classification curves at unspecified or ≤600 px.
