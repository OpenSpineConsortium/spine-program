From Image to Incision — Deck Outline & Image Asset Manifest

MANS 2026 Summer Meeting · APP Session · 50 min + 10 min Q&A Slide-by-slide content with the exact imaging assets required for each

HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

Every slide below carries an ASSET block telling you precisely what to prepare. Assets are tagged by type:

Tag Meaning Prep effort
[STACK] Scrollable image series. Must be cine-scrollable live, not a static export. High — needs viewer + de-identified series
[STILL] Single static image, usually annotated Medium
[PAIR] Two images side by side for comparison Medium
[TEXT] No imaging needed None
[POLL] Audience response moment Low

Total: 7 scrollable stacks, 37 stills, 6 pairs — but far fewer than that to actually pull. One well-chosen index case yields stacks 1–4 plus several stills, and 4 of the stills are PowerPoint graphics needing no imaging at all. The consolidated checklist at the end is priority-ordered; take that to your PACS session.

Universal requirements for every image used: - Fully de-identified (name, MRN, DOB, accession, institution burned-in text) - Institutional approval for educational use per your policy - Exported at native resolution — do not let PowerPoint downsample; stenosis grading dies at low resolution - Window/level set before export; you will not have time to adjust live

SECTION A — SETUP (Slides 1–5, ~8 min)

Slide 1 — Title

“From Image to Incision: Reading the Scan Before the Read” Speaker, credentials, institution, date. Disclosures.

ASSET [TEXT] — none. (Optional: one striking sagittal T2 cord compression as background, heavily dimmed.)

Slide 2 — The Interval Problem

The images land in the chart. The patient is on the phone. The read is hours or days away. In that window you decide who is worked in today, who gets a surgeon’s number, who gets reassured.

ASSET [STILL] #1 — Screenshot of a PACS worklist or chart view showing a completed study with “Report: Pending” visible. De-identified. This single image frames the entire talk.

Slide 3 — What This Talk Is and Is Not

  • Is: a search protocol, a recognition atlas, and a decision table
  • Is not: a literature survey, advanced imaging, or surgical technique
  • You leave with something you can run tomorrow morning

ASSET [TEXT] — none.

Slide 4 — Calibrate Before You Read: Base Rates

  • 52% of asymptomatic adults have disc bulges; 27% protrusions [17]
  • Disc degeneration: 37% at age 20 → 96% at age 80 [18]
  • Degeneration on MRI is as informative as grey hair until it explains this syndrome at that level

ASSET [STILL] #2 — Simple bar or line chart: degeneration prevalence by decade. Build in PowerPoint from the Brinjikji data; no imaging needed.

ASSET [PAIR] #1 — Two lumbar axial T2 images with visually similar stenosis, captioned “asymptomatic” and “severe claudication.” Do not reveal which is which until Slide 5. This is the talk’s thesis in one image.

Slide 5 — The Scope Line + The Safety Rule

Two columns: Call it (compression, level, CSF effacement, cord signal, Schizas grade, root compromise, conus level, study adequacy) vs. Defer it, always (tumor, infection, marrow replacement, non-compressive intramedullary lesions, anything extraspinal, post-op scar vs. disc).

Your read exists to raise urgency, never to lower it. A degenerative-looking scan does not exclude ALS, MS, B12 deficiency, or cord tumor.

ASSET [TEXT] — none. Keep this slide clean; it is the medicolegal and professional anchor of the session.

SECTION B — SEQUENCE ORIENTATION (Slides 6–7, ~4 min)

Slide 6 — The One-Line Heuristic

CSF bright → T2 or STIR. Fat also bright → T2. Fat dark → STIR/fat-sat.

ASSET [STILL] #3 — Same anatomic level, same patient, four panels: T1 / T2 / STIR / GRE. Label CSF and epidural fat with arrows on each panel. This is the single highest-value teaching image in the deck. One sagittal cervical level works for all four.

Slide 7 — What Each Sequence Is For

Table: T1 (anatomy, marrow, myelomalacia) · T2 (CSF contrast, cord signal, rootlets) · STIR/FS (edema — marrow, ligament, denervated muscle) · GRE (calcification, OPLL, hemorrhage) · post-contrast (tumor, infection, scar vs. disc, acute denervation).

Study adequacy is a clinical skill — no axial T2 through the level of interest, or no fat-sat when hunting denervation, is itself a finding.

ASSET [TEXT] — table only.

SECTION C — THE CERVICAL READ (Slides 8–12, ~10 min) ⚠️ CORE

Slide 8 — The Eight Steps

Displayed as a numbered list that stays visible on-screen while you demonstrate. Consider a persistent sidebar for Slides 9–12.

  1. Sagittal T2 — where does CSF disappear?
  2. Sagittal T2 — cord signal at those levels
  3. Sagittal T1 — is the cord dark?
  4. Axial T2 at every flagged level
  5. Alignment
  6. Soft vs. hard
  7. Foramina
  8. Everything you weren’t looking for

ASSET [TEXT] — none.

Slide 9 — LIVE READ: Steps 1–3 ⚠️ SCROLLABLE

Demonstrate cine-scrolling. Talk while scrolling — the rhythm of the scroll is the lesson.

ASSET [STACK] #1 — Cervical sagittal T2, full series (~15–20 slices). Case profile: multilevel degenerative compression, CSF effaced at 2–3 levels, T2 hyperintensity present at one level. Moderate myelopathy, mJOA ~13.

ASSET [STACK] #2 — Cervical sagittal T1, SAME patient, same series geometry. Must show T1 hypointensity at the level with T2 change. Non-negotiable — Step 3 is the step everyone skips, and it only lands if you can toggle between the two stacks at the same level.

Viewer requirement: ability to display two stacks side by side, linked or independently scrollable. If your viewer cannot do this, fall back to [PAIR] #2 — matched static T2/T1 at the identical slice.

ASSET [POLL] — Room calls out the level of CSF effacement before you confirm.

Slide 10 — LIVE READ: Step 4, Axial ⚠️ SCROLLABLE

ASSET [STACK] #3 — Cervical axial T2, same patient, through the compressed levels. Must demonstrate: circumferential vs. anterior-only CSF loss, and visible cord flattening.

Teaching beat: sagittal finds it, axial grades it.

Slide 11 — LIVE READ: Steps 5–6, Alignment and Soft vs. Hard

ASSET [STILL] #4 — Sagittal T2 with cervical lordosis preserved, alignment line drawn ASSET [STILL] #5 — Sagittal T2 with kyphosis, alignment line drawn (These two can be [PAIR] #3 if shown together — recommended.)

ASSET [PAIR] #4 — Soft disc vs. OPLL, sagittal T2 + corresponding CT, annotated. Show that OPLL extends behind vertebral bodies while disc is confined to the disc level.

Slide 12 — LIVE READ: Steps 7–8

ASSET [STACK] #4 — Cervical axial T2 through foraminal levels, at least one with clear unilateral foraminal narrowing.

ASSET [STILL] #6 — A “something else” catch: an incidental non-degenerative finding on a spine MRI (thyroid nodule, lung apex lesion, vertebral hemangioma, Chiari). Reinforces Step 8 and the Slide 5 scope line.

SECTION D — RECOGNITION DRILLS (Slides 13–18, ~9 min) ⚠️ CORE

Each drill slide follows: where to look → what you see → how to grade → what fools you → why it matters.

Slide 13 — CSF Effacement + Muhle Grading

Grades 0–3. (Cord signal change is graded separately — not part of Muhle.)

ASSET [STILL] #7–10 — Four axial T2 images, one per Muhle grade (0, 1, 2, 3), arranged left to right. Ideally same patient at different levels, or four matched patients.

ASSET [STILL] #11 — CSF pulsation artifact mimicking effacement on sagittal T2, with the axial beside it showing patent CSF. The mimic slide.

Slide 14 — Cord Deformation + Compression Ratio

CR = (smallest AP ÷ largest transverse) × 100. ICC 0.80.

ASSET [STILL] #12 — Axial T2, normal rounded cord, both diameters drawn and labeled ASSET [STILL] #13 — Axial T2, flattened/crescentic cord, both diameters drawn (Best shown as [PAIR] #5.)

Mimic to mention: oblique slice angle at a kyphotic apex falsely flattens the cord. A localizer image showing slice angulation would strengthen this but is optional.

Slide 15 — Quantitative Metrics + the Two 0.8s

Table: AP canal < 7 mm · cord:canal > 0.8 · Torg–Pavlov < 0.8 · TA (ICC 0.86) · CR (ICC 0.80) · MCC/MSCC (ICC 0.75–0.88).

The two 0.8s: cord:canal > 0.8 = stenotic (cord ÷ canal). Torg–Pavlov < 0.8 = congenitally narrow (canal ÷ vertebral body). Same number, opposite direction.

MRI vs. CT myelography — Crawford 2026, 203 levels in 50 myelopathic patients [7]: - Agreement 83–95%, highest on axial (95% at the 7 mm threshold) — independent confirmation that sagittal finds it, axial grades it - MRI overestimates stenosis in ~12% of levels. One-sided: at the 0.8 threshold on axial, MRI flagged 28 levels CTM did not; CTM flagged zero that MRI missed - Thresholds were group consensus at one institution, not validated cutoffs, and the study correlated nothing against clinical status

The line to say out loud: “CTM may be useful to verify the degree of stenosis where MRI appears worse than the clinical examination would suggest.” — the paper’s own conclusion, and the imaging–clinical disconnect turned into a next step.

ASSET [STILL] #14 — One axial image with cord AP, canal AP, and transverse cord diameters all drawn and labeled, so the ratios are visually obvious.

ASSET [PAIR] #6 — ✅ ALREADY EXTRACTED: figures/PAIR6_MRI-vs-CTM_axial.png

The Crawford paper’s Figures 1 and 2 are exactly this pair, already pulled at native resolution. Same 55-year-old with congenital stenosis, imaged both ways:

MRI CTM
AP canal 6.7 mm (< 7 mm → surgical) 8.3 mm (≥ 7 mm → not)
AP cord 6.7 mm — fills the canal 5.6 mm
Cord:canal > 0.80 → surgical < 0.80 → not

Same patient, opposite conclusions on both criteria at once. Full 2×2 version also available: figures/GRID_MRI-over-CTM_same-patient.png

Slide 16 — T2 Hyperintensity + Kang Grading

Kang 0–2. Characterize focal vs. diffuse; count levels.

ASSET [STILL] #15 — Sagittal T2, focal cord signal, arrow ASSET [STILL] #16 — Sagittal T2, diffuse/multilevel cord signal, arrows

ASSET [STILL] #17 ⚠️ THE MIMIC SLIDE — do not cut this one. Truncation (Gibbs) artifact on sagittal T2 — thin bright line down the cord center — paired with the axial from the same patient showing normal cord. This is the most common false-positive cord finding and the highest-yield mimic in the entire talk.

Slide 17 — T1 Hypointensity ⚠️ THE MONEY SLIDE

Largely irreversible. Reduced recovery ratio (p = 0.03); lower likelihood of optimal outcome (p = 0.005) [8]. Post-op resolution does not improve outcomes, unlike T2 [12].

Counseling translation, delivered as a spoken sentence: > “This doesn’t mean don’t operate. It means the goal shifts from restoring what you’ve lost toward protecting what you still have.”

ASSET [PAIR] #2 — Sagittal T2 and sagittal T1, identical slice, same patient. T2 bright, T1 dark, arrows on both. (This is the fallback pair from Slide 9 — same asset, reused here at full size for emphasis. Prepare once.)

Slide 18 — Cord Atrophy

Reduced caliber, sometimes with a deceptively roomy-looking canal.

ASSET [STILL] #18 — Axial T2 showing atrophic cord with generous surrounding CSF — the “decompressed-looking canal in a myelopathic patient” trap.

Mimic: physiologic tapering below the cervical enlargement.

SECTION E — THE LUMBAR READ (Slides 19–21, ~7 min) ⚠️ CORE

Slide 19 — Seven Steps + Conus First

ASSET [STACK] #5 — Lumbar sagittal T2, full series. Normal conus at T12–L1 or L1. Demonstrate counting from the sacrum upward.

ASSET [STILL] #19 — Sagittal T2 with low conus (below mid-L2), level line drawn at mid-L2 body. Mimic to mention: transitional lumbosacral anatomy; count from the sacrum and state your convention.

Slide 20 — Schizas Grading ⚠️ HIGHEST-YIELD SKILL SLIDE

Grades A1–A4, B, C, D. The shortcut: can you still count individual rootlets? Yes → A or B. No → C or D.

  • Grades C and D more likely to fail conservative treatment; A and B less likely to warrant surgery [25]
  • Learnable by clinicians, not just radiologists [26]
  • But NORDSTEN: MRI findings had no clinically relevant association with baseline disability or pain [27] — Schizas predicts conservative treatment failure, not pain

ASSET [STILL] #20–23 — Four axial T2 lumbar images: one clear A, one B, one C, one D. Arrange as a 2×2 reference grid. Sourcing note: the original Schizas 2010 paper has a canonical figure. If you reproduce it, obtain permission; otherwise build the grid from your own PACS — straightforward to find all four grades.

Slide 21 — Schizas Drill ⚠️ AUDIENCE ACTIVITY

ASSET [STILL] #24–27 — Four unlabeled axial T2 images, one per slide-click, in mixed grade order. Reveal the answer on click.

ASSET [POLL] — Room calls the grade before each reveal. Use A/B/C/D hand signals or an ARS if MANS provides one.

This is the moment the audience discovers they can actually do this. Protect the time.

SECTION F — BEYOND THE CANAL (Slides 22–24, ~5 min)

Slide 22 — Nerve Root Compromise

No contact → contact → compression [32]. Formalized: Pfirrmann 0–3 and Lee 0–3. Key concept: perineural fat is your contrast agent. Pitfall: exiting vs. traversing root — a wrong-level error.

ASSET [STILL] #28–30 — Three parasagittal or axial images: normal fat halo, fat obliterated but root normal shape, root deformed/collapsed. Arrows on the root in each.

ASSET [STACK] #6 — Parasagittal T2 foraminal stack, scrolling laterally through the foramen. Shows why foraminal disease is missed when only mid-sagittal images are reviewed. High impact if your viewer supports it.

Slide 23 — Paraspinal Denervation Edema

Acute–subacute (~2 wk): T2 FS/STIR hyperintense, T1 iso, enhances. Chronic: fatty infiltration and volume loss. Side-to-side comparison is essential — the finding is a difference. Stated pitfall: canal-focused readers miss it; nearly invisible without fat-sat [37].

ASSET [STILL] #31 — Axial STIR or T2 FS with unilateral paraspinal muscle hyperintensity, both sides visible in frame, arrow on the abnormal side. ASSET [STILL] #32 — Chronic case: fatty infiltration and atrophy on T1, unilateral.

Sourcing note: this is the hardest asset in the deck to find in a degenerative practice, because it requires a fat-sat sequence that often isn’t ordered. Radiopaedia is a reasonable fallback. Budget extra search time.

Slide 24 — Conus Level

At or above mid-L2 is normal at any age; caudal to mid-L2 → concern for tethering [38]. A five-second check, skipped constantly in adult spine clinic.

ASSET [STILL] #19 reused — no new asset. Optionally add a simple schematic of the mid-L2 line.

SECTION G — THE CLINICAL HALF (Slide 25, ~3 min)

Slide 25 — mJOA in Two Minutes

Four domains: UE motor (0–5), LE motor (0–7), UE sensory (0–3), sphincter (0–3) = 18. Mild 15–17 · Moderate 12–14 · Severe ≤ 11 [13] Score what the patient does. Buttoning and stairs items verbatim. A 2-point drop is meaningful.

ASSET [TEXT] — full scale as a table. Also the take-home card.

SECTION H — FINDING → GUIDELINE → DECISION (Slides 26–29, ~6 min) ⚠️ CORE

Slide 26 — The Two Current Sources

2017 AO Spine CPG [36] — the four buckets. 2025 AO Spine Recommendations [33] — what changed.

ASSET [TEXT] — journal cover images optional; check permissions if used.

Slide 27 — The 2017 Buckets, Verbatim

Four rows with recommendation text, strength, and evidence quality. Emphasize the difference between “we recommend” and “we suggest” — that distinction is what you explain to patients.

ASSET [TEXT] — table only. Use quotation marks; this is verbatim guideline language.

Slide 28 — What 2025 Changes

  • Mild DCM: “Surgery should be offered as an option” — conditional, moderate quality. Mild is no longer a reason to defer the surgical conversation.
  • Approach: ventral and dorsal equivalent in equipoise — strong, high-quality (RCT). CSM-S: 163 patients, no SF-36 PCS difference at 1 yr (5.90 vs 6.22, p = 0.84); laminoplasty fewest complications in as-treated analysis [39].
  • MRI signal change endorsed for counseling on expected recovery.
  • Multidimensional: pain, frailty, HRQoL.

ASSET [STILL] #33 — Simple before/after graphic: 2017 mild-DCM box → 2025 mild-DCM box, with the language change highlighted. Build in PowerPoint.

Slide 29 — The Decision Grid ⚠️ THE TAKE-HOME SLIDE

4 × 5 grid: imaging finding (rows) × mJOA band (columns) → recommendation.

The three rules above the grid: 1. Progressive deficit → surgery, regardless of imaging severity [6] 2. Imaging severity never overrides a stable exam in mild/nonmyelopathic bands [5,36] 3. Longer symptom duration → worse outcomes. Delay is a decision [36]

ASSET [TEXT] — grid only. Design note: this slide will be photographed by the audience. Make it legible at phone-camera distance — large type, high contrast, no more than the grid and the three rules.

SECTION J — CLOSE (Slides 33–35)

Slide 33 — The Practice Loop

Read cold → commit in writing → open the report → log the delta → review monthly for patterns. Calibration expectations: studies 1–10 humbling · 10–25 automaticity · 25+ useful calibration. Track over-calls as carefully as misses.

ASSET [STILL] #36 — simple loop diagram. Build in PowerPoint.

Slide 34 — Take-Home Materials

Field Guide · structured read template · practice loop · image libraries (Radiology Education Asia, Radiopaedia, your own post-op PACS cases).

ASSET [STILL] #37 — QR code to the Field Guide PDF if you host it. Recommended — beats a handout nobody keeps.

Slide 35 — Two Sentences and Questions

Degeneration is nearly universal on imaging and only means something when it explains this patient’s syndrome at that level. A finding changes management when it changes the mJOA category, the trajectory, or the guideline bucket — otherwise it is description.

TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS FOR MANS A/V

Send this to the meeting organizers ahead of time.

Required: - Laptop input (HDMI) with your own machine — do not rely on a house PC for a PACS or DICOM viewer - Screen resolution 1920×1080 minimum; grayscale gradation matters for stenosis grading - Ability to switch between PowerPoint and a viewer application mid-talk — confirm no lockdown on the presentation machine

Bring yourself: - A local DICOM viewer with the cases pre-loaded — Horos, OsiriX Lite, RadiAnt, or Weasis. Do not depend on VPN access to your institutional PACS in a resort conference room. - Every stack also exported as a static image sequence as fallback. Assume the viewer will fail; the talk must survive it. - Your own HDMI adapters - Cases on local disk, not cloud storage

Rehearsal note: practice the scroll speed. Cine-scrolling too fast is the most common failure mode — the room cannot see what you see when you already know where the lesion is. Deliberately scroll slower than feels natural, and narrate what you are looking for as you go.

Room lighting: ask for lights down for Sections C–F. Cord signal change is genuinely invisible in a bright room, and that is the section where the audience most needs to see what you are seeing.
