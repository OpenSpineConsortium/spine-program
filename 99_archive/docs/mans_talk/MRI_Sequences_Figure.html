Reading the sequences in degenerative cervical myelopathy

Which sequence you are looking at, and which question each one answers.

Every image below is openly licensed. No single sequence answers the whole question.

1Which sequence am I looking at?

One cervical spine, one patient, one sitting. Read the CSF column first — then the fat behind the neck.

The one line to say. These are not alternatives, they are different questions asked of the same anatomy. T2 tells you the cord is compressed and whether it has been hurt. T1 tells you whether that injury is the kind that comes back. STIR tells you what is acutely angry. GRE tells you whether the thing pressing on the cord is bone or disc — which is the difference between two operations.

And the trap: STIR and T2 both show bright CSF, so the CSF column alone will not separate them. Check the fat behind the neck. Calling cord signal change on a STIR image will over-call it every time.
